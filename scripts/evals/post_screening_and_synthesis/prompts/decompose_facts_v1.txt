You are building ground truth for an evaluation of an automated evidence-synthesis
pipeline. You are given a numbered list of key points taken from a published policy
research briefing. Break each key point into the atomic facts it contains.

Each atomic fact will be graded on its own against an automatically generated report:
the grader sees one fact at a time, without the key point. So each fact must stand
alone and make exactly one claim.

A good atomic fact:
- makes one claim. A sentence that names two outcomes, two mechanisms, two populations,
  two figures or two directions is two facts: split it. Keep an "and" whole only when
  it is part of a single name, such as "Department for Health and Social Care". Split
  a point wherever its claims differ in figure, direction, population, timeframe,
  outcome or mechanism.
- is self-contained: it names its subject, population and timeframe, so a reader who
  sees the fact on its own, without the key point, understands it.
- restates part of the key point and nothing else. Do not add detail, context or
  caveats the key point does not state. Do not drop any claim the key point makes.
- is specific: a named finding, an effect size or direction, a mechanism or a named
  policy outcome, not a vague theme. Quote numbers and units exactly as the key point
  gives them.

For example, this key point:
- "Before the pandemic, remote and hybrid working had been increasing gradually. Between January and December 2019, around 1 in 10 (12%) of the UK workforce had worked at least one day from home in the previous week and around 1 in 20 (5%) reported working mainly from home."

breaks into these facts:
- "Remote working in the UK had been increasing gradually before the pandemic."
- "Hybrid working in the UK had been increasing gradually before the pandemic."
- "Around 12% of the UK workforce had worked at least one day from home in the previous week, between January and December 2019."
- "Around 5% of the UK workforce reported working mainly from home, between January and December 2019."

And this key point:
- "Circadian disruption can produce sleep and metabolic irregularities and can affect immune function."

breaks into these facts:
- "Circadian disruption can produce sleep irregularities."
- "Circadian disruption can produce metabolic irregularities."
- "Circadian disruption can affect immune function."

Choose as many atomic facts per point as the point warrants. Return one entry for
every key point you were given, no more and no fewer.

Output fields:
- points: one entry per key point, in the order given.
  - point_id: the number of the key point, as given.
  - facts: the atomic facts the point contains, each a self-contained statement.
