# Breakfast clubs and attendance in Westland primary schools (SYNTHETIC REPORT)

**Question:** What is the effect of free school breakfast clubs on pupil attendance in primary schools in Westland?

This report is an invented example used to demonstrate the evaluator. It is not a real Policy Atlas output.

## Key findings

- Attendance rises modestly: across nine trials, free breakfast clubs raised attendance by about 2 percentage points over one school year, with moderate certainty [1].
- Targeting matters: the gain was around 4 percentage points in schools with high free school meal eligibility and close to zero where eligibility was low [2].
- Delivery model may affect take-up, with school-run clubs reporting higher participation than catered clubs [5].

## What the evidence shows

Nine randomised trials in Westland state primary schools compared free universal breakfast clubs with no club. Pooled across these trials, attendance increased by 2.1 percentage points over a single school year, and the review rated this moderate certainty because four trials carried some risk of bias [1].

Four of the trials reported eligibility for free school meals. In these, the effect was about 4 percentage points in high-eligibility schools and near zero in low-eligibility schools. This subgroup result rests on four trials and is rated low certainty [2].

Three trials measured reading at 12 months and found no consistent effect on reading scores, with low certainty, while two small observational studies suggested that clubs run by external caterers saw lower pupil take-up than clubs run by school staff, a very low certainty finding [3][5].

## Limitations

All trials measured attendance over one school year. No trial followed pupils beyond 18 months, so it is unknown whether the attendance effect persists [4].
