# Mini dataset, 2026-10-05: search recall per experiment, by source and by review

**What this measures.** Each of fifteen published evidence reviews gives one search text (its *intent*) and a list of the studies it cites (its *target*). Every experiment sends the intent to one search service, keeps the top results, and we count how many of the target studies came back. **Recall** is that share: found studies divided by target studies, averaged over reviews. Only studies with a DOI (Digital Object Identifier) can be matched, so grey literature such as government reports never counts, for any arm.

**The experiments.** The *baselines* are one plain request per review to one service, no language model: `openalex-raw` (OpenAlex's own search), `semantic-scholar` (Semantic Scholar's keyword search, which needs every word to match), `semantic-scholar-snippet` (Semantic Scholar's semantic search, ranked by meaning) and `consensus` (a paid service). `cap50` to `cap1000` is how many of the service's ranked results were kept. 

The two *pipeline* rows are Policy Atlas's own search stage: `rapid` has a language model write about eighteen queries for OpenAlex and Overton and keeps 50 candidates per backend; `standard` adds abstract screening and a second search round with 100 per backend. Compare a pipeline row with a baseline row of similar size: rapid against cap 50 or 100, standard against cap 100 or 200.

**The data.** Langfuse runs labelled `mini-q-2026-10-05` on dataset `retrieval-ground-truth-mini`: the four gap-map rows (3ie, YEF) carry question-shaped intents, the other eleven their review titles. The `standard` run under that label covers only the four gap-map rows, so its figures splice those four with the eleven `standard` scores kept from the earlier label `mini-2026-10-05`; every other experiment is one run over all fifteen. 

**Differences in sources:** the Campbell and SR4ALL rows are whole reference lists that nobody has labelled, and about half of a reference list is background and methods citations no topic search should find, so their maximal recall ceiling will be lower. The 3ie and YEF rows (studies coded into a gap map by its screeners) and the four hand-made reviews are labelled, with no such ceiling. 

These tables can be re-generated by `2026-10-05-mini-question-intents.py`;

## 1. Total recall per experiment

One request to a semantic search engine currently beats pipeline. Semantic Scholar's snippet search (free) and Consensus (about 50 cents a review) tie at 11.7% when a thousand results are kept, against 4.8% for the pipeline's standard depth (about $1 a review) and 2.2% for rapid. Even at the same number of results kept the gap holds: at cap 100 the two semantic engines find 5.7% and 7.3% where rapid finds 2.2% with its 100 candidates. Semantic Scholar's keyword search barely registers, because a title-length text rarely has every word present in a paper.

### 1a. Like for like: rapid against the baselines at cap 100

Rapid keeps up to 50 candidates per backend, about 100 per review, so the fair neighbours are the cap-100 baseline rows. At the same number of candidates, one semantic request finds two and a half to three times what eighteen generated OpenAlex and Overton queries find, and one plain OpenAlex search finds about the same as rapid does.

|---|---|---|---|

### 1b. Like for like: standard against the baselines at cap 200

Standard adds a screening pass and a second search round and held about 330 candidates per review here, between the cap-200 and cap-1000 baseline rows; cap 200 is the nearer and fairer comparison. At a third fewer candidates and no language-model cost, both semantic engines beat it; its cost per review is roughly twice Consensus's and a thousand times the free arms'.

## 3. Recall per review

Review by review the two semantic engines often disagree, which suggests a pipeline that queried both would find more than either alone: Consensus finds 34 of the 84 target studies on child sleep and obesity where the snippet engine finds 18 and OpenAlex none, while the snippet engine finds 27 of 84 on loneliness where Consensus finds 16. The pipeline beats neither engine on any review and finds at most two studies at rapid depth on nine of the fifteen. Its one strong result is parental leave at standard depth (13 of 52), the review whose intent reads most like a research question.
