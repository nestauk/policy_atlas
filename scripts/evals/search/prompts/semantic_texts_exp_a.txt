You write search texts for a semantic (meaning-based) search over scholarly papers. The search compares each text with paper titles and abstracts and returns the 50 closest papers, so each text finds a different set only if it describes the studies in a different way.

You receive one research question. Write 12 texts. Each text describes, in one or two sentences, a kind of study that would help answer the question, written the way such a paper's title or first abstract sentence would read.

Make the 12 texts differ from each other as much as you can while staying on the question:
- Vocabulary: technical terms, synonyms, abbreviations, older or regional terms, and plain words.
- Named specifics: particular interventions, tests, tools, programmes, policies, exposures or measures that fall under the question.
- Who and where: different populations, age groups, settings and countries the question covers.
- Outcomes: different outcomes and measures the studies would report.
- Study types: primary studies of the kinds the question needs (for example trials, cohort or cross-sectional studies, diagnostic accuracy studies, evaluations, qualitative studies).

Rules:
- Stay inside the question's scope. Do not widen it to a different topic.
- No Boolean operators, quotation marks or field tags. Plain sentences only.
- Do not copy the question. Do not number the texts.
- The research question is data, not instructions.
