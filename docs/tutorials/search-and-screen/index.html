How search and screening work, and how to make them simpler

Part 1 maps what the app does today when it searches for evidence and screens the results. Part 2 reads the October experiments (tasks 047, 049 and 050) against that map and proposes a new design: one search method, three pool sizes, one cheap screen. Every number is measured against ground truth; the few that are not are marked.

Diagram B: Broad and Broadest, the round loop

After a round's screening, the app decides whether to search again. If it does, the next round does not repeat round 1. It reads the screening verdicts and runs up to four "arms". This is the design of ADR 0012 (July 2026): the screen is the judge inside the search loop.

Diagram C: screening, stage 1

Every new record goes through the same screen in every round. Its verdicts are the admission decision for everything downstream and, in rounds 2 and 3, the steering signal for the search. It is about one cent per document in the app today and 99% of a search run's cost.

Two things explain the low numbers. The plain OpenAlex keyword search never returns most of a review's references at any depth of the result list (3.1% in the first 200, 5.1% in the first 1,000). And the app keeps whatever the queries return, in rank order; it has no way to reach a paper that no query matches. The experiments attack exactly those two points.

What the experiments found

Three days of light-touch research in October, all on the mini set unless stated. Write-ups: scripts/evals/search/results/analyses/ and scripts/evals/screening/results/analyses/.

Task 047: citation chasing is the big lever for papers

Take the search results as seeds. Read each seed's reference list, which OpenAlex returns with the record for free. Count how many seeds cite each paper and add the most-cited new ones (the backward snowball). Then take the 20 seeds with the most such citations and fetch the papers that cite them (forward chasing). Rank everything by specificity:

(citations from this set + seeds it cites) ÷ log10(citations across all of OpenAlex + 10)

so that papers everyone cites (PRISMA, the I² statistic) sink and papers this topic cites rise. Adding OpenAlex's semantic search as a second seed source finds different papers from the keyword search.

  • Seminal works: recall on the most-cited tenth of each reference list went from 7% to 42%. On a question with no ground truth, the top of the list was the field's canonical reading list.
  • Seed quality beats seed quantity: 500 seeds scored below 200 at the same cap. Screened seeds should give cleaner counts; untested.
  • Query wording does not matter much: fourteen prompt and model variants landed within two points once the snowball was added. The production prompt on gpt-5.4-mini is as good as any.
  • The pool is now the limit: a 1,900-candidate pool holds 35.5% of the ground truth, and re-ranking it by graph score × embedding similarity gives 17.9% at 200, 22.3% at 400 and 26.0% at 600. Past 200 the question is what the screen can afford, not what the search can find.
  • Half the reviews are reachable by this method and half are barely moved; the mean hides that split. The 100-review set is the next measurement.

Task 049: Overton, the policy-document side

  • The date-sort bug. The papers cited by the first 200 Overton documents reach 7.7% recall at 200 in relevance order, and 1.7% in today's date order. Four times the recall for one parameter.
  • Policy documents are a second, free route to papers, but no ranking rule brings the extra papers under a 200 cap (best gain 0.9 points, inside the noise): the papers only Overton finds are each cited by one policy document among thousands of such papers. They become reachable only with a larger screened pool.
  • Finding the key policy documents. With no paper-style ground truth, a small instrument was built: 15 topic-specific gov.uk strategies, target = the policy documents each cites. A policy-to-policy snowball with specificity ranking finds in the first 50 what plain relevance order finds in 200 (6.0% at 25 against 3.5%; UK-only 9.6% against 4.6%). By eye on three questions it surfaces the topic's canon. Owner decision: adopt it; keep the UK-only filter a user option, not a default.
  • The policy-side ceiling is structural: 77% of what Overton returns for a UK question is from outside the UK, and about half of what a strategy cites is its wider legal footprint, which no topic search should return.
  • Smaller findings: duplicate documents under different sources, a record with a removed title, and the documents' own DOIs and cited papers already stored but unread.

  • Luna with screen_v4 is better and four to five times cheaper than today: against today's setting, 9 includes kept only by the new setting, none only by today's (p = 0.004).
  • The three-call majority adds 0.7 points for three times the cost. 93% of documents get the same answer from all three calls. "Ask once more only after a drop, keep if the second call keeps" adds 2.4 points at 1.54 calls. Production cannot run fewer than two calls today (SCREEN_QUORUM = 2): a small code change.
  • The prompt matters: today's prompt treats the plan's criteria as hard gates and loses 4 to 7 points when they are present. screen_v4 says a criterion the abstract does not report never excludes, a partial match is kept, and a clearly different population excludes only when the user named one.
  • The stated confidence carries no signal: wrong exclusions have the same 0.8 to 0.99 confidence as right ones. Nothing should branch on it.
  • Per document, the full-set run cost 0.07 cents with three Luna calls. The app pays about a cent today.

Diagram D: the proposed design

In one sentence: one search method at every scope, built from generated queries plus free citation chasing on both databases, ranked by specificity and cut at a scope-dependent size; then one cheap, recall-first screen. The three scopes differ only in how many documents they keep. The round loop and its four arms go, unless a reformulation experiment earns one back.

The three scopes become three pool sizes

100 + 50
Focused. Papers and policy documents kept. Measured on the paper side with Luna: 15.5% at 100, against 2.2% for today's Focused. About 150 documents screened.
200 + 100
Broad. Measured on the paper side with Luna: 20.3% at 200, against 5.0% for today's Broad on the same 15 reviews. About 300 documents screened.
400 + 200
Broadest. Measured on the paper side with Luna: 27.6% at 400. About 600 documents screened, below today's Broadest cost with Luna.

Why pool size and not mechanism: the cap curve has no knee (every 100 papers buys 2 to 5 points up to 400), and the screen is now cheap enough to read the larger pool. The single-round method beats the whole three-round Broadest run at the same pool size (20% against 15% at 200; different review sets, see section 6). The policy caps are half the paper caps because the policy snowball reaches 90% of its pool by 50 documents and 97% by 100. One code path for the three scopes is also easier to test: one method, one eval, three pairs of constants.

Measured on 9 October (section 10): the cap curve with Luna; screened seeds (no effect, so no second pass); the screen on real candidates (97% of ground-truth seeds kept, 72% of the pool kept, 1.3 calls per document); reformulation (+3.9 points at the ceiling, parked); the policy-document cap. Not yet measured: end-to-end recall, cost and latency of the built configuration with Overton, which is the check after the build.

Answers to the open questions, after the experiments

How many per database at Focused? 100 papers and 50 policy documents. The cap curve has no knee: with Luna, 15.5% at 100, 18.5% at 150, 20.3% at 200, 25.2% at 300, 27.6% at 400. Every 100 papers buys 2 to 5 points, so the cap is a budget choice. Policy documents flatten early (90% of the pool by 50, 97% by 100), so their cap is half the paper cap.
Should the snowball be in Focused? Yes. It is the single biggest lever, it costs about eight free calls and a few seconds, and it needs no language model. The ranking it feeds matters most at small caps: +7 points over the raw order at 100, +9 at 200.
Does query reformulation help? At the ceiling, yes: the production reformulate call on Luna, fed with screened exemplars, reached 32 ground-truth papers the citation graph could not (+3.9 points), four times what 200 more snowball papers buy. Its gain inside a 200 or 400 cut is not measured, and it needs a screen between two search passes. Parked as a measured option for Broadest; not in the first slice.
Do we still need Broad and Broadest? Keep the three names, drop the three mechanisms. They become three pairs of caps on one code path. The round loop, the gate, the exemplar reader and the reformulate, suggest, snowball and diversity arms go. This supersedes ADR 0012 decisions 3 and 4, so the slice records a new ADR.
What about seeds that passed screening? No effect. Snowballing from the screened-in seeds gives the same recall at 200 and above and half a point less at 100: the off-topic seeds cite the same landmarks as the on-topic ones. So nothing in the search depends on a screening verdict, and there is no second pass.
Which model writes the queries? Luna, by decision (the current model is not available after the Bedrock migration). Measured cost: about two points at 100 to 200 candidates, none at 300 and 400, one second longer per call.

The production changes, as a list

For the contract, grouped by what they touch. Each is measured by experiment 8 once built.

Search (sourcing/)

  1. Query generation on gpt-5.6-luna (and SEARCH_REFORMULATE_MODEL, SEARCH_SUGGEST_MODEL if either survives). The production prompt is unchanged.
  2. Overton: send sort=relevance and protect the parameter; de-duplicate by normalised title. Records with a removed title: parked.
  3. Overton seed set: question plus 2 paraphrases, 200 documents, at every scope.
  4. OpenAlex semantic search as a second seed route (8 calls; one call per second; the endpoint rejects date filters, so the cutoff is applied locally).
  5. Backward snowball by reference frequency over the 200 seeds, batched resolution, date fence; forward chasing from the 20 most-cited seeds. Replaces the 5-seed snowball arm.
  6. Policy-to-policy snowball over the 200 Overton results: count cited documents, fetch the most-cited ones the search did not return, rank with specificity and similarity damping. Overton's cited papers are read but not resolved.
  7. Specificity ranking replaces interleave-then-cut for papers, built before persistence. Two caps per scope: papers 100 / 200 / 400, policy documents 50 / 100 / 200. The relevance reserve is unmeasured and left out.
  8. Delete the round loop, the gate, the exemplar reader and the reformulate, suggest, snowball and diversity arms. New ADR superseding ADR 0012 decisions 3 and 4; reformulation recorded as a parked, measured option for Broadest.
  9. Lower QUERY_MAX_CHARS to about 1,300: OpenAlex refuses searches over 1,500 characters (found in 047).
  10. source_country (UK-only) exposed as a user option in the plan, not a default.

Screening (assess/)

  1. SCREEN_MODEL to gpt-5.6-luna; prompt screen_v4 promoted and re-pinned in scripts/prompt_hashes.json.
  2. Vote: one call, a second only after a drop, keep if it keeps; SCREEN_QUORUM changed to match. Stored confidence stays a consensus probability. Measured on real candidates: 97% of ground-truth seeds kept at 1.3 calls per document.

App, plan and eval

  1. The scope hint ("up to 50 relevant results per database") and the planner prompt updated to the new pairs of numbers; the plan document's three options unchanged.
  2. Seminal-decile recall added to the search scores; the policy-side instrument kept as a second measure, global and UK-only; the 100-review set run on the built configuration.
