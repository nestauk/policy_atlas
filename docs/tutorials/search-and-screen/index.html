How search and screening work

What the app does when it searches for evidence and screens the results, as built in task 051: one search method at every scope, built from generated queries plus free citation chasing on both databases, ranked so the cut holds the most relevant, the seminal and the newest papers (task 052; specificity alone before that) and cut at a scope-dependent size; then one cheap, recall-first screen. Every number on this page is either read from the code or measured against ground truth; the measured ones say when.

Diagram A: the search pass

The two databases are searched at the same time, on two threads, and within the OpenAlex leg the seed, resolve and forward calls go out in batches of up to 8 at once. The transport admits one request per interval per database (a lock), and a rate-limit reply pauses every later request to that database for one second. This is why a search takes about 45 seconds at every scope instead of about 130.

Every search runs this once, at every scope. Two databases: OpenAlex, a free index of about 250 million scholarly works with keyword search, an embedding search, and on every record the reference list and citation count; and Overton, a subscription index of policy documents with a semantic search that takes a sentence, and on every record the policy documents it cites. The call counts are the plan for one filter variant; a plan naming more than 100 author countries doubles the keyword calls.

What acquire does with the two pools

The search hands acquire the two ranked lists and the two caps. Acquire maps each record, drops duplicates of a work already in the task (by record id, DOI, or identical text; the best-ranked copy claims the identity), cuts at the cap, and persists and embeds only what it keeps. Every record a call returned is counted exactly once: acquired, already acquired, unusable (no title), or dropped over the cap, and these sum to the raw number returned. The coverage record carries the counts per database; every kept record's source.acquired event carries the origin of the call that found it (generated, variant_sr, variant_rct, semantic, snowball_backward, snowball_forward, verbatim, paraphrase, landmark).

The three scopes are three pairs of caps

100 + 50
Focused. Papers and policy documents kept; about 150 documents screened.
200 + 100
Broad. About 300 documents screened.
400 + 200
Broadest. About 600 documents screened.

Why pool size and not mechanism: the design experiments found no knee in the cap curve (every 100 papers bought 2 to 5 points up to 400), and the screen is now cheap enough to read the larger pool. The policy caps are half the paper caps because the policy snowball reached 90% of its pool by 50 documents and 97% by 100. One code path for the three scopes is also easier to test: one method, one eval, three pairs of constants. The scope hint in the app says exactly this: "Focused: up to 100 papers and 50 policy documents".

Where the plan's filters go

A plan can carry filters (dates, SDGs, document types, languages, open-access status, author countries; for Overton publisher type, country, region, source and language). They are never weakened. Where each one is applied:

  • Keyword searches: every OpenAlex filter on the wire, as before. Overton searches carry the Overton filters.
  • Semantic searches: OpenAlex's embedding endpoint accepts only the type, language and retraction filters and a publication-year fence (probe of 9 October 2026). A date range goes on the wire as a year fence and the exact dates are checked locally on the returned records; SDGs, paratext, open-access status and author countries are checked locally too. The coverage record lists the locally applied keys as semantic_local_filters.
  • Backward and forward calls: every OpenAlex filter except the author-country clause, which is checked locally on the citation-found papers (a paper with no affiliation data is dropped under a country filter: fail closed), so a plan with more than 100 countries does not double those calls.
  • Landmarks: the plan's source_country post-filter applies to the fetched documents as it does to the search results.

What a local filter dropped is counted on the call's event as post_filter_excluded, zero included.

Diagram B: screening, stage 1

Every kept document goes to the stage-1 screen: its title and abstract (or the title alone, when there is no abstract) with the research question and the plan's screening criteria, to gpt-5.6-luna with the prompt screen_v4. The model answers relevant, not relevant, or unsure with a confidence. The vote is adaptive: one call; a second call only when the first says not relevant.

Thresholds (contract § Acceptance checks): mini-set search recall at least 12.5% / 17.3% / 24.6% at Focused / Broad / Broadest (the experiment's numbers minus 3 points of noise and production differences); screen recall at least 90% of search recall at each scope; 0 failed provider calls or the undercount named. The documents, cost and wall-clock columns are from the task 051 runs of 2026-10-09, which the paper order does not change. For comparison, the search before task 051 found 5.0% at Broad on the same reviews (scripts/evals/search/results/history.md, 2026-10-05). Cost note: the dollar figures are at the Luna list price of 2026-10-08. Langfuse reported five times these ($0.17, $0.33 and $0.71) because its price row for Luna is five times list; the verification record carries both. Result: Focused and Broad meet their thresholds by 0.4 and 0.5 points; Broadest does not (22.3% against 24.6%, 2.3 points short); the owner accepted the measured figure on 2026-10-12, since Broadest beats Broad on 11 of the 15 reviews and ties on 3. Screening keeps 96% to 97% of what the search found at every scope. Two calls failed, one Overton call at Focused and one at Broadest, both on the whole-school review: its policy side is an undercount, its paper side complete.

The policy side has no DOI ground truth. It is pinned by replay tests built from recorded Overton responses of three task 049 questions (backend/tests/data/policy_replay/): relevance order, title dedup, the 15 landmarks, the specificity order, the UK-only post-filter and the policy cap.

What it replaced

Before task 051 (task 015, ADR 0012), a Focused search was one round of the 15 keyword calls and 3 Overton calls, interleaved by rank and cut at 50 documents per database; Broad and Broadest repeated acquire and screen up to three times, steered by the screening verdicts through four arms (reformulate from screened exemplars, a 5-seed snowball, model-suggested papers, a diversity reserve) and a stop rule with its own vocabulary. Stage-1 screening made three calls per document on gpt-5.4-mini and took a majority with a quorum of two. Overton results were sorted by date, not relevance, because no sort was sent.

None of the four arms was measured on its own. The whole three-round Broadest measured 15.3% paper recall on four reviews; one pass of the method above measured 20.3% on fifteen at the same pool size, with no screening inside the search (screened seeds changed nothing). The round loop's orchestration is deleted; its last working commit is tagged search-round-loop-last, with the restore recipe in ADR 0038. The reformulate and suggest prompt builders stay in the tree, tested and unreachable. The old stop values stay valid on historical coverage rows.
