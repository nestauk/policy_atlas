Theory of change wireframes

One user, from an option to a checked and exported theory of change, in the existing Policy Atlas app. Version 3 is quieter still: one font, three sizes, and plain paragraphs in place of labels and counts.

What changed

  • Rounded boxes and smooth curved arrows, as in the prototype.
  • Colour shows evidence strength and nothing else. Only connections are graded, each on two questions: did it work elsewhere, and will it hold here. Boxes are neutral and show how they are measured and where they have been seen. Blue is for actions and for things you need to review.
  • No separate assumptions. Each connection names the conditions it needs, and its "holds here" evidence speaks to them. The navy dot marks the critical connection.
  • One font, the app's own, in three sizes: 24 for the page title, 16 for reading, 14 for controls and lists.
  • No small captions, counts or metadata lines. Context, the evidence summary and how to read the canvas are proper paragraphs.
  • "Detailed information" under the canvas uses the report's expand and collapse sections. It labels each step as input, output, outcome or impact, and shows each connection as three short paragraphs: what happens, whether it worked elsewhere, and whether it will hold here.
  • The plan uses the navy plan panel of the other task types, with your original file in its flow.

Four levels of detail

  1. Look: boxes, arrows and their colours. Enough to see where the theory is weak.
  2. Hover: both grades for a connection, or how a box is measured.
  3. Click: the panel on the right, with the evidence.
  4. Read: scroll below the canvas for every profile in order, ready to print.

Example content. The option is real: "Restrictions on unhealthy food advertising to children", from the obesity Options scoping task. The theory, sources and quotes are illustrative and have not been checked. "Less healthy food" means food high in fat, salt or sugar.

Frame 1

Two ways in

The "Theory of change" row on the New task page becomes active. On an option in Options scoping, a short block at the end offers "Design a theory of change" and lists the theories already made from it.

Reuses
NewTaskView and the expanded OptionCard.
Frame 2

The plan conversation

The Task Agent fills the plan from the option, suggests a measurable change, and takes the team's own logic model as a PDF. The plan is the same navy panel as in the other task types. Your original sits inside it, under "Starting point".

Reuses
TaskAgentPane, the plan document and PlanCard.
Frame 3 · core

The result page

Two short paragraphs say what the theory is and what the evidence says. Then come the canvas and the detailed information. "How to read this" in the toolbar opens a short guide. Try it: hover anything on the canvas, change the pathway, click a box, and open a step's evidence below.

Frame 4 · core

The detail panel

A click opens the item's panel. For a connection: what happens, how the step works and what it needs, then Worked elsewhere and Holds here, each with its grade, a cited sentence and its documents. A document that serves both is listed in full once. Here it is the critical connection: it worked where it was tried (medium), but it needs brands not to move their spend, and the evidence that this holds in England is weak.

Frame 5 · core

Editing while the check runs

The draft appears at once. Connections are grey until checked, and take their colour as each check finishes. The user reworded an outcome that was already checked. Nothing reruns: the box and the connection into it get a blue dot, and the connection keeps its evidence, marked Edited · check pending. Clicking the connection opens its panel with the pending block on top (shown open here). Detailed information shows the same step with the same buttons (below), and after the check the Task Agent lists pending steps with Check all and Keep all (frame 6).

Reuses
RunningCard and run events in the rail.
Decide
Is the blue dot enough for "edited, not yet checked", or should the box also stay grey?

The same step in Detailed information carries the label and both buttons, so readers who skip the canvas see it too. In the panel above, the block sits on top. Check again changes as little as possible. It first asks whether the new wording changes the claim. A rewording, like this one, keeps the evidence and reads "Checked: no change". If the claim changed, only the documents already found are judged again, with no new search, and the Task Agent says what changed. If fewer than two documents still apply, it offers Search for more.

Frame 6 · core

Changes to review

When the check ends, you get the usual run notification. Policy Atlas has added pathways found in the evidence and consolidated. It may change your work, and says so: blue dots mark each change on the canvas, and the Task Agent lists every change with its own Undo, plus Undo all. A changed item also shows your version beside the new one. The Task Agent then suggests a stress test.

Rule
T1: Policy Atlas may merge, reword or set aside your items, and flags each change.
Decide
A blue dot clears when you accept the change, keep yours, or undo it.

A plan change after the draft. If you change the plan, here the population, the Task Agent asks whether to redraft or keep the canvas and its evidence as they are, with an optional "Check all again".

Frame 7

Stress test

A separate chat asks four questions, one at a time. The canvas fades everything except the item each question is about. The chat does not change the canvas.

Reuses
ChatSidePanel, with a new chat kind.
Frame 8

Sources

The existing Sources tab: every document read, what it was used for, and the split by source type and country, so any skew is visible.

Reuses
SourcesView.
Frame 9

Export

The Download menu, grouped by what people do next. The new PDF report is the result page on paper: the canvas, then the detailed information.
