How the theory of change capability works

A user describes an intervention and the change they want from it, or brings a theory they already have. Policy Atlas drafts the causal chain on a canvas in the Magenta Book's layout, then checks every step against research and policy documents while the user edits. Each connection shows how strong the evidence is that this step works, for this population and place, and the report opens with a verdict on the theory as a whole.

Part 1 · What the user sees

What a theory of change is

A theory of change is an explicit account of how an intervention is expected to bring about a change: the chain of steps, the assumptions each step depends on, and how strong the evidence is behind them. HM Treasury's Magenta Book treats it as the first step of any evaluation. The diagram shows the theory; the theory itself is the reasoning and the evidence.

Users told us that building one by hand is valuable but slow, and that they want two things from a tool: evidence they can audit, and a clear difference between a step with too little evidence and a step with evidence against it.

The screens are drawn in the wireframes (wireframes.html and wireframes-editing.html in the task folder). This page explains what happens behind them.

A session, start to finish

  1. Start. From New task, in one of three ways: describe the intervention or the change you want; upload a picture or PDF of a theory you already have; or choose "Start drafting yourself" and draw on an empty canvas.
  2. Plan. The Task Agent asks at most two questions: the change you want, in words you could measure, and whether you already have a theory. Everything else is suggested in the plan, where you can edit it, including which years of evidence count (from 2000 by default).
  3. Sense check. When the plan is ready, two quick searches look for evaluations of the whole intervention, and one call asks whether the route from intervention to goal is plausible. If it is not, or if evaluations found it did not work, the Task Agent says so in the chat, with its sources. It never blocks: you decide.
  4. Draft, in about a minute. The canvas appears at once, and the Task Agent says that this is a first draft from reasoning and that it is now checking it.
  5. Evidence check, about 5 minutes. Connections show a still grey line until checked. You can edit, ask questions, or leave; a check you start meanwhile waits in a queue, and Stop ends the current one after its step. You get the usual notification when it ends.
  6. Read. The report opens with the theory's verdict. The canvas suits people who think in diagrams; Detailed information under it suits people who read. Both show the same theory and evidence, and codes such as #4 in the report and the chat link to the canvas.
  7. Refine. Edit the theory, challenge a document's scores, find more evidence, or ask for a review of the theory's shape. Proposed changes wait as suggestions beside the canvas. Every change is in History, and your own changes can be undone.
  8. Share and download. A PDF report, PNG and SVG pictures, a draw.io file, CSV and JSON, all made in the browser. A public link shows the theory read-only, without downloads.

Diagram 1: the canvas

The canvas follows the Magenta Book's layers from left to right. Boxes are the steps and stay neutral; each carries a code such as #4. The connections between them carry the evidence: the line style shows the verdict. A dashed vertical line, the accountability ceiling, separates what the programme directly influences from what it contributes to. A warning triangle marks the critical connection, to test first. Routes that could go wrong sit below a line. The example is simplified from a real run on minimum unit pricing for alcohol in Scotland.

One verdict on every connection

Theory-based evaluation tests the links in a causal chain and the assumptions under them (White 2009; Mayne 2015, 2017). A box is a result: it needs a clear definition and a way to measure it, not a grade of its own. So Policy Atlas grades only connections, and the assumptions are not separate objects: each connection names the conditions it needs. "It worked there" is not "it will work here" (Cartwright and Hardie 2012), so every document is also scored on how well its finding carries over.

What the judge saysFor each document and connection: its support for the step (strong, weak, mixed, refutes, or not about it), with one quote that code checks against the source text, and its relevance, 0 to 3: how many extra assumptions it takes to carry the finding to this population, place and time.
How code weighs itQuality, 1 to 3, comes from the study's appraisal (a systematic review or trial scores high, an opinion piece low). A document weighs quality × relevance ÷ 9, so one strong, fully relevant study weighs 1. Support counts +2, +1, 0 or −2. This is a simplified BEAM, the Balance Evidence Assessment Method (Christie et al. 2023).
verdictrule, from the total weight W and the weighted average support S
SupportedW of 1 or more, S of +1 or more, and under a quarter of the weight refutes.
Leaning supportsS of +0.5 or more, with W of 1 or more but not enough for Supported, or with W between 4/9 and 1.
MixedW of 1 or more and S between −0.5 and +0.5.
Leaning againstS of −0.5 or less, short of Evidence against.
Evidence againstW of 1 or more and S of −1 or less.
Not enough evidenceW under 4/9, about half of one strong, relevant study, or thin evidence that leans neither way.

The verdict is worked out from the stored judgements each time the page asks and is never saved, so it cannot drift from its evidence. A connection within reach of another verdict is marked close to the threshold: one more document could change it. The critical connection is the weakest step that matters: the lowest verdict on a kept success pathway, and of those, the one on most pathways.

Robust models count as fair studiesA modelling study normally scores low on quality. If the judge finds it built on real outcome data, tested for uncertainty or validated, it counts at quality 2: enough for a fair weight, never the top.
Web pages count by their publisherA page from a trusted publisher, such as gov.uk, the Evaluation Task Force, a regulator or a What Works Centre, counts by its appraisal like any document. Any other page counts at quality 1.

The theory's verdict, on two questions

The report opens with one verdict on the whole theory. Mechanism: can this intervention cause this change at all (plausible or implausible)? Evidence: what do the graded connections and the evaluations of the whole programme say (supports, mixed, too little or against)? A headline sentence and the step that carries the theory come with it. A full check adds one search for evaluations of the whole programme, judged in the same pool as the connections, because they can be missed step by step. In red-team runs, nonsense theories came out implausible, not merely "too little evidence".

No overall score, and no counts on the canvas. The verdict is two words and a sentence; the evidence behind every connection is one click away.
Part 2 · How it is built

Diagram 2: mostly existing machinery

The capability sits on what Policy Atlas already has. The Task Agent, the search loop, classification and appraisal of study quality all come from the Evidence search, unchanged. The per-connection searches reuse the small "option search" walks that Options scoping already runs in parallel. New code covers only what nothing in the repo did yet: drafting a causal graph, reading a diagram, picking and judging documents against one causal step, weighing the verdicts, choosing between pathways, and writing short cited sentences and the report.

The evidence check, step by step

This runs in the background after the draft. An item is one thing the check tests: a connection. Boxes are not items.

  1. Turn each connection into a fixed question.new Code writes the text, so the same theory always gives the same search: "If cheap alcohol becomes less affordable, for heavier drinkers, then they buy fewer alcohol units, because keeping previous purchases would take more of their budget…". Each connection also stores a fingerprint of its wording.
  2. Search once per connection.reused Five calls: OpenAlex by meaning on the sentence (20 results), the same with the plan's place (10), the step's keywords with "systematic review" and with "randomised controlled trial", and Overton by meaning (10). Up to 60 records per connection, within the plan's years, at most 80 connections per check. Eight connections search at once, in their own pool, using the walks Options scoping already runs. A full check adds one search for evaluations of the whole programme.
  3. Add the field's most-cited works.reused The Evidence search's citation chase counts which works the pool's papers cite most, and looks up to 25 of them up in one OpenAlex call, so an older study that shaped the field is not missed.
  4. Pick up to ten documents per connection.new The candidates are the connection's own results, the nearest documents the other connections found, and the most-cited works. Missing abstracts are fetched from Crossref. A fast model picks the ten that best test the step.
  5. Discover what the draft missed.new A pass over the screened documents, in batches, looks for mechanisms and failure modes the draft does not have. Each becomes a candidate pathway, labelled "found in the evidence". On a theory the user uploaded or drew, the routes found wait as one suggestion instead of changing the canvas.
  6. Merge.new Boxes and pathways that say the same thing are folded together; boxes the user wrote are never folded automatically. Pathways that do not reach the intended change for this population and place are set aside, each with a reason. This needs no evidence, so it runs before judging and nothing is judged twice.
  7. Search the new links.reused Connections that discovery added get their own search and pick, like the drafted ones.
  8. Judge every pair.new One call per connection and document gives its support, one quote and its relevance. The same call notes any condition the support holds under, any case study and indicator for the boxes at either end, and for a model whether it is robust. The existing quote checker confirms each quote; a judgement without a verified quote is dropped.
  9. Read the size of change.new For each document judged on topic, a fast model lifts the effect it reports: the measure, value, confidence interval, unit, population, place and year, each with its quote.
  10. Classify and appraise.reused The Evidence search steps, unchanged, on the documents the judge found on topic only: what kind of study it is, and how good (a score from 1 to 5).
  11. Weigh the verdicts.new Code, not a model: the rule above, from the stored judgements, on every read. Only judgements on the connection's current wording count.
  12. Choose.new If more than six success or three failure pathways remain, the most promising and best evidenced stay; the rest are set aside with a reason. Conditions recorded on supports that a connection does not yet name are added to it. Every change to something the user made is flagged and keeps the user's version.
  13. Write, then check the citations.new One call writes each connection's title and evidence sentence and each box's description; another writes the report; a third gives the theory's verdict. Code checks every citation: a sentence may cite only its own connection's judged documents, or for a box its case studies. A sentence that fails is replaced by a fixed one.
  14. Tell the user.reused The usual run notification, and a short message from the Task Agent with the verdict.

A check on named connections only, such as Find evidence for a new connection, runs the search, pick, judge and write steps, and leaves out discovery, merging, choosing and the report.

Editing, step by step

Users asked to start editing at once rather than wait for the check. Three things make that safe: every judgement remembers the exact wording it judged, every row carries a revision number, and every change is an event in History.

  1. Every edit is a checked write. Adding, changing, moving or deleting a box or connection sends the row's revision number with the change. If someone or something changed the row meanwhile, the edit is refused with "this changed since you opened it", and the canvas reloads. Each accepted edit writes one History event, with who made it.
  2. An edit flags the step; nothing reruns on its own. The connection keeps its evidence and verdict, labelled "for the previous wording", and reads "Edited · check pending". Changing a box marks every connection that touches it. The user decides: Keep the evidence, recorded as their choice, or Check again.
  3. Check again changes as little as possible. Only the documents already judged for that step are judged again, ranked for the new wording, with no new search. Find more evidence runs a new search; documents it finds wait for the user's Accept before they count.
  4. New connections are checked on request. A connection the user draws gets Find evidence. Connections that arrive in an accepted suggestion, after the first check, are checked at once.
  5. The user can challenge the evidence. Change a document's support or relevance (the model's scores stay visible), remove a document, or add a study by its DOI.
  6. Review the theory. A frontier model reads the theory's shape: steps on no pathway, gaps, missing failure routes. The button offers it after five or more edits since the last review. What it proposes becomes a suggestion; "Update the report" rewrites the report on request.
  7. Suggestions wait beside the canvas. From a review, from the chat, from a later upload, and from the evidence on a theory the user drew. Hovering over one shows it faded on the canvas; each route is accepted or rejected on its own, and an accepted route is one step in History.
  8. The user's changes can be undone. From History: an edit or an accepted suggestion comes back as it was. Undo skips any row changed since, and says which.
  9. Policy Atlas's own changes are flagged. Discover, merge and choose run on the theory as it is when each starts. They may merge, reword or set aside things the user made, but each change stores the reason and the user's version.
  10. One check at a time. A check started while another runs waits in a queue and starts when it ends. Stop ends the running check before its next step; what it finished stays on the canvas.

Your own theory

Upload a diagramA PNG, JPEG or PDF of an existing theory, from the new-task page or the chat. A vision call reads it, and the canvas shows it as drawn. What would complete it, such as a failure route or a missing outcome, waits as suggestions, route by route. A later upload never changes the canvas: what it adds becomes one suggestion.
Draw it yourself"Start drafting yourself" opens an empty canvas. Boxes and connections drawn before any draft are your sketch; the draft keeps a sketch that has connections and suggests the rest.

Files are checked by type and by their first bytes, at most 10 MB and 10 PDF pages, and kept in a private, encrypted S3 bucket, retained. The original appears in the plan under "Starting point", for the owner and colleagues; it is never on the public view. History records each file's name, size and fingerprint.

What is stored

Nine tables, following the Options scoping pattern. Everything else is reused: tasks, plans, the Task Agent's conversation, stored documents and their classification and appraisal, chats and the event log.

tableholds
toc_boxEach box: layer, code, text, result criteria, indicator, its description, who made and last edited it, and its position on the canvas if moved.
toc_linkEach connection: its two ends; if, for, then and because; the conditions it needs; its title and evidence sentence; whether it is the critical one; the wording the user chose to keep evidence for.
toc_band_itemContext and supporting activities.
toc_pathway, toc_pathway_linkSuccess and failure pathways, kept or set aside, with reasons, and their connections in order.
toc_item_evidenceEach judgement: the connection, the document, its support, verified quote and relevance, any condition, case study or indicator, the wording it judged, and any score the user changed, with who changed it.
toc_figureThe size of change each judged document reports, with its quote.
toc_proposalSuggestions from a review, the chat, the evidence or an upload, and what the user decided.
task_uploadUploaded files: where they are in S3, name, type, size and fingerprint. Shared by every capability.

Every graph row also carries a revision number and, where Policy Atlas changed the user's work, the reason and the user's version. The verdicts are never stored.

How we know it works

No public dataset of real theories of change exists; they live as figures inside PDFs. So we built our own reference sets from published reports, and test each part where a labelled set exists.

evalquestion it answersdata
Structure checksIs every draft well formed: layers in order, no loose boxes, at most two conditions per connection and none repeated, at least one failure pathway? A draft that fails is redrafted once.Runs on every draft, in code.

A published evaluationDoes a run find what an evaluation of the real policy found?Minimum unit pricing in Scotland, against Public Health Scotland's final evaluation (2023).

Next: the judge on public labelled data (SciFact, Check-COVID, PubMedQA, Evidence Inference), with the rate at which a challenge is mistaken for support; more published theories; and a blind expert panel.

Built, and next

A simple core first, tried on real topics and tuned over repeated live runs, then the increments, each used in the app before the next started. The capability sits behind a server setting, off in both environments until it is switched on.

  1. Core. The new task kind, the Task Agent and the sense check, the draft, the evidence check on OpenAlex and Overton, the verdicts and the cited sentences, the canvas, the panel, Detailed information and the theory's verdict.
  2. Editing. Checked writes, "check pending" with Keep the evidence and Check again, challenging the evidence, reviews, suggestions, History with Undo.
  3. Your own theory. Uploaded pictures and PDFs, drawing your own, and the uploads bucket.
  4. Downloads. The PDF report, PNG, SVG, draw.io, CSV and JSON.

Next: web search as a third source (pages counted by their publisher); a stress-test chat on the critical connection; "Design a theory of change" from an option in Options scoping, sharing its documents; full text for the connections that need it; PowerPoint export.
