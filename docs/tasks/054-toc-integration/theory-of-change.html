A user describes a change they want, an intervention, or a theory they already have. Policy Atlas drafts the causal chain on a canvas in the Magenta Book's layout, then checks every step against research, policy documents and the web while the user edits. Each connection shows two things: whether that step worked where it was tried, and whether it is likely to work here.

Part 1 · What the user sees

What a theory of change is

A theory of change is an explicit account of how an intervention is expected to bring about a change: the chain of steps, the assumptions each step depends on, and how strong the evidence is behind them. HM Treasury's Magenta Book treats it as the first step of any evaluation. The diagram shows the theory; the theory itself is the reasoning and the evidence.

Users told us that building one by hand is valuable but slow, and that they want two things from a tool: evidence they can audit, and a clear difference between a step with no evidence and a step with evidence against it.

The screens are drawn in the wireframes (wireframes.html in the task folder). This page explains what happens behind them.

A session, start to finish

  1. Start. From an option in Options scoping ("Design a theory of change"), or from New task. From an option, everything the option already answers is filled in: intervention, population, place, outcomes, and its documents.
  2. Plan. The Task Agent asks at most two questions: the change you want, in words you could measure, and whether you already have a theory (paste it, or upload a picture or PDF). Everything else is suggested in the plan, where you can edit it. If the agent answers fast enough, it may ask one or two more.
  3. Draft, in seconds. The canvas appears at once, and the Task Agent says in the sidebar that this is a first draft from reasoning and that it is now checking it.
  4. Evidence check, about 5 to 10 minutes. Connections turn from grey to their colour as each is checked. You can edit, ask questions, or leave. You get the usual notification when it ends.
  5. Changes, listed. Policy Atlas adds pathways it found in the evidence and tidies the rest. The Task Agent lists every change, each with its own Undo, plus Undo all. Blue dots mark the same changes on the canvas.
  6. Read and refine. The canvas suits people who think in diagrams; Detailed information under it suits people who read. Both show the same theory and evidence.
  7. Stress test. The Task Agent always suggests it: four short questions, starting with the critical connection.
  8. Share and export. A PDF report (the canvas, then Detailed information) and a PowerPoint with real boxes and arrows both matter, as do the picture, CSV, JSON and draw.io files.

Diagram 1: the canvas

The canvas follows the Magenta Book's layers from left to right. Boxes are the steps and stay neutral. The connections between them carry the evidence. Each takes the colour of the weaker of its two answers, so the colour shows how sure we can be that the step works here. One navy dot marks the critical connection, to test first. Pathways that could go wrong sit below a line. The example is illustrative.

Two questions on every connection

Theory-based evaluation tests the links in a causal chain and the assumptions under them (White 2009; Mayne 2015, 2017). A box is a result: it needs a clear definition and a way to measure it, not a grade of its own. So Policy Atlas grades only connections. The assumptions are not separate objects: each connection names the conditions it needs, and its evidence answers two questions (Cartwright and Hardie 2012: "it worked there" is not "it will work here").

Did it work elsewhere?Where this step was tried, did the change follow? The evidence is about the effect and the mechanism behind it. For example: "Strong evidence it worked elsewhere. Trials show children eat more after seeing food ads (systematic review, 2022)."
Will it work here?Will it work for this population, in this place, now? Evidence from England counts directly; evidence from elsewhere counts when it shows the step's conditions holding or failing. For example: "Weak evidence it holds here. It needs an effect from short trials that lasts in everyday life. Most of those trials last under an hour; one UK study of daily viewing found a smaller effect (2019)."

The canvas colours a connection by the weaker answer; the panel and Detailed information show both. A connection that worked elsewhere but has weak evidence here, and sits on most pathways, is the critical connection. All the conditions also appear together in an Assumptions list, so nothing the theory relies on is hidden inside a single connection.

Evidence is not repeated. A document appears once in a connection's list, marked elsewhere, here or both. The "here" sentence names a study already cited and says why it also counts ("The London study was in England, so it counts here too"). When every document answers both questions the same way, the two paragraphs merge: "Medium evidence, including in England."

graderule (first version, to be tuned with evals)
StrongTwo or more supporting studies of high quality (systematic review, meta-analysis, randomised trial or quasi-experiment), and no high-quality study against.
MediumAt least one supporting study of moderate quality or better, and nothing against it of higher quality.
WeakSome evidence speaks to it, but only low-quality support, only evidence against, or evidence against that outweighs the support.
No evidence foundThe search ran, and nothing it found speaks to this.
Supports or challengesFor each question, a document either supports or challenges the connection. A support that holds only for some groups notes that condition in its source line, for example "holds for children under 12". Conditions the connection does not yet name are added to it.
Robust models count as fair studiesA modelling study normally scores low on study quality. If its abstract shows it is built on real outcome data, tested for uncertainty or validated, it counts like a fair study: enough for medium, never strong.
Web pages count by what they areA web page is assessed like any document. From a trusted publisher, such as gov.uk, the Evaluation Task Force, a regulator or a What Works Centre, it counts by its design: an evaluation that ran a trial counts as a trial. Other pages, such as news, blogs or company statements, can make an item weak and count as evidence against, never more. Every quote is checked against the source text, or the judgement is dropped.
No overall score, and no counts on screen. The result page opens with two short paragraphs instead: what the theory is about, and what the evidence says, ending with the critical connection.
Part 2 · How it is built

Diagram 2: mostly existing machinery

The capability sits on what Policy Atlas already has. The Task Agent, the search loop, screening, classification and appraisal of study quality all come from the Evidence search, unchanged. The per-item searches reuse the small "option search" walks that Options scoping already runs in parallel. New code covers only what nothing in the repo does yet: drafting a causal graph, judging one document against one causal step, twice over, choosing between pathways, and writing short cited sentences.

The evidence check, step by step

This runs in the background after the draft. An item is one thing the check tests: a connection. Boxes are not items.

  1. Turn each item into a fixed question.new Code writes the text, so the same theory always gives the same search. A connection becomes "If fewer ads are shown, for children aged 4 to 15, then they see fewer ads, because…; it needs brands not moving their spend…: did this work where it was tried, and will it work in England now?" Each item also stores a fingerprint of its wording.
  2. Start from documents already found.reused When the theory comes from an option, the option's documents join the pool first. They are shared, not copied.
  3. Search once per item.reused Each connection gets its own small search on OpenAlex, Overton and the web, with queries for the step itself and for its conditions, using the parallel walks Options scoping already runs, in a separate pool so the two never slow each other down. Up to 40 records per item, and at most 80 items per check. Web pages are fetched once and stored as fixed text.
  4. Screen, classify and appraise.reused The Evidence search steps, unchanged: is the abstract relevant, what kind of study is it, and how good is it (a score from 1 to 5).
  5. Discover what the draft missed.new One pass over the screened documents looks for mechanisms and failure modes the draft does not have. Each becomes a candidate pathway, labelled "found in the evidence". Its new connections get no search of their own; they are judged with everything else against the documents already found.
  6. Merge.new Pathways that share a mechanism, drafted or discovered, merge into one; pathways that do not reach the intended change for this population and place are set aside, each with a reason. This needs no evidence, so it runs before judging and nothing is judged twice.
  7. Pick the best documents per connection.reused The existing reranker orders the pool against each remaining connection's question; the top eight go to the judge.
  8. Judge all connections together.new Drafted and discovered connections in one pass. One model call per connection and document gives two answers, each supports, challenges or not about this, with its own quote: did it work where it was tried, and does it, or do its conditions, hold here. The same call notes any case study and indicator for the boxes at either end, any condition a support holds under, and for a model whether it is robust. The existing quote checker confirms each quote; a judgement without a verified quote is dropped.
  9. Grade by a fixed rule.new Each connection gets two grades, one per question, worked out from the stored answers each time the page asks and never saved, so they cannot drift from their evidence. Only answers on the current wording count.
  10. Choose.new If more than six success or three failure pathways remain, the most promising and best evidenced stay; the rest are set aside with a reason. Conditions recorded on supports that a connection does not yet name are added to it. Every change to something the user made is flagged.
  11. Write the sentences, then check them.new One call writes the header paragraphs, the evaluation questions, two cited sentences per connection (worked elsewhere, holds here, without repeating a finding), and a two-sentence description per box. Code then checks every citation: a sentence may cite only its own item's judged documents, or for a box its case studies. A sentence that fails is replaced by a fixed one.
  12. Tell the user.reused The usual run notification. The Task Agent lists each change, each with its own Undo, and suggests a stress test.

Editing, step by step

Users asked to start editing at once rather than wait for the check. Three things make that safe: every judgement remembers the exact wording it judged, every row carries a revision number, and every change is an event in History.

  1. Every edit is a checked write. Adding, changing, moving or deleting a box or connection sends the row's revision number with the change. If someone or something changed the row meanwhile, the edit is refused with "this changed since you opened it", and the canvas reloads. Each accepted edit writes one History event.
  2. An edit flags the step; nothing reruns on its own. The connection keeps its evidence and grades, labelled "for the previous wording", and gets a blue dot and "Edited · check pending". The user decides: Keep the evidence, which is recorded as their choice, or Check again.
  3. Check again changes as little as possible. First, one small call asks whether the new wording changes the claim. If it is only a rewording, nothing is judged again and the step reads "Checked: no change". If the claim changed, only the documents already judged for that step are judged again, reranked for the new wording, with no new search; the Task Agent says which documents no longer apply and which answers flipped. If fewer than two documents still apply, it offers Search for more.
  4. Edits during the check need nothing special. A connection edited before the check reaches it is simply judged on its new wording. One edited after it was judged is marked pending like any other. When the check ends, the Task Agent says how many steps are pending, with "Check all" and "Keep all".
  5. Changing a box changes its connections. A box's new text changes the question of every connection that touches it, so those connections are marked pending too, keeping their evidence. Their sentences and the box's description are written again when they are checked. Deleting a box removes its connections and any pending results.
  6. Asking the Task Agent is an edit like any other. "Add a failure pathway about price promotions" becomes the same checked writes as a canvas edit. During the check, the agent answers questions and makes canvas edits, but plan changes wait until the check ends.
  7. A plan change after the draft offers a choice. Redraft the theory (the old one stays in History), or keep the canvas and mark the steps the change affects as "check pending".
  8. Policy Atlas's own changes are flagged, and each can be undone. Discover, merge and choose run on the theory as it is when each starts. They may merge, reword or set aside things the user made, but each change stores the reason and the user's version. The merge and choose events store the before-state of every row it changed, so the Task Agent can offer an Undo per change and Undo all. Undo skips any row the user has changed since, and says which. On a changed item, "Accept" keeps the change and "Keep mine" puts the user's version back and marks it "check pending"; "Restore" brings back a set-aside pathway.
  9. Chats never edit the theory. The stress test and the discuss chat read the theory and its evidence. To change anything, the user edits the canvas or asks the Task Agent.

Connection to Options scoping

One connection, kept light on purpose: Design a theory of change at the end of an option's page, and a list of the theories already made from that option.

What is reusedThe intervention, population, place, outcomes and evidence limits from the option and its scoping plan. The Task Agent then only confirms the intended change and asks whether a theory exists.
What evidence is sharedThe option's documents join the new task's pool as pointers to the stored documents, with the new task's own screening, because it asks different questions.
Connected if it is easy. The link is the last build step. If building it would disturb Options scoping, the capability ships standalone first and the link follows. The owner prefers it in.
A known gap, not fixed here. Duplicate checks only look inside one task. If a theory's own search finds a document that an unlinked task already stored, it is stored again. Every capability works this way today.

Web search and uploads

Web searchUses OpenAI's own web search tool. On the Amazon Bedrock route, the London endpoint does not offer it, so only the web search calls go to Bedrock's US endpoint; every other call stays in London. Each cited page is fetched once, stored as fixed text, and treated as untrusted data.
Uploaded diagramsA picture or PDF of an existing theory is read by a vision-capable model and kept in a private, encrypted S3 bucket. It appears in the plan under "Starting point". History records its name, size and a fingerprint.

What is stored

Seven new tables, following the Options scoping pattern. Everything else is reused: tasks, plans, the Task Agent's conversation, task links, stored documents and their screening and appraisal, chats and the event log.

tableholds
toc_boxEach box: layer, text, result criteria, indicator, its two-sentence description, who made it.
toc_linkEach connection: its two ends; if, for, then and because; the conditions it needs; its one-sentence reading; its two evidence sentences; whether it is the critical one.
toc_band_itemContext and supporting activities.
toc_pathway, toc_pathway_linkSuccess and failure pathways, kept or set aside, with reasons, and their connections in order.
toc_item_evidenceEach judgement: the connection, the document, an answer and verified quote for "elsewhere" and for "here", any condition, any case study or indicator with the box it describes, and the wording it judged.
toc_uploadUploaded files: where they are in S3, name, size and fingerprint.

Every row also carries a revision number and, where Policy Atlas changed the user's work, the reason and the user's version.

How we will know it works

No public dataset of real theories of change exists; they live as figures inside PDFs. So we start with three evals that need little labelling, then build our own reference sets from published reports.

evalquestion it answersdata
Structure checksIs every draft well formed: layers in order, no loose boxes, at most two conditions per connection and none repeated, at least one failure pathway?Our own drafts on about 30 descriptions. Runs automatically.
Judge on public dataGiven an abstract, does the judge say supports, challenges or not about this correctly? How often is a challenge mistaken for support?SciFact, Check-COVID, PubMedQA, Evidence Inference. Optionally compared with Consensus and System on the same claims, if the cost is approved.
Rebuild a published theoryFrom the written description of an intervention, how close is the draft to the theory its authors published?20 to 40 reports from the Youth Endowment Fund, GOV.UK and Campbell reviews.

Later: our own labelled pairs, including documents that support a connection elsewhere but challenge it here; reading uploaded diagrams; reviews that found no effect; and a blind expert panel.

Build order

A simple core first, tried on real topics and tuned, then six increments, each used in the app before the next starts. Fast checks run on every change; the full test suite runs at the start, when the database changes, and at the end of each increment.

  1. Core. The new task kind, the Task Agent, the draft, the evidence check on OpenAlex and Overton, the two grades and the cited sentences, the canvas, the panel and Detailed information. Then iteration: real topics, tuning, and the first evals, until the core is good enough to build on.
  2. Editing and changes. Canvas editing, "check pending" with Keep the evidence and Check again, edits through the Task Agent, the change list with Undo.
  3. Web search. The third source.
  4. Stress test and discuss.
  5. Bring your own theory. Pasted text, pictures and PDFs, and the uploads bucket.
  6. Export. The PDF report, pictures, CSV, JSON, draw.io and PowerPoint.
  7. Design from an option. The button, the back-list and the shared documents, if they do not disturb Options scoping.

The full design is in the capability spec under docs/specs/capabilities/theory-of-change/; the screens are in wireframes.html; the build is fixed in the task contract, with the review rulings W1 to W18.
