Part 1 · What the user sees

What a theory of change is

A theory of change is an explicit account of how an intervention is expected to bring about a change: the chain of steps, the assumptions each step depends on, and how strong the evidence is behind them. HM Treasury's Magenta Book treats it as the first step of any evaluation. The diagram shows the theory; the theory itself is the reasoning and the evidence.

Users told us that building one by hand is valuable but slow, and that they want two things from a tool: evidence they can audit, and a clear difference between a step with no evidence and a step with evidence against it.

Three ways in

Start from the outcome"I want more care leavers in stable work." Policy Atlas works backwards from the impact in one pass and proposes ways to get there. The user then changes it on the canvas or in the chat.
Start from the intervention"We plan weekly mentoring for care leavers." Policy Atlas works forwards and treats the draft as a test of the logic.
Bring your own theoryPaste it as text, or upload a picture or PDF of the diagram. Policy Atlas keeps your words and boxes, labels anything it adds, and checks the rest. The original file is kept beside the canvas.
Start from an optionAt the end of an option's page in Options scoping, Design a theory of change opens a new task with the intervention, outcomes, population and place already filled in.

Each way in starts with a short conversation with the Task Agent. It asks for what a good theory needs: the intended change, who it is for, and for an intervention, its dosage, meaning how much each person receives (for example, twelve weekly sessions).

Diagram 1: the canvas

The canvas follows the Magenta Book's example diagram (Figure 2.2): a context band on top, the chain in four columns, the assumptions in a band underneath, and the supporting activities at the bottom. Failure pathways sit under the chain in their own colour. The example below is made up, to show the parts.

Clicking things

  • A link shows its full statement ("If young people attend most sessions, then their confidence and job-search skills rise, because…"), its assumptions, its grade with the rule behind it, and the evidence in three groups: supports, supports under conditions, and challenges. Every item has a title, year, source type, study quality, a checked quote and a link to the source.
  • A box shows its grade, case studies (named programmes or places where that result was seen), the indicators other studies used to measure it, and Mayne's quality checks: is it well defined, measurable, and plausible in time.
  • An assumption shows its type, its link and its evidence.

A pathway selector shows the full theory, one pathway at a time, or only the failure pathways. A list view shows the same theory as pathway cards, then the pathways that were considered and set aside, each with its reason and a Restore button.

What the evidence grades mean

The Magenta Book asks for the strength of evidence behind each assumption but defines no scale. Mayne (2017), whose model the Magenta Book's figure follows, grades each link as strong, medium or weak. Policy Atlas uses those words, sets them by a fixed rule shown in the canvas key, and adds flags so that "weak because of evidence against" never looks like "no evidence at all".

graderule (first version, to be tuned with evals)
StrongTwo or more supporting studies of high quality (systematic review, meta-analysis, randomised trial or quasi-experiment), and no high-quality study against.
MediumAt least one supporting study of moderate quality or better (for example an observational study), and nothing against it of higher quality.
WeakSome evidence speaks to it, but only low-quality support, only evidence against, or evidence against that outweighs the support.
No evidence foundThe search ran, and nothing it found speaks to this step.
Two flagsEvidence against (N) appears whenever any source challenges the step. Holds under conditions appears when some support applies only to some groups or places, and lists them.
Web pages count, but lessA web page can make a step weak and can raise a flag. It never makes a step medium or strong. Every item must carry a quote that is checked against the source text, or it is dropped.
No overall score. The theory as a whole gets counts instead, for example "31 items: 4 strong, 9 medium, 7 weak, 11 no evidence found; 3 with evidence against".

A session, start to finish

  1. Plan. A short conversation with the Task Agent fills in the plan: the intended change, the population, the intervention and its dosage, the place and the context. The user approves it.
  2. Draft. Within a minute, the canvas shows up to six success pathways and three failure pathways, with mechanisms, assumptions and suggested indicators. Everything drafted is labelled "drafted by Policy Atlas".
  3. Evidence check, in the background. Every link, assumption and result box gets its own search. Grades fill in as results arrive. The user can edit straight away.
  4. Discover and consolidate. Policy Atlas looks through what it found for mechanisms and failure modes the draft missed, and adds them as new pathways labelled "found in the evidence". Similar pathways merge, and pathways that do not reach the intended change are set aside. If there are still too many, the most promising and best-evidenced ones stay. Each pathway gets a one-line reason. One Undo reverses the whole step.
  5. Summary and evaluation questions. A short summary, plus questions an evaluation should answer, made from the weak links and untested assumptions.
  6. Stress test, discuss, export. The stress test asks four pointed questions, one at a time, each tied to a part of the canvas. The discuss chat answers questions from the theory and its evidence. Exports: picture (PNG, SVG), reference list (CSV), data (JSON), a draw.io file that Miro, Lucidchart and diagrams.net can open, and a PowerPoint file with real boxes and arrows.
Part 2 · How it is built

Diagram 2: mostly existing machinery

The capability sits on what Policy Atlas already has. The Task Agent, the search loop, screening, classification and appraisal of study quality all come from the Evidence search, unchanged. The per-item searches reuse the small "option search" walks that Options scoping already runs in parallel. New code covers only what nothing in the repo does yet: drafting a causal graph, judging one document against one causal step, and choosing between pathways.

What one item's check looks like

Code turns each item into a fixed sentence, so the same graph always gives the same search. A link becomes "If young people attend most sessions, for care leavers aged 18 to 21, then their confidence rises, because…". A result box becomes "more job applications, for care leavers, from interventions such as weekly mentoring". The search asks for evidence for and against. A model then reads the top eight documents, one at a time, and answers: supports, supports under conditions, challenges, or not about this. For a result box it also notes any case study and any indicator. The existing quote checker confirms each quote is really in the source.

The grade is computed when the page asks for it, from the stored judgements. It is never saved, so it cannot drift from the evidence behind it.

Editing while the check runs

Users asked to start editing at once rather than wait. That works because every stored judgement remembers the exact wording it judged.

  • Changed wording. If a step's wording changes, its old evidence shows as "out of date" and stops counting.
  • Queued checks. Steps added or changed during the run are queued, and checked once the main run ends. After that, "Check again" runs on demand.
  • Your edits are flagged when changed. The discover and consolidate steps may merge, reword or set aside things the user made or edited, but every such change is marked "changed by Policy Atlas" with its reason. The user's version stays one click away, with Keep mine, and one Undo reverses the whole step.
  • Changes through chat. A change asked for in the Task Agent chat counts as an edit like any other, and every edit is recorded in the History tab.

Connection to Options scoping

For now there is one connection, kept light on purpose: Design a theory of change at the end of an option's page.

What is filled inThe intervention from the option's design. The intended change, population, place, context and evidence limits from the scoping plan. Each is labelled "from your options scoping". The Task Agent then asks for what an option lacks, mainly the dosage.
What evidence is sharedThe option's documents join the new task's evidence pool. They are shared, not copied: the new task gets a pointer to each stored document and its own screening result, because it asks a different question.

One option can have several theories, for example one per population, and the option page lists them. If the scoping task runs again, the theory keeps using the original run and shows "a newer run is available".

A known gap, not fixed here. Duplicate checks only look inside one task. If a theory's own search finds a document that an unlinked task already stored, it is stored again. Every capability works this way today; a shared store across tasks is a separate change.

Web search and uploads

Web searchUses OpenAI's own web search tool. On the OpenAI route it works as usual. On the Amazon Bedrock route, the London endpoint does not offer it, so only the web search calls go to Bedrock's US endpoint; every other call stays in London. Each cited page is fetched once, stored as fixed text, and treated as untrusted data.
Uploaded diagramsA picture or PDF of an existing theory is read by a vision-capable model and stored in a new private, encrypted S3 bucket, deleted with the task. The History tab records its name, size and a fingerprint.
Needs a design decision record. Web search opens a door the specs had deliberately kept shut, and the bucket is new infrastructure. Both go in the task's design decision record (ADR), together with the grade rule.

What is stored

Seven new tables, following the Options scoping pattern of capability-specific tables beside the shared ones. Everything else is reused: tasks, plans, the Task Agent's conversation, task links, stored documents and their screening, classification and appraisal, chats and the event log.

tableholds
toc_boxEach box: its layer, text, dosage, quality checks, indicator, and who made it.
toc_linkEach arrow: its two ends and its if, for, then, because and unless text.
toc_assumptionEach assumption, the link it belongs to, and whether it is load-bearing.
toc_band_itemContext and supporting activities.
toc_pathwaySuccess and failure pathways, kept or set aside, with their reasons.
toc_item_evidenceEach judgement: the item, the document, for or against, the quote, any condition, case study or indicator, and the wording it judged.
toc_uploadUploaded files: where they are in S3, and their name, size and fingerprint.

How we will know it works

No public dataset of real theories of change exists; they live as figures inside PDFs. So we start with three evals that need little labelling, then build our own reference sets from published reports.

evalquestion it answersdata
Structure checksIs every draft well formed: layers in order, no loose boxes, every assumption on a link, at least one failure pathway?Our own drafts on about 30 descriptions. Runs automatically.
Judge on public dataGiven an abstract, does the judge say for, against or not about this correctly? How often is evidence against mistaken for support?SciFact, Check-COVID, PubMedQA, Evidence Inference. Also compared with Consensus and System on the same claims.
Rebuild a published theoryFrom the written description of an intervention, how close is the draft to the theory its authors published?20 to 40 reports from the Youth Endowment Fund, GOV.UK and Campbell reviews, preferably from 2025 and 2026.

Later: reading uploaded diagrams, the judge on our own labelled pairs, testing against reviews that found no effect (such as "Scared Straight"), and a blind expert panel.

Build order

One programme of work in nine steps. Each step is usable on its own, and the riskier parts sit apart. The work changes the database, adds a public interface and new infrastructure, so it runs at the highest review tier, with a plan the owner approves before any code is written.

  1. Shell and draft. The new task kind, the Task Agent, the draft, and a read-only canvas with its key.
  2. Evidence check. Per-item searches on OpenAlex and Overton, the judge, the grades, the detail panel.
  3. Discover and consolidate. Pathways from the evidence, set-aside pathways, Restore and Undo.
  4. Editing during the check. Canvas editing, out-of-date marks, Check again.
  5. Web search. The third source, its design decision record and its cost logging.
  6. Stress test and discuss. The two chats.
  7. Bring your own theory. Pasted text, pictures and PDFs, and the uploads bucket.
  8. Export. Pictures, CSV, JSON, draw.io, then PowerPoint once a Google Slides test passes.
  9. Design from an option. The button on the option page and the shared documents.

The full design is in the capability spec under docs/specs/capabilities/theory-of-change/, and the research behind it in docs/tasks/054-toc-integration/research-notes.md.
