Summary

Of fourteen options across six themes, three stand out for full appraisal: intensive one-to-one caseworker support, which has the strongest evidence and reaches the long-term NEET; a youth guarantee, which is structural, tried across the EU, and whose effects are tied to funded delivery; and wage subsidies, which have the largest short-run reach but whose effects fade and favour the job-ready. Paid work experience, careers guidance with follow-up and apprenticeship incentives for small employers stay on the shortlist as alternatives. Nothing here is a ranking: the reasons are different for each, and the table shows them.

Compared against doing nothing. The Jobcentre Youth Offer and current apprenticeship funding, unchanged. NEET is about 1 in 8 and rising since 2022, and the rise is in inactivity the current offer does not reach (ONS 2026, DWP 2024). Cost of inaction: £50,000 to £60,000 per long-term NEET young person over a lifetime (Youth Futures 2022). Open baseline
