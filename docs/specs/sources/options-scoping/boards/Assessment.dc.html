Top line

Collapse −

Seven of fifteen options were assessed at standard depth: the two you added and five proposed, one per lever type present. Expand +

What the evidence base holds

Collapse −

The situation these options would change. The Jobcentre Youth Offer and current apprenticeship funding, unchanged. NEET is about 1 in 8 and rising since 2022; the rise is in inactivity (ONS 2026, DWP 2024). Cost of inaction: £50,000 to £60,000 per long-term NEET young person over a lifetime, as reported (Youth Futures 2022). Key assumption, Policy Atlas's reasoning: the current offer does not reach the inactive group. Open baseline

Transferability and assumptions

Collapse −

Your context came from three sources: facts retrieved from the baseline and the assessment, cited with the geography they apply to; facts stated by you; and plans stated by you, which become named conditions. The verdict word follows the weakest leg: a decisive factor that is unknown or only planned caps the verdict whatever the others say. No factor is counted against a total.

The youth guarantee is conditional on local delivery being funded. Careers guidance and joint commissioning are unknown because no factor was retrieved or stated for them. Each option's profile shows the working.

Low cost per participant, checked now that costs are reported: unresolved for five options, because their reported costs use different denominators and price bases and are not comparable; breaks for the youth guarantee, at €500 to €1,500 per participant-year as reported (Eurofound 2021). The options stay; you decide.

What needs deciding or commissioning next

Collapse −
  • Whether local delivery of a guarantee would be funded. Both guarantee designs rest on it.
  • Whether a guarantee without a take-up condition has run anywhere. No document names one; a full evidence search on the variant would confirm the gap or fill it.
  • Whether the target is all NEET young people or the inactive group. The caseworker evidence is for the long-term unemployed, not the inactive.
  • Whether low cost is a hard constraint. Reported costs are not comparable across the seven.
  • Eight kept options were not assessed, including every market option.
