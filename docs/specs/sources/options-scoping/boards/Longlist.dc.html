Longlist

Fourteen options for reducing the number of 16 to 24 year olds who are NEET, from the evidence base, the option types, and you. Each is read against doing nothing: the Jobcentre Youth Offer and current apprenticeship funding, with NEET about 1 in 8 and rising. Two options are excluded by your constraints and stay in their theme with the reason. On each row: add to the shortlist, exclude, or include again. Show excluded only.

Financial incentives and welfare

Collapse −

Changes the price of hiring, or of staying NEET. Three options: one included, two excluded.

Wage subsidies for employers hiring young people

Government pays part of the wage for six months when an employer hires a 16 to 24 year old who is NEET.

For NEET rate and sustained education or employment.

Sanctions for under-25s who miss Jobcentre appointments

Work-coach engagement from week one of a claim, with sanctions for non-attendance.

Excluded: breaks "No benefit cuts or sanctions".

Conditional cash transfers for training attendance

Cash to young people conditional on attending training, on the Jóvenes model.

Excluded: breaks "OECD evidence only".

Regulation and entitlements

Collapse −

Creates a duty or a right. Three options.

Youth guarantee · on the shortlist

A guaranteed offer of a job, apprenticeship, training or education within four months of becoming NEET.

For NEET rate and time spent NEET. Breaks "No benefit cuts or sanctions" but stays because you added it; to be assessed without sanctions.

Enforce the raising-of-participation-age duty

Use existing powers so every 16 and 17 year old is tracked and in some form of education or training.

For NEET rate.

Ring-fenced apprenticeship levy for under-25s

Reserve a share of levy funds for apprenticeships started by 16 to 24 year olds.

For sustained education or employment. Suggested by Policy Atlas.
