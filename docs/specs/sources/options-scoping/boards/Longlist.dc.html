Longlist

Fifteen options for reducing the number of 16 to 24 year olds who are NEET, from the evidence base, your evidence search, the lever types, and you. The situation they would change is the baseline: doing nothing. Each row shows the option, what it is, the outcomes it is for and its state. Financial incentives and welfare

Collapse −

Changes the price of hiring, or of staying NEET. Three options: two included, one excluded.

Wage subsidies for employers hiring young people

Government pays part of the wage for six months when an employer hires a 16 to 24 year old who is NEET.

For NEET rate and sustained education or employment. From your evidence search.

Sanctions for under-25s who miss Jobcentre appointments

Work-coach engagement from week one of a claim, with sanctions for non-attendance.

Excluded: breaks "No benefit cuts or sanctions".

Conditional cash transfers for training attendance

Cash to young people conditional on attending training, on the Jóvenes en Acción model.

For NEET rate. No in-scope evidence: its only evaluated example is outside "OECD evidence only". Included.

Regulation and entitlements

Collapse −

Creates a duty or a right. Four options, two of them one design and its variant.

Youth guarantee · on the shortlist

A guaranteed offer of a job, apprenticeship, training or education within four months of becoming NEET.

For NEET rate and time spent NEET. Added to the shortlist by you. Breaks "No benefit cuts or sanctions": most implementations attach a sanction to the offer; stays because you added it.

Youth guarantee without sanctions · on the shortlist

The same offer within four months of becoming NEET, with no benefit condition attached to taking it up.

Variant of Youth guarantee · added by you · not yet assessed; it gets its own search.

Enforce the raising-of-participation-age duty

Use existing powers so every 16 and 17 year old is tracked and in some form of education or training.

For NEET rate.

Ring-fenced apprenticeship levy for under-25s

Reserve a share of levy funds for apprenticeships started by 16 to 24 year olds.

For sustained education or employment. Suggested by Policy Atlas.
