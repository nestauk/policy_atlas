Options for reducing the number of 16 to 24 year olds who are NEET

Open longlistExport

Top line

Collapse −

Fifteen options were found for reducing the number of 16 to 24 year olds who are NEET, in six themes, from 412 screened documents, 96 of them inherited from your evidence search, plus three suggestions and one option you added. One is excluded by your constraint on benefit cuts and sanctions; one has no evidence inside the OECD scope you set and stays on the list. Two are on the shortlist so far.

Nothing has been assessed. No option here has a verdict on how big, how sure or how transferable its effects are. What follows is what the evidence base holds on each, read at title-and-abstract depth.

The problem and what is contested

Collapse −

About one in eight 16 to 24 year olds is NEET and the number has risen since 2022; the rise is in economic inactivity, about half of it with a health condition, and the current offer is built for the unemployed (ONS 2026, DWP 2024).

Sources disagree on three points: how much of the rise is a survey artefact (Resolution Foundation 2025); whether the problem is claimants who do not engage (DWP 2024) or inactive young people outside any service (Learning and Work Institute 2025); and whether the current offer reaches the inactive group, which no source evaluates. Open the baseline.

The approaches

Collapse −

Financial incentives and welfare changes the price of hiring, or of staying NEET: wage subsidies for employers, from your evidence search; conditional cash transfers for training attendance, whose only evaluated example is outside the OECD evidence scope; and sanctions for under-25s who miss appointments, excluded under "no benefit cuts or sanctions".

Regulation and entitlements creates a duty or a right: a youth guarantee, on the shortlist by your choice, and the variant you specified, a youth guarantee without sanctions, which has no documents of its own yet; enforcing the raising-of-participation-age duty; and a ring-fenced apprenticeship levy for under-25s, suggested.

Programmes and services works directly with the young person: intensive caseworker support, from your evidence search; paid work experience with employer placement; Youth Hubs, a package of co-located services and a named worker; and employment support inside mental health care.

Information and nudge: careers guidance with scheduled follow-up. Market and employer: apprenticeship incentives paid to small employers. Organisational and delivery: joint commissioning across services, and NEET tracking with data-sharing, both suggested.

What needs deciding or commissioning next

Collapse −
  • Whether the target is all NEET young people or the inactive group: the rise is in one and the current offer reaches the other.
  • Whether a youth guarantee without a take-up condition has been implemented anywhere. No document names one yet.
  • Whether low cost is a hard constraint. It cannot be checked before assessment; the guesses on the longlist are flags, not findings.
  • Which options to assess. Seven places are proposed on the shortlist; the assessment reads full text and takes about fifteen minutes.
