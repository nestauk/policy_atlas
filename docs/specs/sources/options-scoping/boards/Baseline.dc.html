About one in eight 16 to 24 year olds is not in education, employment or training, and the number has risen since 2022. The rise is in economic inactivity rather than unemployment, and the current offer is built for the unemployed. Sources disagree about how much of the rise is measurement and how much is health.

At a glance

  • Now: 12.5%, about 950,000 young people, in the latest quarter (ONS 2026).
  • Direction: rising since 2022, up about 1.5 points in three years; the unemployed share is flat (ONS 2026).
  • Driver, as reported: inactivity, mostly ill-health; mental ill-health is the largest single reported reason (ONS 2026, Youth Futures 2025).
  • Contested: how much of the rise is a survey artefact, and whether the problem is claimants who do not engage or inactive young people outside any service. See below.
  • Key assumption: the current offer does not reach the inactive group driving the rise. This is Policy Atlas's reasoning, not a reported finding.

What is in place

Expand +

The Jobcentre Youth Offer, levy-funded apprenticeships, and no entitlement for young people who are inactive.

Trend if nothing changes

Collapse −

The NEET rate rose from about 11% in 2022 to about 12.5%. The unemployed share is flat, so the inactive share accounts for the whole rise (ONS 2026). Long-term NEET, over twelve months, is a growing share of the total (Youth Futures 2025).

Not found. No published projection of the NEET rate beyond the current year. No reported estimate of how many of the inactive group are in contact with any service. Policy Atlas does not forecast.

Who is affected

Expand +

About half of the inactive group report a health condition; mostly 18 to 24; lower prior attainment; highest in the North East and Midlands.

What is contested

Collapse −

Why the rise. ONS attributes it to ill-health among the inactive (ONS 2026). The Resolution Foundation argues that a share of it is a survey artefact after the 2023 fall in Labour Force Survey response, and a share is health (Resolution Foundation 2025). Youth Futures points to weak entry-level hiring since 2023 (Youth Futures 2025).

Who the problem is. DWP frames it as claimants who do not engage with the Youth Offer (DWP 2024). The Learning and Work Institute frames it as inactive young people who are outside any service and never become claimants (Learning and Work Institute 2025).

Whether the current offer works. The Youth Offer evaluation reports positive outcomes for claimants who took part (DWP 2024). No source found evaluates its reach into the inactive group.

These are disagreements between sources, reported as such. Which reading you take changes which options fit; the longlist is built to cover both. Naming them as the contested points is Policy Atlas's reasoning.

What is already changing

Expand +

Youth Guarantee trailblazers in eight areas, Jobcentre reforms from 2026, Kickstart ended in 2022.

Cost of inaction

Expand +

£50,000 to £60,000 lifetime public-finance cost per long-term NEET young person, as reported.
