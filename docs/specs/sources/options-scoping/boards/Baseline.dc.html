About one in eight 16 to 24 year olds is not in education, employment or training, and the number has risen since 2022. The rise is in economic inactivity rather than unemployment, and the current offer is built for the unemployed.

At a glance

  • Now: 12.5%, about 950,000 young people, in the latest quarter (ONS 2026).
  • Direction: rising since 2022, up about 1.5 points in three years. The unemployed share is flat.
  • Driver, as reported: inactivity, mostly ill-health. Mental ill-health is the largest single reported reason.
  • Key assumption: the current offer does not reach the inactive group driving the rise. This is reasoning, not a reported finding.

What is in place

Expand +

The Jobcentre Youth Offer, levy-funded apprenticeships, and no entitlement for young people who are inactive.

Trend if nothing changes

Collapse −

The NEET rate rose from about 11% in 2022 to about 12.5%. The unemployed share is flat, so the inactive share accounts for the whole rise (ONS 2026). Long-term NEET, over twelve months, is a growing share of the total (Youth Futures 2025).

Not found. No published projection of the NEET rate beyond the current year. No reported estimate of how many of the inactive group are in contact with any service. The tool does not forecast.

Who is affected

Expand +

About half of the inactive group report a health condition; mostly 18 to 24; lower prior attainment; highest in the North East and Midlands.

What is already changing

Expand +

Youth Guarantee trailblazers in eight areas, Jobcentre reforms from 2026, Kickstart ended in 2022.

Cost of inaction

Expand +

£50,000 to £60,000 lifetime public-finance cost per long-term NEET young person, as reported.
