---

Policy Atlas v3 is an AI-enabled, evidence-based policy analysis system for UK government teams working on high-impact policy moments: scoping new policy, scrutinising proposals, spending decisions, and preparing the evidence base for senior decision-making. The backend is designed to serve the v3 UX, not the other way around — users should experience a guided policy-analysis workspace, while the backend supplies the orchestration, evidence handling, provenance, collaboration, and audit machinery that make that workspace trustworthy. The same ordering holds downstream: the persistence substrate is derived from the execution requirements, not the reverse (§9).

The backend's purpose is to help a policy team turn messy inputs — source documents, policy questions, assumptions, analytical methods, user steers, comments, and agent-produced analysis — into an inspectable **decision-support body**. This is more than a set of generated outputs: it includes the artefacts produced by capabilities, the **corpus** of evidence (and extracted data) behind them, the provenance for significant claims, the comments and challenges raised during review, the versions and dependency graph, and the decision log explaining how the work developed.

A **project** is the top-level policy workstream, not a search session or a single generated report — for example "DSIT growth bid", "scrutiny of a spending proposal", or "options for a new regulatory intervention". The terminal product of a project is the decision-support body. Humans then use it to brief, advise, write, clear, and publish *outside* Policy Atlas. The tool supports policy judgement; it does not replace it, and it does not manufacture the deliverable.

### What the backend is responsible for

- managing projects, artefacts, blocks, versions, comments, users, and decision logs;
- ingesting, retrieving, appraising, structuring, and reusing evidence through a shared information layer;
- orchestrating capability sub-agents through human-readable plans that compile into bounded execution configs;
- producing grounded artefact blocks with citations, supporting quotes, evidence-gap annotations, pattern annotations, and traceable reasoning;
- preserving the distinction between agent-proposed, user-steered, and human-decided material — including, when the deferred editing UX lands, human-authored and human-amended content represented honestly in provenance (§3.2, §7);
- supporting collaboration through anchored comments, review windows, catch-me-up, ownership, and lock-on-advance;
- making runs durable enough for v3.0 human-in-the-loop use: off-request execution, park-and-resume, block-boundary checkpoints, and tool-result memoisation;
- exposing the structured state needed for audit, evaluation, export, and future capability development.

### What the backend is not trying to do in v3.0

The backend is not a general-purpose agent platform, a full document-clearance workflow, a replacement for Teams or Word, or a system that automatically writes the final government white paper. It does not attempt to police every claim a human later lifts into an external document. It does not model every departmental clearance process, implement full role-based access control, or solve private deployment for the most sensitive use cases in v3.0.

Those boundaries matter architecturally: they keep v3.0 focused on the tight evidence-and-scrutiny loop where Policy Atlas is distinctive — producing inspectable analysis, supporting human judgement, and maintaining enough structured history that a senior user can ask, *"Where did this come from, what was considered, what changed, and what remains uncertain?"*

### v3.0 success criterion

The backend succeeds if a policy team can:

1. start from a policy intent and get a clear, editable plan;
2. run a set of declared capabilities without being exposed to backend complexity;
3. inspect important claims, sources, assumptions, evidence strength, and gaps;
4. steer the work at substance-shaping moments;
5. collaborate around artefacts rather than detached chat;
6. understand what changed since they last looked;
7. export or share cited artefacts, with deep links back into the live decision-support body held as a deferred seam (§10);
8. rerun or update work with the downstream blast radius surfaced rather than hidden.

Speed matters, but not at the expense of traceability: the system is built for high-pressure policy environments where usefulness depends on being *fast enough to matter and inspectable enough to trust*.

## 2. Principles & central tensions

The five UX principles for v3 are **guide don't overwhelm**, **modular but coherent**, **designed for judgement**, **built for collaboration**, and **evidence-led and inspectable**. The backend translates these into architecture principles. They should be used when deciding unresolved design questions, and when judging whether a proposed capability, data-model change, or execution feature belongs in v3.0.

### 2.1 Backend design principles

#### Principle 1: Make complexity progressive, not absent

The backend must support a simple user experience without pretending the underlying work is simple. Complexity is revealed at the point it becomes useful: plan before run, evidence behind claims, comments beside the relevant block, source appraisal when judging trust, dependency blast radius when rerunning work.

*Backend implication:* store rich structure, but expose it through progressive views. The system can hold tools, components, capabilities, tags, provenance, versions, runs, and logs without forcing users to think in those terms upfront.

#### Principle 2: Treat artefacts (and the evidence behind them) as the units of value

The system is not judged by whether an agent held a plausible conversation. It is judged by whether it produces useful, inspectable artefacts that accumulate into a decision-support body. Chat is the orchestration surface; artefacts are the work product; the project is the memory.

*Backend implication:* artefacts, blocks, addressable units, annotations, versions, dependencies, and decision logs are first-class. Transcripts are persisted for continuity and nuance, but they are not the canonical source of truth.

#### Principle 3: Keep evidence reusable, structured, and separate from analysis

Evidence is both a capability and a shared substrate. Retrieval, screening, extraction, appraisal, clustering, and grounding produce reusable state that other capabilities can read; analysis capabilities then apply their own methods to that substrate to produce artefacts.

*Backend implication:* evidence operations write to the information layer; artefact-producing capabilities read from and add to it. Deep extraction is capability-driven and scoped, not front-loaded across the whole corpus by default.

#### Principle 4: Generate provenance with the claim, not after it

Trust cannot be added as decoration once prose exists. Significant statements are generated from evidence, emitted with citations and supporting quotes, checked, and stored as structured annotations on addressable units. **Provenance and verification are version-specific:** they certify the exact unit version they were established against; if the text later changes — regeneration or human amendment — the original record remains part of history and the current version carries its own provenance or amendment state (§3.2, §3.3, §3.5).

*Backend implication:* provenance lives in a separate annotation layer keyed to addressable units, so claims, citations, evidence gaps, patterns, comments, and later eval results can be queried *across* artefacts — finding weakly grounded claims, unsupported assertions, gaps, and cross-artefact dependencies without parsing rendered prose.

#### Principle 5: Bound agent agency through declared capabilities

Use agents where they add judgement — search strategy, synthesis, recovery from thin evidence — but keep that freedom inside declared capability specs, component skeletons, tool scopes, and escape hatches.

*Backend implication:* agents do not receive every tool and improvise a whole workflow. The orchestrator plans over declared capabilities; capability sub-agents execute declared skeletons; components expose only the tools they need plus a tiny universal core; runtime deviations are logged, flagged, or escalated by their substance.

#### Principle 6: Humans decide substance; agents decide bounded method

The key augmentation boundary is method versus substance. Agents may decide how to search, sequence, draft, compare, and recover within declared bounds; humans gate conclusion-shaping choices — policy options, recommendations, assumptions, counterfactuals, value judgements, and material deviations from the agreed plan.

*Backend implication:* steering modes change how often the user is involved, not whether substance is escalated. Even Minimal mode pauses for declared substance gates or residual decisions likely to shape the conclusion.

#### Principle 7: Make the plan the audit spine of execution

The plan is the bridge between UX and backend execution: human-readable, editable, versioned, and canonical. The machine config is compiled *from* the plan, never a hidden parallel truth.

*Backend implication:* planning is structured selection over declared capability specs. The orchestration plan forecasts the whole run; per-capability plans commit just in time; configs compile deterministically from approved plan fields, and material divergence is recorded in the decision log.

#### Principle 8: Anchor collaboration to evidence and artefacts

Collaboration is valuable because it happens next to the work. Comments attach to specific blocks, units, rows, cells, claims, evidence gaps, or sources — and the agent can act on them.

*Backend implication:* comments are structured annotations, not free-floating messages. Review, refinement, lock-on-advance, reruns, catch-me-up, and version history all operate over the same artefact and event model.

#### Principle 9: Build light for v3.0, but leave clean seams

Meet v3.0 needs without prematurely adopting infrastructure for deferred complexity, while avoiding choices that make later parallelism, private deployment, richer security, or workflow-engine substitution expensive.

*Backend implication:* v3.0 uses serial orchestration, an off-request worker tier, LangGraph checkpoints on Postgres, a `runs` table, block-boundary commits, and tool-result memoisation. The canonical Project event log stays *ours*, separate from execution checkpoints; inference and durable execution sit behind seams so they can be swapped later.

#### Principle 10: Model only what behaves; collapse inert distinctions

Don't surface a flag with no behaviour behind it. A distinction earns a place in the model only if something in v3.0 reads it; a label that implies functionality the system doesn't have is worse than no label.

*Backend implication:* one corpus with a recorded `origin`, not modelled "source classes"; no "strand" entity (just tags + scoping); per-item sensitivity deferred until a behaviour actually reads the label (§3.3 — v3.0 has egress, but nothing yet gates on a per-item label); status badges only for states the tool actually drives.

#### Principle 11: Decompose for observability and evaluation

The decomposition hierarchy — tool → component → capability → artefact → project — is also the audit and evaluation surface. Eval-readiness is a consequence of clean structure, not a bolt-on.

*Backend implication:* capture observable I/O at every boundary; declared I/O contracts serve offline and online eval alike; eval results attach to the same addressable units as comments and provenance; the human signal (comments, overrides, rejected verdicts, edits) is captured as feedback data.

### 2.2 Central tensions and resolution rules

#### Agency ↔ accountability

More runtime agency gives flexibility but makes reproducibility, traceability, and audit harder. Policy Atlas allows agentic method inside declared boundaries while keeping substance gates human-owned; the audit trail must distinguish what the agent proposed, what the user steered, and what the human accepted or decided. This is the recurring axis most design choices in §3–§11 resolve against.

*Resolution rule:* **agent discretion is acceptable where it is bounded, logged, and does not determine the substantive policy conclusion without human review.**

#### Flexibility ↔ coherence

The system must support many policy workflows without becoming a loose bag of tools. Users compose capabilities, change depth, add sections, and scope evidence, yet the workflow must stay coherent and auditable.

*Resolution rule:* **flexibility comes from composing declared capabilities, components, depth settings, inputs, and scopes; coherence comes from the plan, the dependency graph, the shared information layer, and a common artefact model.**

#### Speed ↔ evidence discipline

Policy work happens under pressure, so the system must be fast enough to use — but a fast, unsupported answer is actively dangerous here.

*Resolution rule:* **cheap evidence work is front-loaded; deep evidence work is scoped and capability-driven; significant claims must be grounded, labelled as reasoning, or surfaced as evidence gaps.**

#### Collaboration ↔ accountable ownership

Policy is collaborative, but accountability cannot dissolve across a group. Many users may scrutinise, comment, and request refinements; one person remains accountable for the project's direction.

*Resolution rule:* **v3.0 has one accountable owner and one active driver for shared-state commands, with collaborators contributing through comments, review, and private Q&A.**

#### Auditability ↔ conversational usefulness

The orchestrator must feel like a useful co-pilot, but chat transcripts cannot become the hidden source of truth for policy decisions.

*Resolution rule:* **load-bearing chat is promoted into structured state at capture time; transcripts persist as companion records, while plans, artefacts, versions, and decision logs are canonical.**

*(The build-light ↔ future-extensibility trade-off is governed by Principle 9 above and not repeated here.)*

## Backend architecture overview

This section is the map of the backend. The detailed design lives in the numbered sections; this gives the overall shape before the data model.

At the highest level, the backend has seven layers (functional groupings — note that, per §3.1, the information layer holds both the corpus and the artefacts):

1. **Project & workspace layer** — workspaces, projects, artefacts, blocks, versions, exports, and the decision-support body.
2. **Information layer** — immutable source snapshots, parsed source structure, retrieval passages and embeddings, extracted findings and coverage records, appraisals, structured columns, tags, artefacts, and retrieval/scoping state. (The layer is canonical; lexical/vector indexes over it are derived, rebuildable state — §3.1.)
3. **Capability layer** — declared capabilities, component skeletons, capability sub-agents, tool scopes, dependencies, and output shapes.
4. **Planning & orchestration layer** — the persistent orchestrator/co-pilot, orchestration plans, per-capability commits, steering modes, check-ins, and the dependency graph.
5. **Trust & provenance layer** — claims, citations, supporting quotes, evidence gaps, patterns, grounding tiers and the Unsupported/mis-cited failure state, appraisal labels, comments, and later eval annotations on addressable units.
6. **Collaboration & audit layer** — the orchestrator thread, private Q&A, anchored comments, lock-on-advance, catch-me-up, version history, and the canonical decision log.
7. **Execution & deployment substrate** — off-request workers, serial v3.0 orchestration, the LangGraph checkpointer, Postgres/Aurora persistence, the `runs` table, memoised tool results, own-AWS deployment, and configured inference routes behind the in-house routing layer (§8, §11).

### Architecture in one view

```text
User / policy team
   ↓
Project workspace
   ↓
Persistent orchestrator / co-pilot
   ↓
Human-readable orchestration plan
   ↓
Per-capability committed plans
   ↓
Capability sub-agents
   ↓
Components within declared skeletons
   ↓
Scoped tools and shared operations
   ↕
Shared information layer
   ↓
Grounded artefact blocks
   ↕
Structured annotation layer
   ↓
Decision-support body
   ↓
Exported cited artefacts → external human-authored work
```

The orchestrator is the single user-facing agent surface: it helps frame the task, proposes a plan, coordinates capability sub-agents, mediates check-ins, records decisions, and explains what has changed. Capability sub-agents do the specialised work but never speak to users directly — when they need input they park through `clarify` or `escalate`, and the orchestrator relays the question into the orchestrator thread (§4).

### Runtime flow

A typical v3.0 project proceeds as follows:

1. **Start from intent.** The user chooses or describes a policy intent, which may map to a template orchestration plan (scoping a new policy, scrutinising a proposal, preparing evidence for a spending decision).
2. **Plan before execution.** The orchestrator gathers enough context to propose a human-readable orchestration plan — which capabilities run, in what order, with what inputs, assumptions, dependencies, depth, and steering mode — and the user accepts or edits the shape (§5).
3. **Compile the executable spine.** The orchestration plan compiles into the run-level sequence and dependency graph; downstream capability plans are committed just in time when their inputs exist (§5).
4. **Prepare the evidence substrate.** Evidence operations ingest (snapshot, parse, segment), embed, screen, classify, appraise, search externally, retrieve in-corpus, cluster, or extract as needed — cheap Tier 0 work early; deep Tier 1 extraction only when a capability requires it over a selected subset (§4).
5. **Run capabilities through declared skeletons.** Each capability sub-agent executes its component skeleton — some components deterministic procedures or fan-outs, others agent loops over scoped tools — using bounded escape hatches when evidence is thin (§4).
6. **Produce grounded artefact blocks.** Artefacts are built from blocks that expose addressable units (text spans, rows, cells); significant claims are emitted with citations, quotes, grounding tiers, and appraisal labels, alongside evidence-gap and pattern annotations (§3.2–§3.3).
7. **Verify, steer, and review.** Grounding is verified where possible; method decisions log, flag, or pause by mode and declared escape hatches, while substance decisions pause in every mode (§4, §6).
8. **Collaborate around artefacts.** Collaborators comment on anchored units, inspect evidence, challenge claims, and request refinements; the driver steers shared state; private Q&A lets users ask without changing the project (§7).
9. **Lock, version, and propagate staleness.** Advancing past an artefact locks that version; rerunning a capability mints a new version and surfaces downstream consumers through the dependency graph rather than silently rewriting them (§3.5–§3.6, §7).
10. **Maintain audit and catch-me-up.** The decision log records plans, commits, steers, material deviations, locks, reruns, and supersessions; catch-me-up is a projection over the logs and version history since a user's last-seen point (§7, §9).
11. **Export cited artefacts.** v3.0's primary surface is the tool itself (and, later, a read-only in-tool share view); export and deep-links back to live artefact units are a **down-weighted seam**, not a v3.0 build — the final white paper remains outside the tool (§10).

### Core architectural commitments

A synopsis of the commitments that shape the design; each is detailed in the section noted.

- **Artefact-centred state (§3.2).** Artefacts are the visible unit of value; blocks are the unit of storage, versioning, interaction, and regeneration; addressable units inside blocks anchor provenance, comments, citations, gaps, patterns, and eval scores — so the system can answer structured questions (which claims are weakly grounded, which comments are unresolved, what changed since the last export) without parsing prose.
- **Shared information layer (§3.1).** A shared substrate across capabilities, holding source material, metadata, tags, structured columns, embeddings, extraction and appraisal outputs, and the artefacts themselves. No "strand" entity — organisation is tags + scoping, where scoping is a soft retrieval prior, not a hard boundary.
- **Declared capability system (§4).** Capabilities are declared compositions of components, tools, dependencies, output shapes, and default skeletons; a component is a step inside a capability; a tool is a shared callable with declared I/O. An agent sees only its current component's tools plus a small universal core; cross-capability reuse is via shared tools or by asking the owning capability's expert sub-agent.
- **Structured provenance and trust (§3.3).** Provenance is a structured annotation layer, not inline citation theatre. Grounding tier (inference distance) and source appraisal (source quality) are kept separate because they imply different remedies.
- **Plan-led orchestration (§5).** The plan is the canonical bridge from UX to execution — human-readable, editable, versioned, logged — compiling into executable configs by construction. Planning is progressive: forecast the whole, commit each capability just in time.
- **Human-in-the-loop steering (§6).** Steering modes set involvement *frequency*, not analytical depth; substance-shaping decisions always escalate — the concrete expression of the augmentation boundary.
- **Canonical logs, companion transcripts (§9).** The decision log is the canonical audit spine; versioned content lives in content tables referenced by events; catch-me-up is a projection; transcripts persist for continuity but are not the source of truth, and load-bearing chat is promoted into structured state at capture time.
- **Build-light v3.0 substrate (§9).** Runs move off the request path into a worker tier; LangGraph provides the fixed reasoning harness and durable checkpoints on Postgres; a `runs` table handles status and pending human signals; tool results are memoised; checkpoints sit at block/component boundaries. Heavier workflow engines, intra-run parallelism, durable timers, and run-inspection consoles are deferred behind seams.

### v3.0 shape and future seams

The v3.0 architecture is intentionally focused: serial orchestration across capability branches; data-parallel fan-out inside evidence tools where already needed; one active shared-state driver at a time; project-level collaboration with anchored comments; lock-on-advance rather than full sign-off or clearance; cited artefact export rather than external white-paper policing; deployment-level sensitivity posture rather than per-document gating (with v3.0's real egress — inference calls and search queries — made visible through governance events, §9/§11); own-AWS plus configured inference routes (first pass OpenAI API, target Bedrock); and the in-house inference routing layer as the key future-proofing seam (§8, §11).

Future versions can add richer consensus/sign-off, artefact-scoped permissions, branch-level parallelism, durable workflow engines, chart view-types, private deployments, per-item egress controls, richer personalised catch-me-up, and more formal eval suites. The v3.0 model leaves these doors open by investing early in the parts expensive to retrofit: addressable units, structured provenance, canonical logs, the dependency graph, plan/config separation, and the execution/inference seams.

## 3. Core data model

### 3.1 Entity hierarchy ✅

`tools → components → capabilities → artefacts`, all **within a project** and drawing on a **corpus** of source documents, surfaced through a shared **information layer** — the surface capabilities read from and write to, which holds the **corpus** *and* the **artefacts** (with the unit-level annotation layer cross-cutting both). The derived side is given no separate collective name — "the artefacts" suffices, and a symmetry-only term would be inert. Containing structures: **workspace / project**, and whole-item metadata over the information layer — **structured columns** (facts and derived fields) plus a **tag layer** (open, mostly-inferred labels) — with **scoping** as the behaviour that points an agent at a subset (see below).

✅ **Just tags + scoping — no special container between project and artefact.** Whole-item organisation rests on two plain things: **tags** (whole-item metadata labels on the information layer) and **scoping** (the behaviour of pointing an agent at items carrying a tag). Decisions:

- **Tags are whole-item metadata** — a tag attaches to a whole document or artefact, *not* to units inside it. This is a **distinct layer from the unit-level `(block, unit, type)` provenance layer** (§3.2): different altitude, different purpose (organising the corpus vs tracing assertions to sources). The two *meet* at the document — a unit-level citation points at a source that carries document-level tags — but never merge. (Tag *taxonomy* and extensibility live in §3.3.)
- **Nothing hangs off a tag** — a tag has no owner, status, or lifecycle of its own; it's just a label. Consumers may *read* item metadata to drive behaviour, but that's the *columns* doing it (appraisal reads document-type; egress reads sensitivity — §3.3, §11), and even then the behaviour lives in the consumer, never on the metadata.
- **Many-to-many** — an item carries several tags, overlapping freely (a baseline study can be tagged Housing *and* Funding). Not a partition.
- **Scoping is a soft retrieval prior, not a hard boundary** — pointing an agent at a tag (or a column filter) means "look at these *first*," then widen to the rest of the layer when thin (the evidence escape-hatch, §4). Scoping queries over columns *and* tags alike (§3.3); agents are never penned in. *Tags* are the data; *scoping* is the act.
- **Tags are created by the capabilities that read the documents** — inferred as a capability processes a document (topic/theme from characterise's clustering, methodological/structural from `classify`); the orchestrator does *not* do the tagging. **Usage-proposes is one further growth path, not the main one** — when an agent ranges past its scope and a doc proves useful, a citation of that doc into an artefact carrying tag T flags it as candidate-T (read off the provenance graph) and the agent/user *confirms*. The one place the provenance layer feeds the tag layer.
- **Project-scoped shared namespace, orchestrator-kept** — tags are consistent *across* agents (one agent's "Housing" is another's), or cross-agent "scope to this" wouldn't compose; the orchestrator **keeps the namespace** (vocabulary consistency / consolidation) — a coordination job — but does **not** create tags. Each tag carries a **type** (topic/theme · methodological/structural · organisational/line-of-work · …, an open/extensible type-set) on its namespace entry, to drive type-aware scoping, within-type consolidation and capability relevance — kept light (label, type, proposing-capability), consistent with *nothing hangs off a tag* above.

✅ **The information layer is canonical; retrieval indexes are derived state.** Lexical and vector indexes (and any later reranking infrastructure) are rebuildable projections over source snapshots, passages, metadata, tags and appraisals — never the source of truth. v3.0 implements them Aurora-native (Postgres FTS + pgvector) behind the `retrieve` adapter seam (§4, *Tier-0 retrieval contract*); a dedicated retrieval service is a ⏸ deferred component introduced behind the same adapter only if the Aurora-native path fails on measured quality, latency, filtering, scale or operability. The tool split mirrors the data split: `lookup` reads the canonical layer; `retrieve` reads the derived index (§4).

### 3.2 The atomic units ✅

- **Artefact = the unit of value.** What the user sees and what the system is judged by. Recursive: a meta-synthesis output is also an artefact.
- **Block = the unit of storage, versioning, and interaction.** Users comment on, regenerate, and version *blocks*; the change log references blocks. Review is expected to be holistic (by section), so the block is the natural grain. A block holds either prose or **structured content** — an options table is *one* block, not a cluster of one-cell blocks. Structured content is treated as render-agnostic: a **chart is a deferred view-type** over the same structured-content block (⏸), not a new primitive — so its data points/series are the same addressable units and citations/comments re-anchor across a table↔chart switch for free, exactly like table↔prose. v3.0 leaves this seam open (and open to *several* chart types in outputs) without building it; a chart may later carry extra derived-claim annotations (e.g. a fitted trend) grounded in its data points. A block also carries a **co-versioned summary column** — a parallel representation of the same version (not a new version), excluded from the content hash and so *content-bearing but identity-irrelevant*; it is faithfulness-checked against the block's raw detail, not grounded to source (§4, *Progressive disclosure*).
- **Claims = typed annotations on addressable units, stored in a separate provenance layer** ✅ *(embedded-vs-separate fork resolved)*. A claim carries its *type* (the trust tier, §3.3), its *source refs*, and a *verbatim supporting quote*, and attaches to an **addressable unit** within a block, not to the block as a whole. *Conceptually* a claim still isn't a first-class entity — you never open, version, or navigate to one on its own; it belongs to its unit. *Physically*, though, claims, citations, comments, and (later) eval results and cross-artefact links live in a **separate structured annotation layer keyed by `(block, unit, type)`** — not inlined in the block's prose, not buried on the block record. The decider: almost everything we do with provenance is a query *across* units — find every weakly-grounded claim, roll up evidence-strength, attach eval scores, link statements across artefacts — which needs the annotations addressable as their own rows, not unpacked from blocks. The stable unit ID (below) is the foreign key that makes it work.
  > **These cross-unit provenance queries are collectively a projection — a read-model, named but light** ✅. Walking the foreign keys across the annotation, provenance and dependency layers to assemble a claim- or unit-centred view (claim → sources/quotes/tier/rationale/appraisal; unit → dependents/blast-radius, §3.6; source → everything resting on it) is what backs the citation-inspection, blast-radius and roll-up surfaces — and the orchestrator's *"why does it say this?"* answers (§4). It is **Postgres views over the existing tables, not a new store and not a graph database**; the specific query set is an implementation concern, not fixed here. Naming it records an existing consequence of the separate annotation layer, nothing more.
  > Two prototypes bracket this fork: the mature **v2 synthesis** persists exactly such a separate citations layer (a `synthesis_citations` table, one row per *claim-citation usage* — a source recurs once per claim/section it supports, keyed to a stable per-document `citation_key` — each carrying its grounded quote, which is *more* granular than per-source and already close to a unit-level model); the read-only **food-policy** tool embeds grounding tags inline in the markdown — which works *only* because it never edits or versions. Current SOTA on grounded generation likewise favours fine-grained, separately-stored attribution over inline tags. Since v3 edits, versions, and queries provenance, embedded was never viable for us — our own requirements settle the fork the prototypes illustrate.
- **User feedback signals = review and sampling inputs, not calibration labels** ✅ *(panel-review resolution)*. Users can validate or challenge citation pills and claim annotations. These actions write structured `USER_VALIDATION` / `USER_CHALLENGE` annotations against the addressable unit (version-bound, like every annotation) and emit corresponding governance events (§9); a challenge can be surfaced in the UI (e.g. a soft amber underline), routed to the responsible person, and passed back to the capability sub-agent as context during a rerun or refinement. These signals are valuable online feedback and sampling inputs, but they are **not tier-level ground truth**: a thumbs-up does not prove a claim is Tier 2 rather than Tier 3, and a thumbs-down may reflect disagreement with wording or conclusion rather than with source support. Whether and how the judge is calibrated is therefore owned by the eval workstream (§8); these signals help identify candidates and monitor live friction, but are never treated as ground-truth labels.
- **Provenance/support vs amendment state — two separate facets of a unit version** ✅ *(panel-review resolution)*. A block/unit version carries **provenance/support metadata** (what the text rests on: grounded evidence at a §3.3 tier, reasoning, pattern, evidence gap — or **human-authored**, where the text originates from the user) and, separately, **amendment metadata** (whether *this* version has been amended by a human relative to its predecessor). The two never collapse: a unit originally grounded in evidence and later amended by a human keeps its original provenance record in history, while the current version is marked **human-amended** with its verification flagged stale / needing re-check — never silently treated as still valid. The system can always show the difference between *"this was written by a human"* and *"this was grounded in evidence but amended by a human since verification."* (The editing UX is deferred to user testing; the representation is not — §7.)
- **Addressable units generalise the old "claim-span".** A block exposes addressable units appropriate to its content — a **text-span** in prose, a **cell or row** in a table — and citations, comments, claims, and (later) cross-artefact links all hang off *units*, uniformly. Each unit carries a **stable ID strictly bound to its block version** ✅ *(panel-review resolution)*: unit IDs are **not** expected to map across a substantive regeneration — the immutable versioning system (§3.5) keeps every historical unit permanently addressable instead, so every anchor points at `(block, version, unit)` and never at mutated live text. One deterministic exception: where a new-version unit's normalised content exactly matches a prior-version unit, a **`same_content_as` link** is recorded (hash equality — no semantic matching, no false anchors), which §3.6's staleness check reads to distinguish *"upstream superseded, this unit unchanged"* from *"changed or gone."* Version-pinned units remain the cheap seam for statement-level cross-artefact tracing (§3.4): a dependency is a plain foreign key to a versioned unit.
- **Anchoring is to the unit, not the rendered text.** Because a citation or comment binds to the unit (the datum), it survives re-rendering for free: the same cell's citation holds across the table↔prose toggle, and a comment "on row 2" re-anchors cleanly through an expand/shorten.
- **Evidence-gap = the dual of a claim** ✅. Not a claim with a chain to a source, but an assertion of *absence* — and structurally the *mirror* of a claim: a typed annotation in the same provenance layer (type = gap), attached to an addressable unit, but whose provenance records the **attempted search** (the queries run, the coverage checked) instead of a supporting source. A gap is **asserted by the agent**, and — like a claim — carries a **strength/scope tier** (§3.3), because "we searched and found nothing" is a far weaker statement than "this doesn't exist." It's its own kind because a well-grounded gap is itself a finding, and its credibility rests entirely on that attempted-search provenance — what separates *"we looked and found nothing"* from *"we never looked."*

### 3.3 Provenance & traceability ✅

Grounding taxonomy for claim annotations, ordered by epistemic strength (how far the claim has travelled from the evidence):

1. **Direct quote in evidence** — strongest; chain bottoms out at a document span.
2. **Inferred from a single document** — a reasoning step on top of one grounded source.
3. **Reasoning over synthesised evidence** — inference across multiple sources.
4. **Pure LLM reasoning / background statement** — no evidential terminus; honest only because visibly labelled as such (see *Tier 4 is not a common-knowledge safe harbour*, below).

**Unsupported / mis-cited claims are a distinct failure state, not a tier** ✅ *(panel-review resolution)*. The four grounding tiers describe honest *positive* states: direct quote, single-source inference, synthesis across sources, and visibly ungrounded reasoning. They do not cover the case where a claim presents itself as source-supported but the cited source(s) do not actually support it — that is classified separately as **Unsupported / mis-cited**. A claim is Unsupported / mis-cited when the cited source(s), *read in context*, do not support the claim **as worded** — and this applies to empirical, interpretive, causal, comparative, evaluative and synthetic claims alike. A source being topically related is not enough; it must support the material content of the claim at the tier assigned. The category includes fabricated support, quote-mining, omitted caveats, passages that support only a weaker claim, passages that contradict the generated claim, and synthesis claims whose cited source set does not warrant the conclusion. **Empirical markers** (numbers, dates, measurements, named events, specific factual assertions) are **strict-routing heuristics, not the definition**: their presence makes the judge especially strict about requiring source support, but their absence never implies support.

**Weakly grounded is a distinct flagged outcome, separate from Unsupported / mis-cited** ✅ *(new decision)*. A claim can verify into an honest tier yet not be grounded *as well as it could be* — the cited evidence supports it but thinly, or the bounded verify loop (§4) stopped before reaching the cleaner grounding it was still converging toward. This is **not** Unsupported / mis-cited (where no honest tier holds and the cited source does not support the claim as worded) and **not** a clean tier. The two outcomes carry different remedies — *weakly grounded* says *the claim stands but is under-evidenced, seek corroboration*; *Unsupported / mis-cited* says *the cited evidence does not support this, the claim may not hold* — so collapsing both into one flag loses the signal the reader needs. Modelled as a **completeness flag orthogonal to the tier, not a sixth classification lane**: the judge still assigns exactly one of Tier 1–4 / Unsupported-mis-cited (the panel-review resolution stands), and *weakly grounded* sits **on top of** an assigned tier where it applies — a thin or cut-short Tier 2/3 — coherent with it, unlike the removed binary `is_supported` which *contradicted* the tier. Both non-clean states are soft-flagged and never dropped (flag-don't-drop). Where the line falls between *clean at tier* and *weakly grounded* is a judge-rubric question owned by the eval workstream (§8); the architecture commits only that the state exists and is distinct.

**Deferred seam — support-direction relations and counter-evidence** ⏸. Per-(claim, source) *direction* labels (supports / caveats / contradicts) and user-initiated *counter-evidence* search (retrieve-to-refute a claim) are a v3.0 seam, not built: v3.0 challenge is user comments and chatting with the orchestrator (§3.2), and the consumers of direction labels — contradiction surfacing and divided-evidence roll-ups — are themselves deferred. The grounding rationale (§4) carries the per-citation *how* in v3.0; *direction* and contradiction are what this seam adds later.

**Evidence-gaps get the mirror of this ladder** (the taxonomy applies to absence too). A gap is asserted by the agent and graded by how far the *absence* claim travels:

1. **Corpus/coverage gap** — grounded; the failed search is recorded ("we searched our sources for X and found nothing"). The "direct" equivalent — fully substantiated by its attempted-search provenance.
2. **Acknowledged domain gap** — a gap in the wider field, backed by an in-corpus signal of sparsity (a review noting evidence is limited, a "further research needed" statement). The "inferred-with-support" rung; v2's "fewer than ~2 high-quality contexts" threshold sits around here (thinness, not absence).
3. **Inferred domain gap** — a gap in the wider field asserted from silence + the agent's reasoning, with no positive signal. The "reasoning" equivalent: the weakest tier but often the *highest value*, since a genuine evidence frontier on a novel approach is a key policy finding.

A positive signal of sparsity therefore **raises a gap's tier but never gates it** — the gap-side analogue of a supporting quote. This matters because the most valuable gaps are the *least* likely to carry a signal: true novelty means the field hasn't even framed the question, so there's no "X is understudied" note to find — the silence is total, and requiring a signal would suppress exactly the frontier gaps worth surfacing. As with claims, honesty rides on the **visible tier label**: a reasoned domain gap shows as an inference about the field, never as proven absence (the agent searched our corpus, not the whole literature — absence of evidence isn't evidence of absence).

**A third assertion type — patterns** ✅ *(the dual completes a triad)*. Alongside claims (about content) and gaps (about absence), some synthesis asserts the **shape of the corpus itself** — "the evidence clusters on short-term outcomes in large urban authorities, with little on rural or long-term effects." This grounds in neither a passage nor a group of passages but in a **scan over the set**, making a pattern the structural **dual of a gap** (a gap is "we searched and found nothing"; a pattern is "we scanned and here's the shape" — both grounded in a *scan*, not a source). The two are positive and negative space of the same distribution: a gap is the hole, a pattern is the shape — a gap is nearly a special case of a pattern. Stored like the others: a typed annotation (type = pattern) on an addressable unit in the `(block, unit, type)` layer, with provenance = the scan/query rather than a source ref. It carries a **two-rung strength ladder**:
1. **Metadata-grounded** — checkable *deterministically* against the structured columns/tags: "65% urban" is a count over a jurisdiction tag, "mostly post-2015" a query over the date column. The **strongest grounding in the system** — even cleaner than the quote-presence check, because re-running the query *is* the verification. *(This columns-only computation is also what gives a horizon scan its corpus shape, and every run its inspectability floor, without any deep extraction — see §4's Tier 0.)*
2. **Content-scan** — a pattern the agent reads *across* the corpus that the metadata doesn't capture (e.g. a recurring framing). No deterministic terminus, so — by analogy to the inferred-domain gap — it ships **soft and honestly labelled, never gated**.

❓ **Patterns over extracted findings** *(new with the §4 findings layer)*. Patterns computed over source metadata remain **metadata-grounded** (rung 1). Patterns computed over the extracted-findings layer (§4) — "most reported effects cluster on short-term outcomes" — are deterministically re-runnable over the finding records, but **inherit the extraction dependency** of the records they query: re-running the query verifies the count, not the extraction beneath it. Their provenance records the findings query and the source-record set; whether they grade as a labelled middle position between the two rungs, and how they feed evidence-strength roll-ups, is left open with the roll-up methodology (§4). Thematic **`cluster`** groupings (topic-level at characterisation, facet-level over findings, LLM-labelled) are a **third, softer grade below both rungs** — an interpretive shape, not a count: the grouping is a modelling choice (embedding + algorithm + parameters) and the labels are generative, recomputable but never a deterministic fact (facet grouping over findings additionally inherits the extraction dependency).

So the **to-source requirement widens to three honest categories** (below): trace-to-source, flagged-as-reasoning, *or* a pattern grounded in (and labelled by) a scan over the set.

**Attribution granularity for high-level synthesis** ✅. An interpretive "what this adds up to" synthesis is a **tier-3 (reasoning-over-synthesised) claim attributed at *block* level** — the block declares the source set it "draws on" (reusing §3.4's v3.0 meta-synthesis granularity) and its sentences flow without artificial per-statement cites. Whether such reasoning ranges over a few documents or a *large* group is **not architecturally load-bearing**: the annotation layer, block-level attribution and honest tier label are invariant to source-set size. So the few-vs-many gradation — and the fuzzy point where large-N tier-3 shades into a pattern — is left **open as methodology/eval**, not resolved here; the continuum is handled gracefully because both ends are annotation types on the same layer.

**Three relationships, three scopes — keep them distinct:**
- **Citation** (a claim → the evidence that supports it) always points to a **source/document**, never to another block. Within an artefact, every block's claims ground out in evidence, not in sibling blocks — a user never sees a block "cite" another block in the same artefact.
- **Coherence** (blocks within one artefact telling a consistent story) is *not* recorded as edges — it's a **semantic pass**, not a traversal (see §3.6).
- **Derivation / dependency** (artefact B was built using artefact A) is the **staleness graph**, and it lives mostly **across artefacts** (re-running Baseline flags Options Assessment). Internal wiring, never a user-facing citation. The deferred statement-to-statement tracing (§3.4) is also cross-artefact, so even claim→claim links never look like self-citation.

**v3.0 traceability requirement:** every significant statement either **traces to its source**, is **visibly flagged as reasoning**, or is a **pattern grounded in a scan over the set** — three honest categories. A statement that presents itself as source-supported but is not supported by its cited source(s) is **Unsupported / mis-cited**, regardless of whether it is empirical or interpretive. The cardinal sin remains an ungrounded or mis-cited statement masquerading as grounded evidence. This satisfies principle 5 for the core outputs and powers red-teaming (find weakly grounded or unsupported claims → go get evidence).

**Tier 4 is not a common-knowledge safe harbour** ✅ *(panel-review resolution)*. Tier 4 is honest only because it is *visibly labelled* as ungrounded reasoning or background framing — it is the second of the three honest categories, not a judge-certified exemption from traceability, and it never counts as grounded evidence or improves evidence-strength roll-ups (§4). The system does not let the judge decide that something is "universally accepted" and therefore safe without source support. Stricter rule for load-bearing material: **any statement that is policy-specific, empirical, causal, comparative, evaluative, source-attributed, or framed as "the evidence suggests…" must either be supported by its cited evidence or be classified Unsupported / mis-cited — it must not escape into Tier 4** merely because it lacks obvious empirical markers. Tier 4 is reserved for genuine background or structural reasoning.

**Citations are co-emitted, never post-hoc.** A claim is generated *from* its evidence, with its support edges recorded as it is written — there is no step that attaches citations to already-written prose (that route produces mis-attribution and citation theatre). "Re-grounding" a claim means *regenerating* it from new evidence, not stapling a source onto fixed wording. Grounding is therefore the single enforcement point for traceability (see §4 — `produce-grounded-block`). *(This corrects v2 at one specific seam: v2 already gathers evidence, generates from it, and runs a verify-and-regenerate loop (the §4 precedent) — but its **citation/quote attachment is post-hoc**, pinning supporting quotes onto already-written prose by string/fuzzy match, which is exactly the mis-attribution step this rejects. The fix is to the attachment step, not the verify loop. v2's three-way `attribution: direct / synthesised / inferred` is, separately, the direct precedent the §3.3 grounding taxonomy extends — the idea of a typed trust tier travelling with each claim already exists.)*

**Provenance and verification attach to a specific unit version** ✅ *(panel-review resolution)*. A grounding judgement certifies the wording it was run against. If a later version changes that wording — regeneration or human amendment (§3.2) — the original record remains in history and the new version carries its own state: re-verified, flagged stale / needing re-check, or human-authored. Verification is never silently inherited across a text change.

✅ **One corpus; classes collapsed to `origin`.** The wireframe "source classes" have no distinct v3.0 lifecycles, so we don't model them as classes (an inert distinction). There is **one project corpus**; *how* a document entered is a recorded **`origin`** attribute — **`uploaded`** (user-provided) or **`acquired`** (brought in by the `search` tool from a configured external evidence backend: OpenAlex, Overton — with the acquiring backend recorded on the snapshot). Origin is known at ingestion, recorded deterministically, and feeds provenance and the §11 egress story (a `search` query going out vs an `uploaded` file are different egress profiles). *(Terminology note: `acquired` replaces the earlier `retrieved` origin value, freed when `retrieve` was renamed to mean in-corpus retrieval — §4.)*

- **`Library` and `Connected` dropped as v3.0 classes, held as deferred seams.** Per-project Library is indistinguishable from an Uploaded reference, so it adds nothing in v3.0; "Library" is reserved for a later **cross-project curated collection** (roadmap: **per-project v3.0 → per-user → team/org**, each step adding only the store, then the access control, it needs). "Connected" — auth'd ingest from a department's *own* repositories/drives — is deferred wholesale. OpenAlex/Overton are *not* "Connected": they're public search backends behind `origin = acquired`.
- ✅ **Ingested sources are immutable source snapshots; document-versioning lifecycle deferred** *(replaces the earlier "documents are versioned" stub; full segmentation/anchor detail in §4, "Source snapshots & evidence segmentation")*. Every ingested source is stored as an immutable **source snapshot** — the immutable record of the source as Policy Atlas ingested it. **No original bytes are retained**: the raw file is fetched transiently to parse, and the **frozen parsed chunks are the content-of-record** — what claims ground, cite and audit against (§4). The snapshot's **identity** rests on a **content hash computed at ingest**, the **§9 search-governance event**, and the source locator — not on any stored original; re-parsing a retained original without re-fetching is explicitly out of scope (a regime upgrade re-ingests — §4). Claims, extraction fingerprints (§4) and provenance pin to the `source_snapshot_id` they used. Acquired sources are not monitored for upstream changes; a corrected or re-uploaded user document is ingested as a **new snapshot**, optionally carrying a human-asserted **`supersedes(source_snapshot_id)`** edge ("this replaces X") declared at upload — a link only: no diffing, no monitoring, no detection. The supersedes edge is the **second entry point into the staleness machinery** (§3.6): consumers of the superseded snapshot are found by **provenance back-reference** (every citation, extracted finding and dataset snapshot keyed to that `source_snapshot_id`) and flagged, never silently rewritten; the supersession is emitted into the Project event log like other supersessions. Everything heavier — replacement workflows, diffing, upstream refresh/update detection, automatic propagation from external source changes — is ⏸ deferred. Deletion/erasure interactions remain a §11 question.
- **Origin drives classification richness and default priority, not appraisal.** A given evidence document is appraised the same whatever its origin (an uploaded departmental systematic review joins the evidence pool alongside acquired SRs). But `acquired` docs are many and — coming from evidence APIs — almost all evidence, so a light evidence-category pass suffices; `uploaded` docs are few, higher-priority, more cross-project, and where the *non-evidence variety* lives (draft policies, memos, briefs), so they warrant richer treatment. Priority (a departmental SR over a general one) is handled by **scoping** — a soft prior surfacing authoritative sources first — orthogonal to the methodological appraisal tier, *not* a hidden re-weight in aggregation.
- **Inferred function on the uploaded side (illustrative — to be specced).** Each uploaded document is given an **inferred** `function` (never user-entered — friction-free, like classification; user-confirmable on a misroute) that routes it to a treatment lane. Open within-function variety lives in **tags**, and directive content is *read* by the orchestrator, not crammed into the label. The lanes are kept abstract for now; examples: **evidence** (→ appraise + synthesise; joins the evidence pool); **framing / subject** (a draft policy, the object of analysis → the orchestrator's *task-definition*); **directive** (a memo / ToR — decision points, constraints → the orchestrator's *execution-shaping*); **contextual** (background, baseline data → a *context lane* for capabilities like Baseline); **prior work** (an existing brief / analysis to build on). The team refines this set closer to implementation.

✅ **Acquired snapshots are a shared, content-addressed, cross-project substrate; uploaded snapshots stay project-private** *(new — pulls the public slice of the Library seam forward)*. The "one project corpus" framing is refined: a project's corpus is a **membership set of references** over a snapshot store that is *partly shared*, gated entirely by the existing `origin` column (no new flag — Principle 10).

- **`acquired` (public evidence) → one shared snapshot, referenced by many projects.** A document brought in by `search` (OpenAlex, Overton) is identified by a **content-addressed key — content hash × parse-profile version × segmentation-policy version × embedding-model version** — and exists as a **single `source_snapshot_id` that every project pulling it references**. Its **derived substrate (parsed structure, retrieval passages, embeddings)** is computed **once per unique key** — **no original bytes are retained**, the raw file being fetched transiently to parse and then discarded; a second project pulling the same paper resolves to the existing snapshot and re-derives nothing. This is the deliberate optimisation: re-download and re-vectorisation — the two expensive costs — happen once, and the derived substrate is stored single-instance too. Citations in different projects may point at the same snapshot, leaving a clean (unbuilt) seam for cross-project provenance later.
  - **The processing-regime key preserves the existing invariants.** "One parse, one segmentation per snapshot" and "a regime upgrade is a new snapshot" (§4) hold unchanged: a project ingesting under a newer segmentation/embedding regime simply *misses* the old shared snapshot and creates a new one under the new key. The same public document can therefore exist as more than one shared snapshot across regimes, each referenced by the projects that ingested under it — consistent, never silently mixed.
  - **Reference-counted lifecycle; deletion is GC, not compliance.** A shared acquired snapshot persists while ≥1 project references it; deleting a project drops *its* references, never the shared snapshot, and an unreferenced snapshot becomes garbage-collectable. This stays clean because the shared store holds **only public content** — there is no right-to-erasure pressure on a public paper as there is on an uploaded document (§11). Per-project erasure remains simple despite the shared entity.
  - **Reference edges are project-private; no cross-project enumeration.** A project sees only its own membership set; it cannot observe which other projects reference a snapshot, and a cache hit is invisible at the surface (it only makes ingestion faster). The shared store therefore discloses nothing across projects beyond the public content itself — the residual "who pulled what" signal never crosses a project boundary.
- **`uploaded` → per-project, never shared** *(settled this session)*. Identical content uploaded into two projects yields **two independent snapshots**, two derivations, no cache lookup, no cross-project reference. The existence-and-contents disclosure hazard of a content-addressed store is sidestepped at the root: the shared store never holds uploaded content, and no sharing machinery is built for uploaded documents in v3.0.

**Reconciliation with the Library roadmap (above).** This **partially un-defers the Library seam, for the public/acquired slice only**: the shared acquired snapshot store *is* a cross-project shared substrate, arriving early because public evidence is where the duplication actually bites and where sharing carries no confidentiality cost. The deferred "Library" proper — a *curated*, per-user/team/org collection with its own access control — is unchanged and still deferred; v3.0 gains the unscoped, automatic dedup of public evidence beneath it, not the curation or the access layers.

**Forced follow-ons (recorded):**
- ✅ **Retrieval indexes are shared over acquired passages, project-scoped by membership.** Acquired passages exist once, so they are indexed once; `retrieve` scopes into the shared lexical/vector index by the project's membership set, with the usual tag/column scoping as filters on top (§4). §3.1's "indexes are derived, rebuildable state" is intact — the index simply spans projects on the acquired side; the soft-prior scoping behaviour is unchanged.
- ✅ **The project-scoped overlay stays project-private.** Tags, scoping/line-of-work groupings, the uploaded-doc function lane, citations, and extracted findings remain per-project *over* the shared snapshot — only the source-intrinsic substrate is shared. Cross-project finding reuse is **not** in scope (it sits next to the rejected global knowledge graph, §4 — deferred).
- 🟡 **Appraisal may attach to the shared snapshot.** Appraisal is source-intrinsic and rubric-versioned (below; §4), so an acquired snapshot's appraisal can be shared and reused like its embeddings, keyed by rubric version. A minor extension to the shared substrate, pinned when appraisal is specced — not core to this decision.
- **`search` egress precision** *(amends §4/§9 governance event)*. A `search` *query* still egresses to the backend and still emits its egress governance event; dedup removes only the **full-text fetch** of an already-held document. The acquisition record distinguishes *fetched-fresh* from *resolved-from-shared-store* so the audit trail stays honest about what actually left and entered.

⚠️ **Sharpens the §7/§11 tenancy flag (carry forward).** The shared acquired store is genuinely **cross-tenant**. It holds only public content, so cross-tenant *content* visibility is a non-issue — but its existence makes the tenancy boundary load-bearing for the reference model (whose references count toward GC; who may read the store), which the Clerk→Cognito note (§7) must now account for explicitly rather than treat as organisation-only.

✅ **Item metadata splits two ways structurally: columns vs tags.** Whole-item metadata on the information layer isn't all "tags" — the deciding axis is *cardinality + vocabulary*, not provenance:

- **Structured columns on the item record** — single-valued, closed/known vocabulary: **origin** (uploaded / acquired), **document type**, **date/year**, **format**, plus derived/inferred fields like the **appraisal quality tier** (§4) and, on uploaded docs, the inferred **function** (above). These are *fields*, queried directly. The behavioural hooks read columns — appraisal reads *document type* to pick its yardstick (§4); provenance and egress read *origin*. Mostly facts known at ingestion (recorded, never inferred — don't hand a model a fact you hold); the **appraisal tier and function are the exceptions — single-valued *derived/inferred* columns**.
- **A tag layer** (item × tag, many-to-many) — open/emergent vocabulary, multi-valued, mostly inferred, each tag carrying a **type**: **topic/theme**, **methodological/structural** (e.g. mixed-methods, economic / qualitative / implementation-process component — capability-inferred, *not* a closed column), and **scoping/line-of-work groupings**. The type-set is itself open/extensible. The vocabulary is **open but seeded with common values and namespace-consolidated** (consolidation buys scoping *precision*; *recall* is carried by the soft-prior widening over hybrid retrieval, §3.1, whose dense arm bridges synonymous tags). This is the genuine tag layer, and the *only* part the §3.1 soft-prior / hybrid-mutation / many-to-many story applies to.

**Scoping queries over both** — "look first at *Uploaded* docs (column) tagged *Housing* (tag) since *2020* (column)" — so scoping generalises beyond tags to a filter across columns + tags. Both sides stay **extensible** (add a column or a tag type without re-architecting), but the columns are the stable, closed set and the tag vocabulary is the open, growing one. *(Borderlines: jurisdiction can be multi-valued → a multi-field or a tag; stance is relational, toward a specific intervention → an edge, parked.)*

⏸ **Sensitivity/classification — deferred, not built in v3.0 (no inert flag); rationale corrected** *(panel review)*. By the axis it *would* be a column (single-valued, closed government-classification vocab — OFFICIAL / SECRET / TOP SECRET, with OFFICIAL-SENSITIVE as a handling caveat). It stays deferred — but **not because v3.0 has no egress**. v3.0 *has* live external-egress paths: inference calls through the configured inference route (§11), and search queries to configured evidence backends (§4) — where the query itself can disclose policy intent, framing, or live analytical interest, which in a spending-review or pre-announcement context can be sensitive in effect. The reason not to add a per-item sensitivity column in v3.0 is that **no v3.0 behaviour reads it**: nothing yet blocks, reroutes, generalises, requires approval, or selects a deployment path off the label — so the column would be an inert security label implying unenforced protection (Principle 10), while pushing real classification friction onto upload for zero behavioural payoff. v3.0 therefore handles egress at **deployment, inference-route and tool-governance level**: search-query egress is logged as a canonical **governance event** (§4, §9) and accepted as a documented v3.0 risk because external evidence gathering is core to the product; the active inference route and its data-control posture are recorded canonically (§11). Per-item sensitivity gates return when labels drive concrete behaviour — blocking external search, approval steps, outbound-query generalisation, higher-assurance route selection, or a private deployment path (an additive column + gate, not a re-architecture). The distinction to hold onto: **control** (can the system prevent/reroute egress?), **visibility** (can the owner inspect what left?), and **risk acceptance** (has the product explicitly accepted this boundary crossing?) — v3.0 provides visibility and recorded acceptance; control beyond route/tool governance is the deferred part.

### 3.4 Meta-synthesis — v3.0 vs deferred ✅

- ✅ **v3.0: meta-synthesis at block + source-document granularity, with version-pinned staleness.** A v3.0 meta-synthesis can combine artefacts and uploaded documents and say *"this section draws on these blocks and these sources."* A downstream artefact's dependencies are **plain foreign keys to a specific `(block, version, [optional unit])`** (§3.2), so the staleness check is a deterministic database query against the upstream block's current head — no LLM matching (§3.6). This reuses the to-source provenance the core artefacts already need — no new machinery.
- ⏸ **Deferred behind the addressable-span seam:** statement-to-statement cross-artefact tracing; composing chain-strength across a multi-hop chain (a chain is only as strong as its weakest link, so the honest signal isn't the terminus); how to display a chain to a reviewer; version-pinned cross-artefact staleness; deeper cross-project reconciliation. Buildable later *because* spans are addressable from day one.
- ❓ **Inherited-artefact edit — open.** When a meta-synthesis builds on an inherited artefact and the user then wants to edit it, whether that is **copy-on-write** (fork a local copy) or **in-place** (edit the shared original, propagating to all consumers) is unresolved — it trades provenance cleanliness against single-source-of-truth. Parked.

### 3.5 Versioning, multiplicity & change log ✅ *(granularity, linearity, multiplicity & the supersede/sibling rule settled; regeneration re-anchor reliability is eval-adjacent, §8)*

**Three grains, three roles.**
- **Block = the capture grain.** Edits, regenerations and comments land on blocks (§3.2); each block carries its own version chain. A block's **summary column co-versions with it** as a parallel representation, not a separate version — it is hash-excluded and has no independent staleness (§4, *Progressive disclosure*).
- **Artefact = the snapshot grain.** Lock-on-advance (§7) freezes an artefact — a named, immutable binding of specific block versions — and supersede-by-rerun mints the next one with the prior retained as history. The artefact version is what gets pinned, exported, and flagged "changed since" (§10).
- **Project = the aggregate, not a snapshot.** A living workstream; its "history" is the union of its artefacts' chains plus the decision/change logs — the timeline catch-me-up (§7) reads over. A project is never frozen as a unit.

**History is linear, not a branchable tree.** Supersede-by-rerun appends to a single artefact's chain (old version retained, head is current); there are **no parallel live forks of one artefact**. Branching lives elsewhere and already exists: cross-artefact composition (meta-synthesis; Value for Money (VfM) ← Options Assessment + Baseline Analysis) is the **derivation DAG** (§3.6), not a fork of any one artefact's history. So "linear vs tree" dissolves into linear per-artefact chains + the existing cross-artefact derivation graph.

**Capability → artefact is one-to-many.** A project can hold **several sibling artefacts of the same capability** — two Baseline Analyses on different sub-questions, Options Assessments under different framings — produced by running the capability again. Siblings are **distinct artefacts: not versions of each other, not forks**; each has its own identity and version chain. "Multiplicity" is therefore a third, orthogonal axis to versioning (same capability type, different *instances*). Two consequences:
- **"Re-run" splits into two operations, disambiguated by default + explicitness — no inference.** The **default keeps work inside the existing artefact instance**: refine-on-comments and regeneration improve *this* artefact (block-version updates while unlocked; **supersede-by-rerun** → a new artefact version once locked, §7) — a linear chain, never a sibling. Creating a **new sibling is the *only* path that requires an explicit orchestration-plan act** (§5): either planned in from the start, or appended/modified at a review-step check-in by conversing with the orchestrator. So the risky inference (overwrite-vs-proliferate) is designed out — superseding is the quiet default; proliferation can happen *only* through a human-gated plan edit.
- **The orchestration plan is the registry of artefact instances.** Because siblings enter only via plan edits, the (human-approved) plan's instance set *is* the project's set of artefacts; versions then accrue under each instance via supersede.
- **Dependencies resolve to a specific artefact instance, not a capability type.** When VfM depends on "the baseline" and several exist, the plan (§5) selects *which* instance feeds it; derivation edges (§3.6) are instance-level (already the case).

**Annotation fate across a regeneration** ✅ *(resolved: version-bound IDs + block-level resolution checklist; no semantic re-anchoring)*. Comments — like claims and citations — are typed annotations on **addressable units** in the separate `(block, unit, type)` layer (§3.2), and every annotation pins to `(block, version, unit)`; a comment and a claim on the same datum still co-locate at the unit. Because supersede-by-rerun mints a new version with **entirely new unit IDs**, the system does **not** attempt to map old units onto regenerated text — no LLM semantic matching, no false anchors. Instead, any unresolved comments from the previous version are recorded in the Project event log (§9) as *carried forward* and surfaced at **block level as a resolution checklist** beside the new version's text; the driver clears them manually, and nothing is silently dropped (flag-don't-drop). The one deterministic re-anchor: where a new-version unit's normalised content hash exactly matches a prior unit, the recorded **`same_content_as` link** (§3.2) lets carried annotations and downstream pinned dependencies resolve as *"content unchanged"* automatically. Historical annotations remain permanently addressable on their original version. Distinct from the ⏸ §7 mid-regen *lock-and-batch* (not orphaning a comment *in flight*) — this is the at-rest question of where an existing annotation lands across the version boundary.

**Change log = a projection, not a store.** The per-artefact change log is a **projection over the Project event log** (§9) — edits, refinement requests, locks, reruns and supersessions scoped to one artefact; planning-stage decisions live in the plan (§5). There is no separate canonical change-log store.

### 3.6 Edit propagation: staleness vs coherence ✅

When a block changes, two distinct propagation mechanisms fire — and they differ *in kind*: *(The same cross-artefact dependency edges also scope a downstream capability's **document selection** — Transferability ← the selected options' supporting docs — see §4's capability-driven selection.)*
- **Provenance / derivation staleness — traverse.** Cross-artefact derivation edges (the staleness graph) are followed to flag downstream claims/artefacts whose grounding may now be out of date. Structural; a graph traversal. Flag, don't auto-run (humans decide). **Precision under version-bound IDs** (§3.2): a dependency pins to `(block, version, unit)`, so the check is a cheap head-version query — and where the superseded version's unit survives by exact content (`same_content_as`), the flag **auto-downgrades to informational** (*"upstream superseded; your input unchanged"*) rather than demanding attention; only genuinely changed or vanished inputs flag as stale. This keeps the signal close to claim-precision without semantic matching, and avoids false-positive staleness storms on depth-only reruns. *(The same traversal also backs version-drift detection — e.g. flagging an exported or relied-on version as "changed since" — see §7, §10.)*
- **Two entry points, one behaviour** ✅ *(new with source snapshots, §3.3)*. Artefact supersession enters staleness via the **derivation-edge traversal** above. **Source supersession** — a human-asserted `supersedes` edge recorded at upload (§3.3) — enters via **provenance back-reference**: a query over the annotation and findings layers for every citation, extracted finding and dataset snapshot keyed to the superseded `source_snapshot_id`, flagging the containing units, blocks and artefacts. It lands precisely on the affected claims (more precise than whole-artefact flagging), and the ordinary derivation traversal carries onward from any flagged artefact. Both entry points converge on identical handling: flag, don't auto-run; route through check-ins by steering mode.
- **Narrative coherence — verify.** Within an artefact, a substantive change can break consistency in sections that didn't *derive from* the changed block (drop an option, and the Framing, Discussion and summary may now contradict the table). There are no "coheres-with" edges to traverse; instead a **coherence pass** (a semantic check) reads the changed block against the rest of the artefact and flags what no longer hangs together.
- **Cascade behaviour ✅** *(resolved: orchestrator-mediated, never silently auto-edited)*: scope by **change magnitude** (local edits don't ripple; structural ones do — same "material change" threshold as staleness) and **surface the blast radius**. When a substantive edit breaks narrative coherence elsewhere, the orchestrator flags the affected sections and **proactively asks the driver whether the agent should draft updates** to the inconsistent sections (augment-not-replace); if approved, the agent generates the fix and commits it as the new version, with the whole interaction logged — the checkpoint is a steer-point, not an approve/reject button.
- **Summary consumers ride this machinery, flag-and-propose** *(new with §4 Progressive disclosure)*. The **block summary** is co-versioned and hash-excluded, so it has no independent staleness. The **artefact summary** is the one stale-able summary object: version-pinned to its covered block-version set, it is flagged on material block-composition change and its regeneration is *proposed* through the check-in (steering-mode gated), **never auto-run** — an abstractive regeneration is not a deterministic projection and its emphasis can shift, so it obeys the flag-don't-auto-run rule like any other content. Recorded ripple: a leaf-block edit can flag the within-artefact **key-findings/conclusion block** via the coherence pass; that block's regeneration is itself a material composition change, which then flags the artefact summary — two flags from one edit, both flag-and-propose.
- ⏸ **Body-level coherence — deferred seam** *(panel-review addition)*. Derivation edges and the within-artefact coherence pass don't catch artefacts that contradict *without* a derivation edge (a re-framed Baseline making a locked Theory of Change narratively wrong despite never being its input). Future versions may run the same semantic-pass mechanism across a **selected decision-support-body view** — current artefact heads, an active plan path, or an export set — to flag narrative contradictions and unresolved tensions between artefacts; *selected* views matter because sibling artefacts may intentionally represent different framings, scenarios or assumptions. Body-level coherence flags and routes concerns; it never auto-edits locked artefacts.
- 🟡 *Open:* where the magnitude threshold sits. (Detecting incoherence reliably is a semantic judgement — eval territory.)

## 4. Capabilities, tools & the execution model

### Execution model ✅
- **Orchestrator + capability sub-agents.** The persistent project chat *is* the orchestrator agent (see *The orchestrator & co-pilot* below): at *plan time*, reading the capabilities' declared specs, it decides which capabilities and components meet the user's directive, and delegates execution to **capability sub-agents** that run the agreed per-capability plan. Plan-time authority sits with the orchestrator; the capability agent's only runtime discretion is the escape hatches below.
- **Sub-agents never address the user directly — the orchestrator mediates.** A capability sub-agent surfaces a request for input or a decision *only* through the universal-core `clarify` / `escalate` (below): it **parks** on a durable signal (a §6 checkpoint awaiting the human), and the orchestrator relays the request into the orchestrator thread (the driver's surface) — **attributed to the capability it came from** — collects the response, and signals the parked sub-agent to resume. So there is **one user-facing surface** (the orchestrator), not N chatbots — matching the UX's single-assistant-surface intent.
- **Plan-then-run over a curated skeleton.** A capability has a default skeleton of components; the planning conversation adapts which run, their depth, and the check-in points; execution then follows the agreed plan. Flexibility lives at *plan time* (cheap, human-gated, logged), not over the structure at run time.
- **Bounded runtime escape hatches.** At defined points the agent may deviate (e.g. evidence is thin → run an extra search) provided it flags/logs the deviation.
- **Uniform framework, variable rigidity.** Every capability uses the same mechanism (plan-then-run + curated tools + escape hatches); *rigidity* is a per-capability parameter (Evidence Base fairly structured; a scrutinise/red-team mode looser; others in between).
- **Evidence is both a capability and a shared layer.** The evidence *tools* (search / retrieve / appraise / ground) sit at the free end — callable by *any* capability at any time without invoking the whole evidence chain — which is what makes evidence flow into everything. The Evidence Base *capability* (producing the evidence-base artefact) is plan-then-run like the others. Keep the two separate; don't collapse the artefact into "just a tool."

### How a capability sub-agent is realised ✅ *(the rigid-vs-loose fork, resolved)*
A capability sub-agent is **not** a graph built per-plan, and **not** a single agent handed a goal + every tool. It is a **declared skeleton (a subgraph) of components** — the ordered, "more linear" backbone — where **each component is realised by the mechanism its nature demands, not uniformly**:
- **Deterministic procedure / fan-out map** for mechanical work — appraise N documents, extract from N (routing per document-type to fixed sub-graphs), compute the coverage snapshot. The iteration count is runtime-determined by corpus size; the per-item logic is fixed. This is exactly v2's mature evidence engine, and it is the facade-tool principle in action ("orchestration pushed into deterministic code"): the agent invokes it as *one* tool, and the fan-out happens inside it.
- **Agent-loop over scoped tools** for deliberative work — synthesise a discussion, compare options — where the agent chooses which of its declared tools to call (tool-call discretion + the bounded escape hatches). This is what the plan's *approach* field captures by describing the method "without reaching the individual tool calls."

**Rigidity is tuned by how much skeleton a capability declares and its deterministic-vs-agentic mix** — the "variable rigidity" parameter made concrete. The two extremes are degenerate cases of the *one* construct: a **pure agent-loop** (thin/single-node skeleton, broad palette) is reserved for the deliberately-loose capabilities (scrutinise/red-team); v2's **fixed linear pipeline** (all-deterministic, no agency) is the opposite degenerate. The default sits between — a few ordered components, some deterministic-fan-out, some agentic — which is why "fully fixed" and "fully agentic" were never the choice; they're the endpoints of one dial.

> Substrate realisation (§9): a **fixed** LangGraph harness interpreting the plan-as-data, *not* a graph rebuilt per run. The skeleton is a subgraph; deterministic components fan out within a step (LangGraph `Send` / plain async); agentic components are agent-loop nodes; `interrupt()` sits at the check-in/clarify points.

### What a capability is ✅ *(edges still open)*
- A capability is authored as a **declarative spec**: the output/artefact shape; its constituent components (the analysis steps); the tools each needs; the data it must acquire or ingest; its dependencies on other capabilities; and the skeleton plan that assembles these for the agent to work around.
- That spec sits on a **parts layer** (tools + components) where new code does get written — so adding a capability isn't pure composition. But there's a **maturation curve**: early capabilities drag along new parts; as the shared library fills, later ones become mostly spec (artefact + skeleton), less code. "Declaration or code" is "both, at different layers, in a shifting ratio" — and the *who-can-add* answer eases over time.
- Capabilities declare **dependencies** on other capabilities' artefacts — e.g. Value for Money consumes the selected option (from Options Assessment), the theory of change, and the baseline. A concrete instance of inter-capability flow.

### Tools, components & capabilities — the vocabulary ✅ *(consolidated; cross-capability rule new)*
The three layers of the §3.1 hierarchy, made operational and kept crisp:
- **Tool** — a single, shared, callable operation with declared I/O, living in the registry (below). *What the system can do.* A tool may be a thin primitive or a **facade over a substantial internal workflow** (`produce-grounded-block`; a corpus-scale `appraise` fan-out): size or duration is **not** what makes something a tool — the defining property is the single declared interface. "Tool vs workflow" is therefore a category error — a workflow is the *implementation* behind a (possibly facade) tool, never a rival to it.
- **Component** — a step in *one* capability's declared skeleton: a phase of analysis toward that capability's artefact, realised as a deterministic procedure or an agent-loop (both of which *call tools*). *How a capability is structured.* Owned by its capability and **run by that capability's expert sub-agent**.
- **Capability** — an artefact-producing composition of components, run by a dedicated expert sub-agent, coordinated by the orchestrator.

One-liner: **tools are the shared verbs; components are a capability's sentences built from them.**

- **Tools are not capability-agnostic — they are registry-shared, declaration-scoped.** Many tools are capability-specific in *relevance* (an NPV/discounting calc only VfM wants; a polling tool only Public Opinion). The registry is a shared namespace tools are *declared from*, not a palette every agent sees: an agent only sees its current component's declared tools plus the tiny universal core (*shared tool library*, below). So the registry can grow large without ever degrading an agent's choice — reuse without exposure.
- **Cross-capability reuse splits by *what* is being reused** ✅ *(new):*
  - A shared **operation** → reference the **tool** (any agent calls it; context-free, so safe to share).
  - Another capability's **analysis** (one of *its* components) → the **orchestrator invokes that capability's expert sub-agent** to run the component — possibly just that one component, not a full artefact — and feeds the result back via the plan/DAG. A capability **never runs another capability's component itself**: a component carries its owning capability's expertise/context (a tool does not), which is *why* components are agent-bound while tools are shared.

### Gradation vs distinct operation — the I/O test ✅ *(resolves the former "gradation vs mode" open item)*
First, a terminology note: **"mode" is already taken** — a *mode* is a pre-baked point in the plan's selection space (a named *bundle of gradation settings*), which is itself a gradation construct: **capabilities declare a small set of named, validated configurations** (bundles of scope / depth / sub-lens selections), and selecting one just picks a point in the gradation space. So the real dichotomy is **gradation vs distinct operation**, not "gradation vs mode."

The discriminator is the **I/O test — does the *shape* of the input/output change, or only its intensity/depth?** — and it resolves *three* ways, reusing the component/capability line above:
- **Only intensity/depth varies (same I/O shape)** → a **gradation**: a plan *parameter* (a bounded selection over the declared spec, §5; depth-per-section, criticality, check-in frequency, or a named bundle = a "mode"). Cheap — nothing new authored. *(How depth is **surfaced to the user** is resolved in §5, Thoroughness as a relative nudge: orchestrator-inferred from task scale, anchored by the concrete proposal + a cost signal, and adjusted by a relative lighter/deeper nudge rather than an absolute level the user sets; named bundles survive only as an orchestrator/authoring convenience, never a user-facing absolute dial.)*
- **I/O shape differs, output is intermediate state consumed inside a capability** → a **component** (authored, lives in a capability's skeleton).
- **I/O shape differs, output is a standalone artefact** → a **capability** (fully authored — declared I/O, artefact, skeleton, expert agent).

So the test decides whether a thing costs a *parameter*, a *component*, or a *capability* — and it **guards against capability proliferation**: most apparent variation is intensity (a parameter), and only a genuine I/O-shape difference earns a component or capability.

A single intuitive notion can **span** the three. *Scrutinise / red-team* is the clearest: a *posture-dial* on a producing capability ("stress-test harder, surface more counter-arguments" — same artefact) is a **gradation**; a *critic step* that reads draft claims and emits downgrade/challenge adjustments consumed within the producing capability (v2's critic-pass) is a **component**; a *red-team deliverable* that takes a finished artefact and emits a standalone critique is a **capability** (the loose pure-agent-loop one). *Gap analysis* splits two ways: gap *annotations* every capability already emits (§3.3) are a byproduct/gradation; a *Gap Analysis deliverable* characterising the evidence frontier as a standalone graded-gaps artefact is a **capability**.

### Human-in-the-loop & the verdict ✅
- The generic routing rule (§6) carries the **reactive** human-involvement load; capabilities don't re-implement it.
- A capability **may render a verdict/recommendation**, provided it's transparent and contestable: the reasoning chain is visible, and for any verdict the **considered-and-rejected alternatives are carried as first-class artefact content with their rejection reasons** — not just the winner. Checkpoints are **steer-points** (the user injects their own reasoning and reshapes the output), not approve/reject buttons.
- ✅ **Capabilities declare anticipated mandatory checkpoints** — known, domain-specific substance gates that fire regardless of steering mode (e.g. confirm VfM's discount rate and counterfactual). These are the **substance-pre-declaration half of the §6 routing rule**: pre-declaring the known conclusion-shaping decisions means escalating them never depends on the agent recognising substance in the moment — the routing rule's runtime classification is left only the unanticipated residual (bias-to-escalate). A declared checkpoint may be **unconditional** (a mandatory gate, fires regardless of mode — VfM's discount rate) or **conditional** (a steer-point with explicit **escalation triggers** that fire *above* the mode baseline when a declared condition holds, otherwise routing by mode). Both pre-declare the known so escalation never waits on runtime recognition; the conditional form simply keeps the default light. EB's **deepening-selection** steer-point (which clusters go to deep extraction — a known, recurring, emphasis-shaping judgement) is the first conditional instance: triggers on dropping a large / high-priority / **user-nominated** cluster, a thin evidence base, or a policy supportable only by below-policy sources, with intent-violations escalating even in Minimal.

### The shared tool library ✅
- The library is a **registry of tools available to be *declared***, not a runtime palette every agent sees — this is the move that prevents tool overload.
- **The spec is the scope.** At runtime an agent sees only its current component's declared tools plus the small universal core. A tool authored for one component that another needs is simply *referenced* from the registry — reuse by reference, not global exposure. (Cross-capability reuse happens at the **tool** level; components aren't exposed across capabilities — but the orchestrator can invoke a capability's *own* expert agent to run one of its components for another; see *Tools, components & capabilities*.)
- **Palettes are phase-scoped for free** — plan-then-run means the agent does one component at a time, so it never holds all tools at once.
- **Prefer coarse "facade" tools** over granular primitives (one `gather_evidence`, not {search, dedupe, rank, fetch}): fewer agent choices, and orchestration pushed into deterministic code.
- **Dynamic tool-retrieval only as a fallback**, for the genuinely open cases (escape hatches, scrutinise mode) the skeleton can't pre-scope.

### The universal core ✅
The small set every agent carries regardless of capability. Discipline: keep it tiny — the bar is "needed by essentially *all* capabilities," not "useful." **The universal core applies to stateful capability execution** ✅ *(panel-review clarification)*: non-stateful conversational surfaces — notably private Q&A — receive a narrower, read-only scope (see *The orchestrator & co-pilot*, below; §7).
- **search** — acquire *new external* evidence into the layer, over **configured external backends**, each carrying a declared **trust class** (v3.0 configures only curated evidence APIs: OpenAlex, Overton). **An external-egress operation — and the only agent-invocable egress verb in v3.0** ✅: the query leaves the Policy Atlas boundary. Every search call emits a canonical egress **governance event** (§9) recording destination/backend; query text (access-controlled) plus a query hash for lower-privilege audit; the triggering run/capability/component/tool context; the trigger class (planned search, evidence-thin escape hatch, grounding retry, or user-requested search); and links to any acquired sources that entered the corpus.
  - ⏸ **Open-web backend — deferred seam.** A general web-search backend of trust class *untrusted open web* can later be configured **behind the same verb** for specific components — declaration-scoped (a component declares `search` with a backend set, so the plan makes "this run touches the open web" visible and human-gated; enabling the backend at all is a deployment/config decision, §11) — with mandatory injection screening at ingestion (§8) and routing into the low-trust appraisal lane. **The web backend ingests the page as frozen parsed chunks; there is no cite-the-live-web path**: anything that will support a claim enters the corpus as an immutable snapshot whose **frozen chunk text is the content-of-record** (no original page bytes retained, §3.3), so page edits and link rot cannot invalidate historical provenance. Backends scale; egress verbs don't multiply.
- **retrieve** — in-corpus **relevance-ranked retrieval** over the project corpus *(formerly the unnamed search-corpus leg; renamed from the external tool, which is now `search`)*: hybrid lexical + vector search with application-side fusion, scoping filters, optional reranking and evidence-envelope hydration behind one declared interface — see the **Tier-0 retrieval contract** and **retrieval profiles** (below). Reads the *derived* index, never the canonical layer directly (§3.1).
- **lookup** — deterministic, identifier- or filter-addressed, side-effect-free access to **canonical project state** *(formerly read-from-layer)*: documents and snapshots, artefact blocks, annotations, extracted findings, columns and tags — **including deterministic aggregate queries over columns/tags** (counts, distributions, date ranges), the queries whose re-running *is* the verification of metadata-grounded patterns (§3.3). Returns complete, exact results; no ranking, no egress, no writes.
- **appraise** — assess evidence quality (below).
- **produce-grounded-block** — turn analysis into a written block: synthesise → cite → verify → write, with **cite and verify as mandatory internal steps**, not optional tools.
- **escalate** — hand a *decision* to the human (transfers authority; usually substance; blocks the dependent branch).
- **clarify** — request *missing information* (the agent stays the decider; usually method; can be non-blocking). Listed with the acquisition family deliberately — information enters via the outside world (`search`), the corpus (`retrieve`), canonical state (`lookup`), the extractions (`query-findings`, registry-scoped, §4 *Queryable extracted-findings layer*) or the human (`clarify`); there is no sixth path.

Principles attached to the core:
- **Data vs action:** *provenance* is data (the typed claim→support edges), not a tool; *cite* is the action that creates it. No "attach citation" path — citations are co-emitted at generation time (see §3.3).
- **Trust is enforced at grounding.** `produce-grounded-block` requires each cited claim to have been generated from its evidence *and* to cite only **appraised** evidence. So **search/retrieve and appraise stay separate tools** (pure search or retrieval = coverage/gap checks; pure-appraise = uploads, re-appraisal, red-team), but the coupling is enforced as a rule at grounding, not by fusing them.
- **Not universal — scope, don't core:** compute/calculation (only the quantitative capabilities need it); `query-findings` (only capabilities reading the extracted-findings layer need it — §4); and any future open-web `search` backend (trust risk; declaration-scoped per component behind the seam above, never default).
- **Ingestion is not a tool** ✅. Documents enter the corpus by user upload or as the consequence of `search`; there is no agent-invocable "ingest this content" verb. This closes the door on an injected document instructing an agent to ingest arbitrary material — indirect injection through ingested documents being the named primary attack surface (§8).
- **Transcripts are not a tool target** ✅. Recall of older chat turns is **harness-level context assembly** for the orchestrator surface and private Q&A sessions (§9, *Chat persistence & agent context*), not a registry tool — `lookup`'s domain is canonical project state only. Stated consequence: **free-form chat never enters a capability sub-agent's context through any tool**; capability agents see only promoted structured state — a cheap hardening of the injection story, not just tidiness.

### The grounding mechanism — how verify works ✅
`produce-grounded-block` runs **synthesise → cite → verify → write**, with cite and verify as mandatory internal steps (above). The **verify** step has two parts:
- **A deterministic quote-presence check.** The verbatim supporting quote must actually occur in the cited source (normalised string match). If it doesn't, that's a *hard fail* — it catches the scariest failure mode, a fabricated quote pinned on a real document. Cheap and deterministic; not a model's job.
- **An LLM-as-judge grounding classifier** ✅ *(panel-review resolution; replaces the lenient `is_supported` + tier pair)*. A structured-output model reads the claim, the cited passage(s), and enough surrounding context to judge the support relationship, and assigns **exactly one mutually exclusive classification**: **Tier 1 (direct quote / directly stated)**, **Tier 2 (supported inference from a single source)**, **Tier 3 (supported synthesis across sources)**, **Tier 4 (visibly ungrounded reasoning / background statement)**, or **Unsupported / mis-cited** (§3.3). The single lane removes the separate binary `is_supported` field, which risked model self-contradiction and leniency. The judge is **permissive about legitimate policy inference, but strict about attribution fidelity**: it does not require strict NLI-style textual entailment for Tiers 2–3 (the taxonomy is designed to preserve inference distance), but it must check that the cited source(s) support the claim *as worded*, preserving material scope, caveats, population, intervention, comparator, outcome, direction, magnitude, uncertainty and context — *a source merely relevant to the topic is not sufficient support*. Claim-type analysis (including empirical-marker detection: numbers, dates, measurements, named events, specific factual assertions) is a chain-of-thought **routing aid that increases strictness, never the definition of unsupportedness** — interpretive, comparative and synthetic claims can also be Unsupported / mis-cited when the cited set does not warrant them, and load-bearing claims must not escape into Tier 4 (§3.3).

**The judge reads the evidence envelope; measurement conditions are recorded** ✅ *(with the segmentation decision, §4 above)*. The grounding judge reads the claim against the cited **evidence envelope**, not an isolated retrieval chunk — material support in policy evidence often rides on caveats, population, comparator, intervention detail, outcome definition or table notes sitting *adjacent* to the quoted passage; the deterministic quote-presence check runs against the cited source's **stored passage text** — the document's chunks, matched over their **concatenated** text so a quote spanning a chunk boundary is not a spurious miss — **not** a load of the full snapshot: the parsed passages already hold the text, so reconstructing contiguous full text per verify (or storing it twice) is redundant over a large evidence corpus, and matching against the passages verifies the quote against the text the model could actually have read. The **frozen chunks are both the content-of-record and the match target**; the source snapshot retains **no original bytes**, so audit and the no-cite-the-live-web guarantee rest on the frozen chunk text, while **identity and cross-project dedup rest on the content hash** (computed at ingest) plus the §9 search-governance event and source locator (§3.3). Because envelope construction partially determines verdicts, each verification record captures the **segmentation-policy and envelope-policy versions** alongside the judge prompt/model versions already persisted for eval-readiness — otherwise judge drift and envelope drift are confounded in the SLIs (§8).

**The judge co-emits a free-form grounding rationale** ✅ *(new decision; chosen over a fixed citation-role vocabulary)*. Alongside the tier, the judge emits a short **grounding rationale** on the claim — a plain-language account of how each cited quote underwrites it: for a Tier 2, what kind of inference links the quote to the claim; for a Tier 3, how the several quotes combine; for a weakly-grounded claim, why it falls short. It is **co-emitted with the tier, not a post-hoc explanation** — the same co-emission discipline as citations (§3.3); the judge already produces this reasoning to assign the tier — and persisted on the verification record, version-bound. *Chosen over a fixed citation-role label set* (v2's *direct / contributing / supporting premise*): the **tier already is the structured coarse axis**, so a fixed role vocabulary would re-tread it and mint a second calibratable taxonomy for the eval workstream to own while adding little the tier doesn't; the free-form rationale instead carries the claim-specific *how* the tier cannot, is what a reader needs to **understand and challenge** a grounding (the v2 lesson: a bare label/score left users unable to see how it was reached), and doubles as the weakly-grounded explanation. It is **attached explanation, not parsed**: the structured provenance queries still run off the tier / citation / appraisal fields (§3.2), so the rationale doesn't cut against the structured-annotation principle. *Rejected:* a fixed role vocabulary (re-treads the tier, adds calibration burden, served for badge/filter by the tier chip anyway); and a label-plus-prose hybrid (the label half re-treads the tier for no load-bearing v3.0 gain).

**Verify is a bounded loop, and its job is claim↔evidence convergence, not just pass/fail.** When a claim over-reaches its evidence, the loop's **primary repair is to reword the claim *down* to what the cited evidence actually supports** — narrow the scope, fold in the caveat, weaken the magnitude — bringing the claim into honest alignment with the sources; *re-gathering* targeted evidence (the escape hatch — querying with the failing claim, regenerating from what comes back) is the alternative move, used when the claim is right but the cited source is wrong or insufficient. Either way the claim is **regenerated from evidence, never re-worded onto a fixed citation** (§3.3 co-emission). The loop is bounded to a small retry count, and **it does not always finish at a clean tier** — on exhaustion the claim lands in one of the two non-clean outcomes (§3.3): **weakly grounded** (a tier holds but thinly, or convergence was still improving when the budget ran out) or **Unsupported / mis-cited** (no honest tier holds). Both are soft-flagged and never dropped or silently downgraded into a positive tier — the human decides (flag-don't-drop).

**Grounding governs what is committed, not how the model reasons** ✅ *(clarification)*. The co-emission and verify discipline applies to the **claims a block commits** — the shipped, asserted content — not to the reasoning that produces them. The capability sub-agent reasons freely upstream of commitment (exploring, weighing, following and dropping leads as it researches); that reasoning is captured for eval/debug but is **not** itself grounded or shipped as a claim. Only committed claims are generated-from-evidence, tiered and verified. The boundary matters for output quality: an implementation must **not** force the model to ground each reasoning step as it thinks — the grounding harness sits at the *commit* boundary, leaving the reasoning that precedes it unconstrained. (Distinct from Tier 4, which is ungrounded reasoning that *does* ship, visibly labelled; this reasoning does not ship at all.)

**Grounding judgements are persisted with full versioning — an eval-readiness seam, not a calibration commitment** ✅ *(supersedes the earlier "calibration status travels with the judge" resolution; rolled back this session)*. Every grounding judgement is persisted with its judge prompt version, model id/version and family, and the input/output payload — the plain *observable-I/O-at-every-boundary* instrumentation §8 commits independently (Principle 11), and exactly the historical record the eval workstream needs to measure the judge **retroactively** when it gets there. What v3.0 deliberately does **not** do is pre-commit a calibration scheme: there is **no `calibration_status` field on judgements**, and judge-derived SLIs are **not** framed as "uncalibrated until measured." Whether the judge is calibrated, how (rubric, gold set, confusion matrix, thresholds), and whether any judge-derived metric is ever promoted to a product-quality measure are **owned wholesale by the eval workstream** (§8) — the architecture's only commitment is that the persisted judge I/O makes that work possible later. *(The earlier panel-review "measurement mechanism" over-reached into calibration design, which belongs to evals; the persistence survives on its own eval-readiness justification.)*

**Why LLM-judge, not a dedicated entailment/NLI checker.** A strict entailment model is rigorous on literal support but flattens legitimate inference to "neutral," collapsing the graded taxonomy we depend on; and its one reliable case (direct/literal support) is already covered by the deterministic presence check. So an independent verifier is squeezed — trivial where it'd be reliable, brittle where it'd add value. The trust guarantee instead rests on the **tier label being honest and visible** (a weakly-supported claim shows as "inferred," never hidden as "direct") — the same honest-tiering the co-pilot relies on. This builds directly on v2, where verification *is* this kind of mandatory structured grounding: v2 even has verify-as-a-*tool* but blocks it, having found unconditional grounding more reliable than letting the agent choose when to verify — vindicating the mandatory-internal-step stance.

### Progressive disclosure — block & artefact summaries ✅ *(new decision)*
Principle 1 made *system machinery* progressive (plan before run, evidence behind claims); this extends the same move to the **analysis content itself** — a user reads at multiple levels of detail, drilling from a high-level summary into full grounded detail rather than meeting a wall of text. Summaries are a **navigation device**, not a new evidential surface: their job is to let a user grasp what a block or artefact holds and decide whether to dive in, with the provenance always one layer down.

- **A summary is a condensed rendering of the detail beneath it, not independently grounded content.** It carries **no citations**; grounding lives in the detail the user drills into. Its integrity property is **faithfulness-to-detail**, not grounding-to-source — and the faithfulness terminus mirrors the grounding terminus (sources) one level up: each summary anchors to **raw full detail**, never to another summary. Summaries are **generated (abstractive) prose**, not extractive selection, and they sit **outside the grounding economy** — they never contribute to evidence-strength roll-ups (*Appraisal*, above), because the underlying claims are already counted via the detail. Citation-free is acceptable here precisely because export/sharing is a v3.0 seam (§10) and a **display invariant** holds: *a summary never renders detached from its drill-down affordance* — so "citation-free" means provenance one layer down, not provenance absent (consistent with §1: the tool's job ends at a well-cited corpus, not at policing the reader's next step). The only surfaces where a summary could appear detached are internal read-models (catch-me-up line, an inventory row, §7/§9); a subtle "AI condensation" marker is reserved for those, not carried as a per-summary badge in the drill-down view.
- **Two grains, flat faithfulness.** Block-level and artefact-level, both generated from and faithfulness-checked against the **raw detail** they cover — never another summary (**no summary-of-summaries**). The rejected RAPTOR-style **citable hierarchical summaries** (§4 *findings layer* rejection) stay rejected and are a *distinct construct*: those were citation targets / retrieval nodes with no evidential terminus; these are display-layer renderings, never citation targets, never retrieval substrate. Display may be a ladder (artefact summary → block summaries → block detail → provenance) but faithfulness is **flat** — each summary anchors independently to raw text.
- **The detail is prose *plus* its epistemic annotations.** A summary faithful to the prose alone can still launder the annotation layer — stating as plain fact something the detail flags **Unsupported / mis-cited** (§3.3), eliding a gap annotation's hedge, or presenting a soft content-scan pattern as established shape. So the faithfulness terminus is the block's prose **and** its claim / gap / pattern / Tier-4 annotations: flagged, Tier-4, gap and soft-pattern content is either **excluded** from the summary or **carried with its status**, never silently promoted into a plain assertion.
- **Faithfulness mechanism = LLM judge alone, bounded regenerate-on-fail.** A verify-against-detail judge (does the summary over-claim, omit a material caveat, assert something the detail doesn't carry, or foreground what the detail doesn't?) with a bounded retry loop. There is **no deterministic leg** — unlike grounding's quote-presence hard-fail, abstractive text has no verbatim anchor (a numeric/entity presence backstop was considered and rejected — see rejections). The verdict is stored as a **verification record keyed to `(block-or-artefact, version)`**, deliberately **outside** the `(block, unit, type)` annotation layer (which is full-text-only, §3.2). Its verdicts are persisted with judge prompt/model versioning like any other judge output (the §8 eval-readiness seam); **whether and how the faithfulness judge is measured is owned by the eval workstream** (§8), not pre-committed here, and its SLIs are debugging/trend-monitoring signals in v3.0, never authoritative quality measures. Exhausted-retry terminus: the summary is marked **`failed`** and surfaced as such — never silently dropped, never silently shipped as if verified (flag-don't-drop). Rubric obligations the architecture fixes (calibration owned by §8):
  - **Fidelity** — no over-claim, no omitted material caveat, no assertion absent from the detail.
  - **Epistemic-annotation awareness** — flagged / Tier-4 / gap / soft-pattern content carried-with-status or excluded (above).
  - **Conclusion-fidelity** (artefact grain) — where the summary states a headline conclusion, it may not assert one the **key-findings block doesn't carry**: not stronger, not different in direction, not stripped of a material caveat.
  - **Emphasis inherited, never originated** — the summary reflects the artefact's *own* emphasis (located structurally, below); **selective emphasis is a defect**, not a feature. Genuine prioritisation — the agent deciding what is load-bearing — is substance and lives in a grounded block, not the summary layer.
- **Block summary — one row, two columns, co-versioned.** A second column on the block record (content + summary), produced as the **trailing step of `produce-grounded-block`** (… → write → summarise → verify-faithfulness). It is a **parallel representation of one version, not a temporal successor** in the version chain — modelling it as its own version would be a category error — so it has **no independent staleness**: being co-versioned, it is always consistent with its block version (§3.5). Mechanics:
  - **Inside the block's commit boundary, but nullable with a generation-status marker** (`pending` / `verified` / `failed`): the block content commits durably at the block-boundary checkpoint (§6), the summary populates within the same logical step, and a crash mid-summarise resumes into a well-defined *summary-pending* state rather than a silent null. Its write rides the block's existing lifecycle event under the §9 transaction invariant — no separate event.
  - **Excluded from the block content hash** (§3.2): summary prose is abstractive and varies stochastically across regenerations of identical block text; including it would break `same_content_as` hash-equality and reintroduce the false-staleness storms §3.6 designed out. Consequence to hold: the summary is **content-bearing but identity-irrelevant** — persisted, disclosable text deliberately not part of block identity.
  - **Human amendment → stale-pending, gated** (§3.2, §7): when the deferred editing UX lands, a human amendment marks the block human-amended with its verification stale; the summary column is likewise marked **stale-pending-regeneration** (regenerating immediately would faithfulness-check agent prose against detail whose own verification is stale; persisting unchanged would break co-version consistency), and regeneration is gated, not automatic.
- **Artefact summary — a facet on the artefact, structurally anchored.** A field on the artefact record (**not a block** — it has no units, so it accrues no claim/citation/comment annotations; Principle 10 keeps it a field), regenerated on material block-composition change (§3.6 threshold), snapshotted into each artefact version and version-pinned to the covered block-version set. Distinctive properties:
  - **The one stale-able summary object.** Unlike the block summary it can go stale, and it rides §3.6 by **flag-and-propose** — staleness flags, regeneration is *proposed* and routed through the check-in by steering mode, **never auto-run**. An abstractive regeneration is not a deterministic projection like the table↔chart toggle (§3.2) and its emphasis can shift, so auto-writing it would be the only canonical content written with no human gate upstream — refused on the §3.6 flag-don't-auto-run rule. The §10 superseded-banner pattern shows the stale state meanwhile.
  - **Emphasis located structurally, not judged.** Following §6's resolve-structurally-not-by-runtime-judgement pattern, the summary anchors on the **conclusion-bearing component the capability skeleton declares** (Options Assessment's recommendation, VfM's verdict, …). Where the skeleton declares none (a horizon scan), it falls back to a **structural-shape condensation** — corpus shape in prose, the §3.3 metadata-grounded-pattern register, not invented findings. This keeps selection out of the summariser: the summary *inherits* the artefact's declared emphasis rather than originating it.
  - **Headline conclusion included, under conclusion-fidelity.** The summary states the artefact's headline so a user can judge worth-opening, kept honest because it is a faithful pointer to the grounded key-findings block, not a free-floating claim. Citation-free is tolerable for the reason above (drill-down + display invariant); the conclusion-fidelity rubric leg is what stops it asserting more than the grounded conclusion carries.
  - **Faithfulness terminus is the raw covered blocks, including the key-findings block** (grounded full detail, not a summary — so still no summary-of-summaries).
  - **Two-flag ripple (recorded):** a leaf-block edit can flag the conclusion block via the coherence pass (§3.6); the conclusion block's regeneration is itself a material composition change, which then flags the artefact summary — two flags from one edit, all flag-and-propose, all existing machinery.
- **The key-findings / conclusion block is grounded content, not a summary.** The artefact's *"what did it conclude"* front door is a **grounded block**, inside the grounding economy — cited, contestable on its full text, versioned, steerable at check-ins. It is the existing recommendation/verdict block **designated as the entry point** where the capability already produces one, or an **authored synthesis block** where it doesn't — present only where the skeleton declares a conclusion-bearing component (the same structural signal the artefact summary anchors on). It grounds to **sources, never to sibling blocks** (§3.3: no block cites a sibling) — a Tier-3 reasoning-over-synthesis claim attributed at **block level** (the §3.3 attribution-granularity machinery), its derivation on the sibling blocks being internal wiring (the staleness graph), not a visible citation. **Produced last** (its inputs are the other blocks), **shown first** (it is the entry point). As a **within-artefact dependent** its staleness flags via the §3.6 coherence pass — flag-and-propose — when an upstream block regenerates. Reuses the *Human-in-the-loop & the verdict* discipline above (rejected alternatives carried as first-class content); not new machinery.
- ⏸ **Summaries may route, never substitute.** As a by-product, persisted summaries are a cheap selection signal for an agent navigating the artefact side — e.g. a cross-artefact meta-synthesis (§3.4) reading summaries to decide *which blocks to pull in full*. The discipline, recorded now so it survives: a summary may **narrow what gets read, never be the thing generated *from*** — nothing load-bearing (grounding, synthesis, citation, extraction, agent context) is produced from a summary; the agent grounds in the full blocks it selects. Under that rule a confident-but-wrong summary is bounded to a recoverable *selection* error (widen scope; the evidence escape-hatch), never laundered into prose (§9's confident-but-wrong-summary warning). v3.0 likely doesn't need it — at v3.0 scale the orienting-context artefact inventory plus `lookup` covers block selection — so it is a **deferred seam**, route-never-substitute recorded.
- **Size** (sentence / few sentences / scaled to source) is **eval/UX tuning, not architecture.**

**Rejected alternatives** *(rationale preserved so it survives future discussion)*:
- **Independently grounded summaries carrying their own citations** — would force picking claims from specific citations rather than a holistic condensation, and recreates the citation-target hazard; faithfulness-to-detail plus drill-down to grounded detail is the cleaner contract.
- **Extractive (selected-text) summaries** — abstractive condensation reads better and compresses harder; extraction is not the job.
- **Ephemeral on-demand summaries** — persisted summaries are needed for consistent navigation and the audit record; an on-demand render is the co-pilot's *generative-synthesis* job (*orchestrator & co-pilot*, below), a different surface.
- **Nested faithfulness** (artefact summary checked against block summaries) — would compound summary error; faithfulness is flat, every summary against raw detail.
- **A deterministic numeric/entity backstop** — unlike grounding's quote-presence check, an abstractive summary has **no pre-emitted anchor to match against**: a backstop would need an extraction-plus-normalisation layer (identify numerals/dates/entities in generated prose, fuzzy-match against detail with rewording tolerance) whose cost and failure surface aren't justified when the faithfulness judge already covers the class. Considered and rejected; the faithfulness check stays a single judge-only mechanism.
- **Block summaries as separate block rows/versions** — a summary is a parallel representation of one version, not a temporal successor; a co-versioned column, not a row. *(The artefact grain was re-opened against this — whether the artefact summary should be a typed block — and resolved as a **facet**: with comments/challenges kept on full text and the verification record outside the annotation layer, it accrues nothing a block would, so Principle 10 keeps it a field.)*
- **Commentable / challengeable summaries; summaries in the annotation layer** — comments and challenges stay on the **full text** (a summary is a navigation device, not a unit of scrutiny); the annotation layer is full-text-only. The single residual — *"this summary misrepresents the detail"* — is served by a lightweight **report-summary → propose-regenerate** action emitting an event (a faithfulness-failure signal and an eval gold-set candidate, §8), not an annotation.

### The orchestrator & co-pilot ✅ *(framing settled; fast-path tuning open)*
The persistent project chat is a single **orchestrator agent** — a unification of three things we'd treated separately (the co-pilot, the planning agent, and orchestration). It owns the conversation and the **orchestration plan**, decides at plan time which capabilities and components meet the user's directive (reading the declared specs), and delegates execution to **capability sub-agents**. It is the agent behind the two-level plan: orchestrator ↔ orchestration plan; capability agents ↔ per-capability plans (§5).
- **Plan-time authority, not runtime.** The orchestrator decides the shape at plan time (human-gated); it does not reshape the run on the fly. Capability agents execute the agreed plan, with the bounded escape hatches as their only runtime discretion — so the audit spine (plan-as-canonical) holds.
- **The co-pilot is the same agent pointed at chat.** Alongside planning/orchestration it answers questions and produces **no artefact** (answers are ephemeral). Two flavours: *provenance lookup* ("where does the £0.32 come from?") reads back grounding already appraised/verified when the block was made — inherently grounded and fast; *generative synthesis* ("show HMT's counter-argument") is a `produce-grounded-block` rendered into the chat rather than persisted.
- **Deliberately faster, but honestly tiered.** To stay conversational the Q&A runs at a lower-latency setting — cites where it cheaply can, may skip the verify pass — so it does *not* carry the full block discipline. The trust story is protected not by slowing it down but by **reusing the §3.3 trust-tier taxonomy**: every answer carries its tier, so a quick reasoned answer is *visibly* "pure LLM reasoning" rather than masquerading as a verified claim. The cardinal sin in an evidence tool — an ungrounded answer indistinguishable from a grounded one — is what the labelling prevents.
- **Promotion re-runs the full bar.** If an answer is worth keeping, promoting it into an artefact block re-runs the full appraise + verify discipline — the ephemeral/loose vs persisted/rigorous seam.
- **Private Q&A runs a reduced, read-only tool scope** ✅ *(panel-review resolution)*. It may read existing project state — artefacts, annotations, extracted evidence, corpus material — but its scope is **the three internal read tools** (`retrieve`, `lookup`, `query-findings`) — **no `search`**: it cannot acquire new external evidence, add documents to the shared corpus, appraise newly acquired material, mutate tags/annotations/artefacts/extracted evidence, or create shared project events. (Shared/private follows write-scope, §7 — and `search` is external egress, §9, which must not originate from a surface outside the audit record.) If an answer needs evidence the project doesn't hold, the co-pilot says so and offers to convert the question into a **shared search request** for the driver/orchestrator.
- 🟡 *Open:* the exact fast-path discipline — skip verify entirely? reuse existing appraisals or re-appraise? what latency budget — and the (lighter) provenance for in-corpus Q&A retrieval (external `search` is now resolved out of Q&A scope, above).

### Appraisal ✅ *(one tool; orthogonal to grounding; rubric kept high-level)*
Don't force one academic hierarchy on everything. **The document type — from the separate `classify` tool — decides which yardstick applies** (rigour ladder for empirical studies; authority for policy documents; context/low-trust for memos); classification (*what kind*) is a distinct operation from appraisal (*how good*). Model appraisal as an **extensible set of typed dimensions + a rubric version**; populate only a couple in v3.0 and add dimensions/rubrics later without a schema change. The **type→tier rubric is not a fixed global hierarchy** but **steerable, default-first** — the orchestrator puts a **provisional default** rubric in the plan it first generates (inferred from domain/intent); the user need not specify or design one, but may **inspect and adjust** it (in plain language, which the orchestrator compiles; or directly, for advanced users — progressive disclosure, §1). The **rubric version travels with each appraisal** for provenance (v2's rigid SR > RCT > observational ladder is exactly what users flagged: domains without RCTs cap out low, so good evidence reads as weak). Appraisal yields a per-source **quality tier** (the single-valued derived column, §3.3) and is re-runnable. **v3.0 is a single light pass** (cheap tier, all screened-in, primarily document-type-based, ≈ v2's evidence-strength stage); ⏸ a fuller **second pass** (richer full-text methods / risk-of-bias gated to the selected subset, plus later modifier-tag dimensions) is deferred — `appraise` being re-runnable, the seam adds it without a new component. ⏸ **Relative-to-feasible seam:** a richer tier normalised against the best evidence *achievable* for the question (cross-document, edging into the deferred roll-up) — recorded, not built in v3.0. **Build on v2's existing pipeline** — Policy Atlas v2 already has a developed evidence-categorisation + evidence-strength-assessment stage concerned with source quality; v3 appraisal carries it forward (de-rigidifying the hierarchy) rather than inventing it.

**Appraisal and the grounding trust tier are two orthogonal axes — kept distinct, never collapsed** ✅:
- **Grounding tier** (§3.3, from `verify`) measures **inference distance** — how far a claim travelled from its source (direct quote → inferred-single-doc → reasoning-over-synthesised → pure-LLM).
- **Appraisal** (from `appraise`) measures **source quality** — how good the underlying evidence is, independent of how faithfully it was reported.

They are independent because a direct quote from a weak source is *faithfully reported but weakly evidenced*, while a careful inference from excellent sources is the reverse — same point on one axis, opposite on the other. So appraisal does **not** alter the grounding tier — the two are distinct axes, not one feeding the other. Both travel with the claim as **separate labels**, and they would combine **only at an aggregate roll-up** (per-claim grounding tier × per-source appraisal → option/artefact-level confidence) — **down-weighted to a v3.0 seam** *(revised this session)*. Evidence-strength roll-ups, and the divided-evidence *direction* verdict the early wireframes showed, are **deferred, not a committed v3.0 build**: how to weigh a thin or divided evidence base hasn't been worked through, and a premature formula would mislead more than it informs. One constraint is canonical ahead of any such roll-up ✅: **Unsupported / mis-cited claims and Tier-4 statements never contribute positively to evidence-strength aggregates** (§3.3), and weakly-grounded claims contribute only at a discount — so whenever the roll-up is built it inherits these rather than rediscovering them. Keeping the axes separate preserves the remedy signal: a low score on grounding says *get corroboration / regenerate from better evidence*; a low score on appraisal says *get better sources* — collapsing to one scalar would lose which.

### The evidence/analysis decomposition & the extraction model ✅ *(v2 monolith decomposed; horizon-scan theming 🟡)*
This elaborates "evidence is both a capability and a shared layer" (above) and decomposes v2's monolithic synthesis pipeline.

- **Evidence vs analysis — the dividing line.** Evidence operations are **shared, state-producing** (search, retrieve, extract, appraise, `cluster`, `screen`, `classify`, `select`), whose *typed outputs* are reusable substrate; **analysis capabilities** apply a **capability-specific computation or synthesis** over that substrate to produce an artefact. The line is *not* question-independence — the *invocation* of an evidence operation is question-driven (see extraction below) — it is **shared operation whose output is reusable (tool) vs artefact-producing composition (capability)**. Impact and Transferability are question-relative ("benefit for *your* target," "fit to *your* context") so they are analysis; effect-size extraction or appraisal is the same operation whoever asks.
- **Evidence Base = a flexible capability + a shared layer.** Its *operations* are tools (search / retrieve / extract / appraise / `cluster` / `screen` / `classify` / `select`); its *components* are skeleton steps — now specced in the EB capability design (acquire → screen → classify → appraise → characterise → select → extract → group → synthesise). The three tools added by that design: **`screen`** (per-document relevance fan-out; v3.0 screens on **metadata** — title+abstract, **degrading to title-only** where no abstract — **fail-open** so a missing abstract never drops a doc; → `is_relevant` + relevance `confidence` + a **`screen_basis`** flag (`title_abstract` | `title_only`) and a retryable **`screen_failed`** state distinct from not-relevant; may re-invoke `search` on the evidence-thin escape hatch. ⏸ The richer **tiered content peek** — exec-summary/headings/passage scan for poor-metadata grey lit — is deferred), **`classify`** (per-document classification → a single-valued **`primary_evidence_type`** closed column + proposed open **methodological/structural tags**; distinct from `appraise`), **`select`** (strategy-parameterised selection-scan over a candidate set on cheap signals → chosen subset + selection rationale **capturing both selected and, in aggregate, not-selected**; source/evidence policy a soft prior, never a gate; rationale uniform because selection shapes coverage/emphasis, §6) — all registry-scoped, declaration-scoped, not universal core. The v2 always-full-extraction-of-the-whole-screened-set monolith is **rejected**; breadth and depth are intent-derived, the profile compiling from the plan (§5) rather than fixed.
- **Impact, Transferability, Options Assessment, Theory of Change are analysis capabilities** (Appendix A), each with its own components. Impact and Transferability — v2's per-intervention enriching computations — **leave the pipeline** and become these capabilities; their structured inputs (effect sizes; context/population) are extracted on demand (below).
- **Structured extraction vs RAG — not substitutes.** RAG is *retrieval* (surface relevant passages); structured extraction is *structuring* (passages → typed fields). Deterministic aggregation/comparison (an impact roll-up, a transferability context-match) needs the fields — you cannot deterministically average effect sizes over prose, and the structuring is what keeps the computation reproducible, auditable, and its fragility *visible* (v2's evidence-strength scoring is an explicit structured pipeline — base scores + caps over extracted fields — rather than a single holistic LLM-as-judge score, the form this argues for). RAG can **feed** extraction (retrieve the results section, extract from it) more cheaply than loading the whole document, but does not **replace** it for the quantitative cases. For **narrative/qualitative** synthesis it is overkill — RAG + `produce-grounded-block` (cite the span) is the whole job. So *whether a capability needs structured extraction at all is plan-derived.* v3.0 seam: extract fields now → present a table the human analyses → layer the deterministic scoring on the *same* fields later (pure-RAG would forfeit that seam).
- **Two tiers — only the cheap one is front-loaded.**
  - **Tier 0 — front-loaded, cheap.** `search` yields metadata (title/abstract); the **relevance screen** runs on that (the `screen` tool — its own step, able to loop back to *search* if the screened base is too thin, the evidence escape-hatch applied early), as do **cheap `classify` + `appraise`** (document type + quality tier, from title/abstract — §3.3). Then, **for the screened-in set only**, full text is fetched and ingested per source: **snapshot** (the immutable ingestion record — frozen chunks + content hash + metadata, **no original bytes retained**; §3.3 and *Source snapshots & evidence segmentation* below), **parse** into best-effort source structure, **segment** into retrieval passages under the current segmentation policy, and **embed**. When full text can't be fetched (paywall, dead link), the source is **not** dropped — it is snapshotted and ingested on the text in hand (abstract + metadata), each source carrying a **`text_basis`** (`full_text` | `abstract_only`) so grounding and coverage see which a finding rests on. Vectorisation is **eager and uniform** over the whole screened-in set — **lazy / on-demand vectorisation is rejected**, since embedding only a subset biases retrieval toward what was vectorised early, making the long tail invisible to dense search for reasons of ingestion order, not relevance. Gating full-text ingestion behind screen means non-relevant documents never incur acquisition / parsing / embedding cost (the v2 efficiency); the screened-in corpus (text + vectors) is the shared substrate downstream capabilities, retrieval and Q&A reuse. This is *not* structured extraction; it is the substrate the selection scans read, *and* the inspectability floor (every run can show the shape of what was found and what it didn't touch). It runs on the screened-in set because the selection scans need it anyway.
  - **Tier-0 retrieval contract** ✅ *(firms up the earlier 🟡 — the implementation contract for `retrieve`, named because Tier 0 is load-bearing: selection-scan-first makes Tier-0 **false negatives** the dangerous failure mode — a poor retrieval layer doesn't merely weaken an answer, it can keep the right documents from ever reaching Tier-1 extraction, grounding, appraisal or synthesis)*. v3.0 retrieval is **hybrid lexical + dense** over the project corpus — Postgres FTS + pgvector on the existing Aurora (confirmed feasible with DevOps) — with **application-side rank fusion** (e.g. RRF), never assumed from the database. **Cross-encoder reranking sits behind the retrieval-profile seam** (the Bedrock Rerank API would keep it inside the inference trust boundary, §11), used where precision materially affects grounding or artefact production — **never always-on magic**: selection scans must preserve recall, coverage and source diversity, because in this architecture a missed awkward source is worse than a slightly noisy shortlist. The tool returns a **traceable candidate set**, not just final snippets — query text and variants, scope filters, lexical and dense candidate sets, fused ranking, selected passages, near-misses and coverage metadata — captured on the **telemetry plane** by default (§8, *Retrieval observability*); the **gap-coverage profile additionally writes canonical attempted-search provenance** (§3.3). Results carry source-anchored locations and can expand into **evidence envelopes** (below). The canonical corpus stays in the information layer; **indexes are derived and rebuildable** behind the adapter seam (§3.1); a separate retrieval service is ⏸ deferred unless the Aurora-native path fails on measured quality, latency, filtering, scale or operability.
  - **Retrieval profiles** ✅. Retrieval behaviour varies by use case behind one tool interface — implementation profiles of `retrieve`, not separate operations (same I/O shape, different intensity: gradations by the §4 I/O test). **Selection scan** — recall- and diversity-oriented, because it decides what the system inspects more deeply. **Grounding / citation support** — precision-oriented; may cross-encode over the fused candidate pool (the grounding-retry profile). **Gap-coverage search** — records coverage and failed queries as canonical attempted-search provenance (§3.3). **Q&A lookup** — lower-latency, within the private Q&A scope restrictions. Large corpus-shape / horizon-scan retrieval avoids collapsing early to a narrow cross-encoder top-k.
  - **Tier 1 — capability-driven, scoped, deferred.** Deep structured extraction — a capability's own specs over its own selected subset — triggered by that capability's selection. **No mandatory front-loaded deep-extraction stage:** the "broad" horizon-scan case is simply the Evidence Base capability running broad *as its job*; opt-in pre-warm is a scheduling policy, not a stage.
- **Source snapshots & evidence segmentation** ✅ *(named decision — segmentation is trust-relevant, not a hidden retrieval detail: passages are upstream of what `produce-grounded-block` sees, what supporting quotes are available, what the quote-presence check can verify, and what the grounding judge reads — a naive fixed window could sever intervention from population, comparator, outcome, caveat or context)*.
  - **Immutable source snapshots.** Every ingested source gets a `source_snapshot_id`, `origin`, acquiring backend where applicable, acquisition timestamp, source locator where available, content hash, parse profile and parse-quality metadata — the immutable record of the source as Policy Atlas ingested it. **No original bytes are retained**: the raw file is fetched transiently to parse, and the **frozen parsed chunks are the content-of-record**; identity rests on the **content hash + the §9 search event + the source locator** (§3.3; the supersedes edge and the staleness entry point live there and in §3.6).
  - **Structure-aware first, semantic second.** Ingestion parses each snapshot into the best structure available — pages, headings, sections, paragraphs, bullet groups, tables, captions, footnotes, annexes — falling back gracefully to page/paragraph-like spans with a parse-quality flag for poor PDFs and weakly structured uploads. Retrieval passages are derived from that structure by a named, versioned **segmentation policy** (structure-, table- and section-aware; token limits, overlap, and semantic merge/split rules where useful). Purely semantic splitting is a layer *over* the structural parse, never an opaque model-dependent replacement for it — fixed-window and purely semantic chunkers both mangle tables, and tables are where effect sizes live.
  - **One parse, one segmentation, per snapshot** ✅ *(recorded scope decision)*. Chunking and embedding happen **once at ingestion**; the system does not design for re-chunking, re-embedding or re-parsing within a project's life, so passage IDs are stable for the only lifetime that matters. Recorded consequence: a segmentation-policy or parser improvement applies to **newly ingested snapshots only** — each snapshot records the policy/parse version it was processed under, a corpus may legitimately contain documents segmented under different policy versions, and "upgrading" an existing document means re-ingesting it as a new snapshot — and since **no original bytes are retained** (§3.3), re-parsing in place is not possible by construction: a parser upgrade necessarily re-ingests (re-fetches), the intended behaviour, not a limitation *(re-parse-without-refetch explicitly judged unnecessary, this session)*. *(Revisited only with the cross-project Library, where re-embedding into a shared index becomes plausible — deferred with it.)*
  - **The provenance anchor is (source, quote, recorded location)** ✅. The load-bearing anchor is the pair **(source, verbatim quote)** — the source identified by its `source_snapshot_id`, the quote verified against that source's **frozen chunks** by the deterministic presence check, self-relocating by string match (v2's working pattern: quotes pulled from chunks). The **location is the recorded by-product of the verify step**: when the presence check locates the quote, it persists where it found it (passage ID + offset) — costing nothing, and buying precise highlighting for citation pills and export deep links, plus disambiguation when the same wording recurs in a document (boilerplate, repeated table notes — real in grey literature). Passage/chunk IDs are legitimate operational context throughout; "span" is **not** a designed coordinate system.
  - **Evidence envelopes.** Grounding and extraction never rely on a naked top-k chunk: `retrieve` can expand a focal passage into an **evidence envelope** — focal passage, section breadcrumb, adjacent passages where needed, parent-section context, table headers/notes/captions, source metadata, parse-quality signals — which is what the verifier or extraction worker reads. This generalises the envelope the Tier-1 document-scoped workers already assemble (below): **one definition, two consumers**. It reduces the risk that an intervention, outcome, comparator or caveat is severed at a passage boundary — exactly the severance that would quietly tax the trust machinery.
- **Capability-driven document selection — scan first, extract second.** A capability opens with a **selection scan** riding on **cheap signals only** (the Tier-0 columns + embeddings/RAG + screening) plus **dependency-derived scoping** from the plan/DAG (§3.6): Transferability runs after Options and scopes to the *selected* options' supporting documents; Baseline scopes to the contextual/problem-scoping documents — a different set entirely. *We never deep-extract in order to decide what to deep-extract.*
- **Extraction scheduling — a queryable extracted-findings layer, not an opaque cache** ✅ *(promotes the panel-review extracted-evidence store; replaces `(document, spec)` memoisation)*. Structured extraction writes **reusable extracted evidence into the information layer**, not merely tool-cache payloads — a tool cache answers "have we already run this task?"; the findings layer answers "**what structured evidence has the project extracted, and how can capabilities query it?**" A capability/component **commit** declares an **extraction requirement** (the capability's **extraction profile**) over its selected source set; the extraction service resolves the requirement against existing **finding records, dimension values, extracted values and coverage records**, and creates per-source **extraction tasks** only for the missing coherent record bundles. Exact per-source task results are memoised by **`(source snapshot, extraction-task fingerprint)`**, but **reuse happens through the findings layer, never through private cache payloads**. Capabilities consume findings through pinned **evidence dataset snapshots** — the records, values, dimension matches and coverage records actually handed to a capability at that point in time, preserving point-in-time provenance for artefact versions and reruns (and the replay-pinning pattern §8's harness generalises). Each **extraction attempt** records its **execution profile** — model id/version, prompt version, tool/parser/schema/rubric version, trace refs — for audit. **Model/prompt upgrades set future execution defaults; they never invalidate existing extracted findings or historical project state**: projects are point-in-time records, old results are never represented as products of a newer regime, and an explicit rerun adopts the new profile (§8). Per-source extraction runs in its own fan-out branch (v2's pattern); amortisation is **intra-capability** (one read of a source covers that capability's whole requirement), and full overlap with an earlier capability is a pure layer hit. *(Provider-side prompt caching remains an unreliable backstop under HITL pauses, so batching-within-the-pass carries the amortisation.)*
  - ⏸ **Forecast/prewarm — optional, and only modelled if built** *(Principle 10)*. The orchestration plan *may* carry provisional **extraction forecasts** for likely downstream needs, used solely for opt-in prewarming of high-confidence, low-regret record bundles. Forecasts are **non-execution-bearing**; the just-in-time commit remains the source of truth, with the extraction service reusing compatible records and delta-extracting only what the committed requirement adds. If prewarm does not ship in v3.0, the forecast object is not modelled — no inert entities.
  - **Source-scoped extraction workers** ✅. Tier-1 extraction runs through workers scoped to **one source snapshot**, with the **full retrieval-passage set, the parsed source structure, a best-effort document sketch and the current segmentation-policy metadata** in scope: the worker uses retrieval, sketch signals and passage-level scanning to assemble a high-recall evidence envelope (the same envelope definition as grounding — *Source snapshots & evidence segmentation*, above), with fallback to broader passage coverage or full-source extraction where the requirement is exhaustive, relational, table-heavy, low-confidence or completeness-sensitive. The whole document is *in scope* for the worker without the whole document entering the LLM every time — avoiding both naive top-k retrieval as a recall bottleneck and v2-style always-full-text extraction. Workers record coverage, validation status, and the execution profile used for each attempt.
- **Queryable extracted-findings layer — v3.0 scope** ✅. Vocabulary, kept deliberately un-taxonomic: a **finding record** is an actual extracted record from a source; a **finding schema** is the shape of that record; a **reusable finding schema** is a schema more than one capability can query safely; an **extraction profile** is the bundle of extraction requirements a capability commits over a selected source set. The useful insight (translated out of the GraphRAG/hypergraph framing it arrived in): **where a finding is multidimensional, preserve it as one coherent typed record with its dimensions intact and queryable** — an effect finding holds the relationship between intervention, population, comparator, outcome, context, direction, magnitude, uncertainty and source grounding as one record, never flattened into disconnected fields or prose fragments.
  - **First reusable finding schema: `intervention_outcome_finding`** ✅ — driven by the Evidence Base / horizon-scan capability (the v3.0 build order), leaving a clean seam for Options Assessment, Impact, Transferability and VfM. It preserves the relationship between an intervention/approach and an **observed, reported or missing outcome**, with effect-specific fields (direction, magnitude, uncertainty, timeframe) where present — effect evidence is a structured subset of the record, so the schema also covers horizon-scan cases where evidence reports an outcome without a formal estimate, or reports outcome evidence as weak, mixed or absent. Each record pins to its source snapshot and located quote/envelope, with appraisal labels, coverage state and extraction profile. **Grain:** a finding is one *(intervention, outcome, effect)* claim grounded in a **single source**; `intervention`, `outcome` and **study population** are **source-named references** (groupable/canonicalisable downstream, not baked-in canonical entities) — lifting v2's flat result-row-linked-by-`intervention_idx` grain to a first-class record. **Field-set boundary (source-groundability):** the base finding holds what the source reports — effect direction, effect size + type, uncertainty (CI/SE), p-value, study-design/sample metadata (design, N, k, I²), the **study population** (source-named, enabling deep group-by-population), the descriptive causality-by-design label, and primacy/prevalence flags; **question-relative judgements (normalised magnitude, causal *weighting*, is-beneficial) are analysis enrichment** layered by Impact/VfM, not base fields (v2 conflated both in one `ResultItem`), keeping the finding reusable rather than pre-committed to one analysis. ⏸ Additional reusable schemas are deferred until another capability demonstrably queries them — the first named candidate being **`implementation_context_finding`** (a *different evidence kind*: mechanisms, barriers, implementation conditions, delivery / process findings — the "how / why / under what conditions", not "what effect"). It is to carry the **same source-named reference vocabulary** (intervention / outcome / population / setting), so **cross-schema linkage is reference-mediated**: findings of different kinds naming the same intervention are joined by the existing `group` reference-clustering (no explicit cross-schema link objects), letting synthesis span effect + context evidence while each finding stays single-source-grounded and the two evidence kinds stay related-but-distinct.
  - **The boundary sentence** ✅: a reusable finding schema is a **schema for reuse when extraction happens**. It is **not a mandatory extraction target, not an ingestion-time obligation, and not a global ontology**. Selection-scan-first is unchanged: v3.0 never deep-extracts in order to decide what to deep-extract; a capability first selects a source subset on cheap signals, retrieval and dependencies, then commits extraction only for the records it needs. *(Recorded rejection, from the SOTA-RAG assessment, so the rationale survives the next GraphRAG paper: **ingestion-time global knowledge graphs/hypergraphs** re-create the v2 front-loaded monolith and violate selection-scan-first — the ontology insight survives only as per-capability extraction profiles plus this findings layer; **citable hierarchical summaries** (RAPTOR-style nodes as citation targets) break trace-to-source — a claim citing a summary-of-summaries has no evidential terminus, and corpus shape is already served honestly by metadata-grounded patterns + `cluster`. Hierarchical retrieval *routing* may return as a Library-scale consideration — deferred with it. The §4 *Progressive disclosure* block/artefact summaries are a **distinct construct** — display-layer renderings, never citation targets or retrieval substrate (route-never-substitute), with faithfulness terminating at raw detail.)*
  - **Coverage states are gap provenance** ✅. Coverage records distinguish **`searched_and_absent`**, **`not_applicable`**, **`not_selected`**, **`not_extracted`**, **`unclear`** and **`extraction_failed`** (`not_selected` — doc-level: screened-in and relevant but not chosen for deep extraction, the gap the selection scan creates — distinct from field-level `not_extracted`). This is part of §3.3's gap provenance: "no effects reported for rural settings" is a corpus/coverage gap **only where the relevant source set was actually searched or extracted for that field** — `not_extracted` and `not_selected` are never evidence of absence. The adjoining distinction, held crisply: a source that **reports** a null or non-significant effect asserts a **finding** (the source says it — evidence, extractable, citable, never represented as a gap); a source that simply **doesn't mention** the outcome is a **coverage** fact. Conflating them runs in the dangerous direction — treating reported absence-of-effect as absence-of-evidence understates what the corpus actually says.
  - **Every gap claim carries its coverage base** ✅ *(EB-prompted, framework-level)*. A gap / absence claim is meaningless without the population it rests on, so the base is a **required structural field**, not prose, drawn from the pipeline ladder: **attempted-search → acquired → screened → selected → extracted**. Each rung narrows, and each narrowing is a place absence can be manufactured. **Only `searched_and_absent` over an adequately-searched base licenses an absence claim;** `not_selected`, `not_extracted`, `extraction_failed` and `unclear` never do. Even `searched_and_absent` is **bounded by acquisition scope** (§5 — configured backends / trust classes): absence is always "absent from the *searched* space", never absolute, and a corpus-level absence claim carries a search-adequacy caveat. A capability's **shallow** coverage rests on the **screened** base (the whole relevant corpus); its **deep** coverage rests on the **selected / extracted** base (a subset) and must be base-labelled, **never promoted to corpus absence** — the shallow landscape is the structural check on a deep absence.
  - **The `search_coverage_record` operationalises "adequately-searched"** ✅ *(EB-prompted, framework-level)*. So the absence licence is structured, not prose: a corpus-level absence claim carries — or references — a record with the **search-space boundary** (backends / trust classes), the **stop condition** (v3.0: *breadth-truncated* / *re-searched-still-thin* / *error*; *saturated* is **not** a v3.0 value — saturation-based stopping, iterating retrieval↔screen until no new relevant docs surface, is a ⏸ seam), and an **adequacy verdict + origin** (model or human — the field the fail-closed rule reads); plus, by reference, the **queries + expansions** run (→ the `search` governance events, §9) and the **scope filters** applied (date / geography / language and a **citation / recency floor**, → the acquisition constraint). **Fail closed:** absent a non-`inadequate` record, an absence degrades to "not found in extracted / selected material", never corpus-level. *(Instance detail in the EB design.)*
  - **Graph-structured synthesis — a live seam, distinct from the rejected global KG** ⏸. The rejection above is specifically of an *ingestion-time, global, canonical* knowledge graph; it does **not** rule out exploiting the findings layer's graph *shape* at **query time**. `intervention_outcome_finding` (and the `implementation_context_finding` seam) are typed, qualified **edges**; source-named references are proto-**nodes**; `group`'s reference-clustering is **entity resolution**; cross-schema linkage is **multi-relational traversal**. Query-time graph-structured synthesis — multi-hop cross-finding patterns ("interventions A/B/C all share barrier W"), community detection over the *findings* graph (truer than abstract-text clustering), and structural contradiction-location — is the **candidate mechanism for the deferred divided-evidence / counter-evidence / weighted-strength roll-up** items, so it stays a live seam rather than a closed door. **Escalation ladder, all within a project (never global / cross-project, per the project-private overlay above):** (1) **run-local view** over the project findings layer — ephemeral, recomputed per run, no new infra, *already project-scoped in data* — the cheapest rung and the first to try; (2) **project-scoped persistent graph** — materialised + maintained across runs (Aurora-able; *persistence ≠ a graph datastore*), buying amortised recompute + **stable cross-run entity identity**, only if recompute cost or cross-run consistency demands it; (3) **graph datastore** — only if (2) plus heavy multi-hop traversal needs graph-DB performance. **Precondition on every rung:** an explicit **entity-resolution-quality bar** and sufficient extraction density — under-resolved traversal manufactures *confident false patterns* (a mis-merge inventing a shared barrier), a worse failure than under-synthesis for a grounded government tool; enter evidence-first. **Lens, not machinery:** borrow the KG *concepts* (reified qualified edges, lazy mention→entity resolution, provenance-per-edge, new schemas as new edge types over a shared node vocabulary) and refuse the *machinery* (no eager / global resolution, no ontology / OWL reasoning, no triple-shattering of findings, a datastore only at rung 3).
  - **Hybrid-queryable finding dimensions** ✅/🟡. v3.0 hybrid-indexes the **`intervention`** and **`outcome`** dimensions of `intervention_outcome_finding` (✅ — part of the v3.0 commitment, not a deferred promotion: the v2 lesson is that differently-worded interventions and outcomes defeat structured extraction unless the dimensions themselves are searchable, and v2 paid for it with a mandatory global theming stage). Dimension search **reuses the `retrieve` hybrid adapter with a second index target** — source passages and finding dimension values share the retrieval primitives (lexical + vector, application-side fusion, filters, reranking) with **target-specific configuration** (embedding text, lexical fields, fusion weights, hydration). A dimension value embeds with a compact **semantic envelope** (raw value, source context, linked intervention/outcome, population, setting, source hints) — a bare label like "community connectors" is too thin. A dimension hit **hydrates back to the parent finding record**, linked dimensions, source location/envelope, appraisal labels, coverage state and extraction profile, and combines with structured filters over schema, source metadata, appraisal tier, date, geography, population and coverage state. 🟡 Other extracted fields are stored, and filterable where closed or reliable, but **not hybrid-indexed by default** — the rule: *store extracted fields; make them filterable where reliable; hybrid-index a dimension only when there is evidence that passage RAG plus structured filters is not enough.*
  - **The dimension-promotion gate** 🟡. A field becomes a hybrid-indexed dimension only when the checks show it is worth the ingestion, storage and retrieval complexity: a committed capability repeatedly needs it for retrieval, comparison or aggregation; passage RAG over-returns prose, misses semantically similar values, or requires expensive LLM filtering; the query targets the specific dimension rather than the source as a whole; equivalent concepts are worded many different ways, defeating exact/fuzzy matching; the output needs counts, roll-ups or comparisons over semantically similar values; absence claims over the field would be useful (so coverage records matter); extraction accuracy is high enough that indexing won't amplify noise; and expected query volume or artefact value justifies the embedding, storage and reindexing cost. Promotion is **observed behaviour, not schema enthusiasm** — fed by the query instrumentation (§8, *Retrieval observability*).
  - **`query-findings`** *(registry-scoped, not universal core — §4)*: reads the findings layer; accepts finding schema, dimension query, structured filters, required coverage state, ranking preferences and result limit; returns matching finding records with dimension-match explanation, source references, appraisal labels, coverage state and dataset-snapshot eligibility. `cluster` may take finding records or dimension values as inputs, not just raw concepts or document passages.
- **Thematic analysis — scoped grouping, not the default access path** ✅ *(role sharpened by the findings layer)*. v2 ran fixed theme discovery across issues/interventions/outcomes/risks to make extracted concepts aggregable — understandable in the monolith, but it must not become v3's default route to extracted evidence. The **default access path is hybrid querying over finding records and indexed dimensions** (`query-findings`, above): "what effects exist for interventions like X?" queries the `intervention` dimension semantically across differently-worded sources, and runs a grouping step only if the output actually needs stable categories. `cluster(concepts, dimensions)` remains a **shared tool** for general/lightweight grouping (already less restrictive than v2's fixed four dimensions; it may take finding records or dimension values as input). Deeper thematic analysis **carries method** — dimension choice, concept prep, and grounding the result as a §3.3 *pattern* (its scan provenance, the metadata-vs-content-scan tier, and the ❓ extracted-findings middle position) — so it stays a **component, per-capability**, owned by the capability whose expertise the theming needs and run over a **selected subset** of findings or dimension values when the artefact needs stable groups, counts or narrative patterns: Options Assessment owns intervention-theming, an outcome/impact capability owns outcome-theming, and so on. Run by that capability's expert agent; invoked cross-capability via that agent when another wants it (the rule above).
- ✅ **Consequence — theming distributes *out* of the Evidence Base** *(resolved — EB components now specced)*. EB's deliverable shape is assembled by depth: at the **shallow landscape**, metadata-grounded patterns (deterministic coverage over the columns, §3.3) **+ topic-level `cluster`** over abstracts (lightly labelled); **deeper**, intent-derived **facet grouping** over extracted findings (EB's `group` component — `cluster` over finding records/dimension values + `query-findings`), the facet not fixed (departs from v2's fixed four). Specialised theming still arrives via the relevant analysis capability when it runs.

## 5. The plan as an object

### Plan vs config ✅
- The **plan** is the human-readable, method-level, editable layer and the **canonical** object — it's what the user approves and what goes in the decision log. The **config** is the machine-level execution spec (tools, components, parameters), **compiled from** the plan.
- **Robust compile by construction, not by a smarter translator.** The plan is a **structured selection over the capability's declared spec** (which components run, at what depth, on which inputs, with which steering), so compilation is a deterministic mapping (section → component → declared tools; depth → parameters; named input → source), and the target is valid by construction because the plan can only reference what the capability declares. Supporting properties: execution-bearing fields compile while free-text annotation (framing, assumptions) stays as non-executing context; the compiled config is validated against the spec (a config that doesn't validate is a caught error, never a silent run); and because the mapping is deterministic the config round-trips to the approved plan, so "approved plan" and "executed config" are provably the same — the point of plan-as-canonical for audit. Genuinely fuzzy edits are surfaced for confirmation, not compiled silently.
- **Forecast vs commit — what actually compiles.** With the two-level plan (below), the orchestrator's up-front plan is a **forecast** and each capability agent's just-in-time selection is the **commit**. The *commit* is the execution-bearing layer — it *is* the structured selection over the declared spec, so it's what compiles, by construction, into the capability's executable config. The per-capability **forecast does not compile** — it's the orchestrator's provisional sketch that drives the preview and time estimate, the same non-executing category as free-text annotation.
- **Two tiers of config, staged compilation.** The orchestration spine's *sequence and wiring* (which capabilities, order, data-flow, steering) compile up front into the run-level control. Each *commit* compiles into its capability's config **just-in-time** — forced, not merely allowed, by progressive planning (a downstream capability's inputs, and so its real selection, don't exist up front). "Executed == approved" therefore holds at two tiers.
- **Audit posture across the modes.** Approval lands at the **forecast/shape** level up front in every mode; per-commit approval happens in Frequent/Moderate. Every commit is recorded in the decision log (so nothing executes invisibly), and a commit that diverges from its forecast in a way that shapes the conclusion (**substance**) escalates even in Minimal. So Minimal's guarantee is approved-shape + recorded-commits + substance-escalation; higher modes add per-commit gating.
- **Plan-field ↔ chat-turn provenance.** Each compiled plan field **back-references the conversation turn(s) that produced it**: prose answers in the planning conversation are *interpreted* into structured fields at plan-build time (the raw prose retained behind the field), and those structured fields then compile deterministically at run time. So the plan carries its own "why" into the decision log without coupling compilation to free text.

### What a plan contains 🟡 *(candidate)*
- **Frame** — a **thin narrative intent statement**: the question/intent and how the agent understood the user's context. It is a **living field, not frozen at the initial planning conversation** — the orchestrator **revises it when a material plan edit changes scope** (e.g. an incremental user who began with step one and later added Options + VfM), re-deriving it from the structured inputs (current capability set, assumptions, the triggering steer) rather than re-summarising chat, and it versions with the plan like any field. It backstops nothing on its own: the *authoritative* statement of "what this project is doing" is the structural reading (current capability set + assumptions + statuses), and the narrative is the readable gloss over it — so a lagging narrative never silently misleads. Consumed by the §9 orienting context (the project frame), where it is especially useful for a user-facing "what is this project" line and for orienting **collaborators added mid-project**.
- **Inputs & dependencies** — sources, uploads, and prior artefacts it draws on (e.g. VfM ← selected option + theory of change + baseline), and whether it'll fetch new evidence. Also half the audit record. *(Also carries the capability's **extraction specs** and its **selected source subset** — the per-capability forecast that drives selection-then-extraction, §4; the forecast is per-capability, not a cross-capability union.)*
- **Output structure** — the sections/blocks it'll produce.
- **Approach** — how each section is done, described as *method, not tools*.
- **Depth per section** — the thoroughness gradation and the compile target (drives the time estimate). **Orchestrator-inferred and surfaced as concrete scope, not a user-entered field**; the user adjusts it via the proposal itself or the relative *lighter / deeper* nudge (*Thoroughness as a relative nudge*, below).
- **Steering & check-ins** — mode (Minimal/Moderate/Frequent) and expected pauses, including any mandatory gates.
- **Assumptions & boundaries** — what it's assuming and deliberately not doing, surfaced so the user can correct cheaply before the run.
- **Source / evidence policy** — the evidentiary standard the analysis should rest on (e.g. *official statistics and peer-reviewed evaluations only*): the trust classes that may be searched (acquisition) and the appraisal-tier bar for citable grounding support (use), the latter enforced as **flag-not-block** (*Source / evidence policy*, below; §4 grounding mechanism).

### Thoroughness as a relative nudge, not an absolute level ✅ *(UX resolution, this session)*
Depth/thoroughness stays a per-section **gradation** in the plan object — the deterministic compile target (above; §4 I/O test) — but it is **never a user-entered field, and no absolute thoroughness level is surfaced to the user**, because an abstract label ("standard" vs "thorough") has no referent a user can calibrate. The model is unchanged; what changes is how depth is set and shown.
- **The orchestrator infers depth from the scale of the task** — the breadth of the intent and of the forecast orchestration plan (how many capabilities, corpus size) — and sets the per-section depths as part of the forecast. Urgency is **not** elicited as a separate question.
- **The anchor is the concrete proposal plus a cost signal, not a label.** Per *system proposes, user disposes*, the orchestrator surfaces the inferred depth as **concrete plan scope** — e.g. "Options Assessment: compare 3 options, screen ~50 sources, light per-source extraction, recommendation block" — alongside a rough time/effort estimate. The user reasons about *what it will do and how long it takes*, never about an abstract level. *(The cost half leans on at least a coarse estimate; the estimate model is ⏸ deferred for v3.0, but a band — minutes vs an hour-plus — suffices, and the scope description anchors even without a precise figure.)*
- **Two adjustment paths, both anchored to that proposal.** (1) **Edit the proposal / converse** — "compare 5," "go deeper on the evidence," adjust a section's scope (the existing plan-edit path). (2) A single **relative nudge — *lighter / as proposed / deeper*** — that re-derives the whole plan's per-section depths up or down in one move, *relative to the orchestrator's proposal*. The nudge is anchorable precisely because it is relative to a concrete, visible baseline, not an absolute scale the user must define.
- **The granularity question dissolves.** There is no user-facing per-capability or per-section rung; per-section depth stays in the plan object (orchestrator-set, tweakable through the proposal), and the only coarse user control is the relative nudge, which acts on the whole plan. Named bundles ("modes", §4) survive only as an orchestrator-internal / authoring convenience for presetting depth combinations, never as a user-facing absolute dial.
- **Rejected:** an absolute discrete thoroughness ladder surfaced to the user (unanchorable — the labels have no calibratable referent) and user-entered per-section depth (too much friction). Both are superseded by inference + concrete proposal + relative nudge.

### Source / evidence policy ✅ *(new, this session)*
A plan-level declared constraint — a bounded selection over the declared spec, so it compiles like depth (above) — expressing the **evidentiary standard** the analysis should rest on (e.g. *"official statistics and peer-reviewed evaluations only"*). It has **two faces, only one of them new**:
- **Acquisition face — already covered.** Which external backends / **trust classes** may be searched is the existing `search` machinery (§4): trust class is a property of the configured backend, and the open-web backend is declaration-scoped behind its seam. The policy here merely *bundles* an existing capability, it adds no mechanism.
- **Use face — the new bit: a grounding standard, not a retrieval boundary** ✅. The agent may still **retrieve and read** any in-corpus source — *agents are never penned in* (§3.1), and blinding the agent to a document it already holds would forfeit useful leads — but the policy sets the **appraisal tier** (source *quality*, §3.3 — deliberately *not* the grounding/inference tier) a source must meet to be cited as **grounding support**. Enforced at the boundary `produce-grounded-block` already owns: the cited source's appraisal label (§3.3) is checked against the policy as part of verify.
- **Flag, not block** ✅ *(resolved this session)*. When a claim can only be grounded on a **below-policy** source, the source is **not withheld and the claim is not dropped**: the claim is produced and the citation **marked as resting on below-policy evidence** (a soft typed flag in the annotation layer, surfaced like the Unsupported / mis-cited flag, §4), so the user *sees* that useful lower-tier information exists, knows it falls below the standard they asked for, and decides what to do with it. This follows the project-wide **flag-don't-drop** disposition: the standard is a preference to surface against, not a gag. *Rejected:* a **hard block** (hides useful evidence, manufactures spurious gaps, and overrides the user's own judgement — against flag-don't-drop); and a **retrieval boundary** (pens the agent in, against §3.1, and is redundant with appraisal-column scoping where a soft prior is all that is wanted).
- **Substance-relevant**, so it lives in the plan and the audit record: the evidentiary standard shapes what the conclusion rests on, and a reader must be able to see the bar that was set.
- 🟡 **Open extension — a quality/recency-aware ranking prior.** The same policy could also bias *retrieval ranking* (a quality-weighted, optionally recency-weighted profile behind §4's rerank seam) so higher-appraisal sources surface first, not only gate citation. **Deliberately not built yet:** the **novelty-vs-established-evidence** balance is genuinely use-case-dependent — a horizon scan privileges novel approaches, a VfM privileges well-evidenced findings — so a baked default would be wrong; it should be a *steerable* preference per policy / retrieval profile, recorded here as a seam rather than designed in the abstract.

### Flexible yet robust ✅
- Not a trade-off when **flexibility lives in the combinations and robustness in the pieces** (Lego): each plan choice is a bounded selection over a declared spec (so it always compiles), while flexibility comes from which capabilities you combine, in what order, with which components, depth, inputs and framing — a combinatorially large space of valid options. Free-text annotation adds expressive flex that informs without breaking bounded execution.
- **Honest limit:** you can only compose what's declared; a genuinely novel need falls back to the loosest capability or signals a new one to author. Bounded flexibility now, boundary expanding along the maturation curve.
- **User-added free-form section — the in-bounds escape.** A user can add a free-form output section to a plan; it **compiles to a generic grounded-section component**, so it still grounds via `produce-grounded-block` like any other block. This gives expressive freedom over *output structure* without breaking bounded compile — the free-form section is itself a declared (generic) component, not an unbounded one.

### Two-level planning ✅
- **Orchestration plan (the forecast spine)** — authored by the orchestrator: which capabilities run, in what order, how each output feeds the next (the inter-capability dependency graph), overall steering, *and its best-guess forecast of each capability's likely components and depth* (which drives the preview and time estimate). It's a **living** object — as the run proceeds it tracks forecast-vs-actual as commits land. The **template is a default orchestration plan**; selecting/editing capabilities edits it. *(The time/cost estimate it drives is a deterministic function over the plan selections, recomputed on each edit — ⏸ no estimate model is specified for v3.0.)*
- **Per-capability plan (the commit)** — authored by each **capability agent** just-in-time, once its inputs land: the actual component selection within the declared skeleton. Linked back to the spine via the dependency graph. So forecast and commit live at the two levels — the spine holds the forecast, the per-capability plan holds the commit.
- **Plan is artefact-*like*.** It's a versioned, user-facing, editable, structured object whose edits feed the decision log, so it **reuses the artefact machinery** (blocks, addressable units, versioning, change log) rather than getting its own — but stays a distinct *kind* (an orchestrator-authored, executable instrument, not capability output). Structure is **separate-but-linked** (orchestration spine + per-capability plans), not one monolith: the two hold different things, authored by different agents at different times.
- **System proposes, user disposes:** intent → a recommended capability set (the template), accepted wholesale (fast path) or edited — remove / add / reorder / pick individually (power path). Ordering is bounded by declared dependencies (can't run VfM before the option exists).

### Progressive planning ✅
- Planning is **progressive, not all-at-once**: later capabilities can't be fully planned up front because their inputs don't exist yet (Options' plan depends on what Evidence found; VfM's on which option was selected). Settle the orchestration plan and the first capability's plan now; (re)plan each subsequent capability just-in-time as its inputs land. Between-capability check-ins sit naturally here.
- ✅ **Forecast the whole, commit each just-in-time.** Rather than fixing the whole sequence up front or committing only one capability ahead, the orchestrator **forecasts** the whole sequence up front (so the user approves the shape), and each capability is **committed** just-in-time as its inputs land. Material divergence between a commit and its forecast surfaces at the between-capability check-in (a steer-point), gated by steering mode; substance divergence escalates in every mode.

### Enough context to propose — honesty-calibrated, ask only on shape ✅ *(resolves the former "enough context" open item)*
The orchestrator's planning-role threshold for *when it has enough context to propose the orchestration plan* is resolved as a **principle, not an intake schema** — the elicitation tactics (how it asks, how many turns) stay implementation/eval. It is **not** a bare "can I produce a steerable plan?" test, which is near-vacuous: the orchestrator can almost always propose *something* (worst case the template), and licensing a confident, detailed plan on near-zero understanding is the anchoring hazard policy work is most sensitive to — a misframed plan presented with false confidence is worse than no plan, because the user steers *from* it. Nor is it a fixed intake checklist (friction, and the brittleness §6 warned against for enumerated taxonomies). Instead, two thin principles:
- **The proposal is honestly calibrated to what the orchestrator actually knows.** It may propose on thin context, but it must surface its assumptions and open guesses as first-class, cheaply-correctable plan content — reusing §5's *Assumptions & boundaries* and the *Frame* ("how the agent understood the user's context"), promoted to structure at capture time (§9). A thin-context plan is therefore *visibly* thin, not falsely confident — which is what defuses anchoring, more than withholding the proposal would.
- **The orchestrator asks only when a missing piece would change the plan's *shape*, not its detail.** This applies §6's method/substance line to intake: shape-determining unknowns (which capabilities, their order, the core framing, what is explicitly out of scope) are expensive to unwind, so a single targeted question beats guessing; detail-level unknowns are cheap — the plan edit and the *lighter/deeper* nudge absorb them — so propose-and-refine. Asking is sparing and reserved for the expensive class.

So the threshold lives in the *output's honesty and the plan's shape-stability*, not in a pre-proposal intake gate, and the plan remains the elicitation device. The plan is the **first decision-log entry** (§3.5). *(Rejected: a bare output-defined threshold — near-vacuous; and a declared intake checklist — frictionful and brittle.)*

## 6. Execution, steering & durability

### Steering modes ✅ *(naming clash resolved)*
The per-run dial the user picks for how often to be involved. Three settings (from the wireframe run control):
- **Minimal (auto):** runs start to finish without proactively stopping; the user steers via the co-pilot and reviews at the end. *(Renamed from "Silent" — substance still pauses even here, so "silent" overstated it.)*
- **Moderate (recommended default):** pauses when it judges it has hit an important decision.
- **Frequent:** checks in at every section/block before proceeding. *(Renamed from "Thorough": this dial is a check-in **frequency**, and "thorough" connotes **depth**, which is the separate depth-per-section gradation in §5 — the word was both colliding and mis-fitted. "Thoroughness/depth" is now reserved for that depth axis. UX-facing label: sync with the wireframe run control, which currently reads "Thorough.")

**Mode is mutable mid-run, and check-ins are suppressible — both hard-constrained.** The dial can be changed partway through a run, and a user can author standing "skip this kind of pause" rules as a routing input (a persistent check-in suppression). Both are **hard-constrained so they can never silence a *substance* check-in** — the routing rule's firm principle (below) holds regardless of the current mode or any suppression rule.

### The routing rule ✅ *(classification structural; cell values by construction; Minimal flags batch-active)*
When a run hits something its plan didn't anticipate, the agent routes it to one of — **pause and ask** / **flag and continue** (bounded detour, marked for review) / **silently log and continue** — replacing the prototype's "plough on regardless." The route is chosen by *kind of decision* (method vs substance) × *steering mode*:

| Situation | Minimal | Moderate | Frequent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Method call — within a declared escape hatch | log + continue | flag + continue | pause |
| Method call — outside the hatch envelope (large/uncertain) | flag + continue | pause | pause |
| **Substance** — declared gate *or* residual that shapes the conclusion | **pause** | **pause** | **pause** |

- **Firm principle:** substance escalates to a human in *every* mode (augmentation principle).
- **Classification is resolved structurally, not by a from-scratch runtime judgement** ✅. The method/substance call is squeezed from both ends by what's already declared, leaving only a small residual to judge live:
  - **Substance is pre-declared** as **anticipated mandatory checkpoints** (§4) — a capability's known conclusion-shaping gates (e.g. VfM's discount rate and counterfactual) fire regardless of mode, so they never depend on the agent recognising them in the moment.
  - **Sanctioned method is pre-declared** as the **escape hatches** (§4): inside the declared envelope (evidence thin → extra search) is auto-OK method; outside it is, by construction, not a sanctioned method move.
  - **The residual = agent judgement calls** ✅ *(panel-review resolution: renamed, broadened, instrumented)*. An **agent judgement call** is a runtime judgement not covered by a declared gate or hatch **where the choice could plausibly affect the artefact's conclusion, emphasis, prioritisation, interpretation or downstream use** — deliberately broader than *"would the conclusion change?"*, because in policy work emphasis, ordering, prioritisation and framing can be substance-shaping even where the headline conclusion holds. The agent classifies each call as bounded method or substance-shaping, with **bias-to-escalate** when substance-or-unsure — and, whichever route it takes, **it emits a durable `agent_judgement_routed` governance event** (§9): project / run / capability / component / plan-version IDs, model + prompt version, the judgement being made and known alternatives, the classification, the route taken (continue / flag / pause), a rationale summary, affected artefact/block IDs where known, and timestamp. *"Method, continue"* decisions are therefore no longer invisible: they don't interrupt the user, but they are reviewable, sampleable, and comparable across model versions after the fact. The residual isn't tiny, so this observability does real work.
- ⏸ **Later iteration — now with a measurement surface, not a vibe.** v3.0 does not attempt a first-principles runtime classifier, and deliberately does **not** pre-enumerate an exhaustive judgement taxonomy (brittle, falsely complete, and it would bias the model toward recognising only the listed examples). Instead the `agent_judgement_routed` stream is the evidence base for later work: audit sampling, model-version comparison, capability-specific evals, and conversion of recurring judgement patterns into declared gates or hatches. **Known residual-of-the-residual:** emission itself depends on the agent recognising it is making a judgement call, so *under-emission* is the remaining invisible failure — lower-stakes than under-escalation, and now measurable: periodically sample run traces against emitted events to estimate the under-emission rate.
- **Minimal-mode flags: batch-active, passively-live** ✅. A "flag + continue" deviation in Minimal is **not** pushed as a mid-run interrupt (proactive interruption is what Moderate is *for*, and what a Minimal user opted out of). Instead it lands in the §7/§9 append-only event stream — **passively visible** to anyone watching, no push mechanism — and is **collated into the end-of-run review pass** (the §7 "in review" phase) as the active surface. Keeps Minimal's run-to-completion intent intact and adds no new machinery.
- **Cell values follow by construction** ✅. With the row categories now defined by the declared envelope (in-hatch / out-of-hatch / substance) and the firm principle (substance pauses in every mode), the table values fall out of the mode semantics (Frequent pauses per section; Minimal logs/flags rather than pauses on method; Moderate sits between). Settled at this altitude; fine-grained tuning is eval/UX, not architecture.
- **Checkpoints are steer-points, not approve/reject.** At a check-in the user can inject their own reasoning and reshape the output — including overriding the agent's verdict, since the rejected alternatives and their reasons are visible (see §4) — rather than only accepting or vetoing.
- **Human substance enters the system two ways** ✅ *(panel-review clarification)*: through **steering** the agent incorporates at check-ins, and through **human-authored or human-amended artefact content** (§3.2, §7). Both are represented honestly in the provenance and audit model — human substance is never paraphrase-laundered into agent-attributed text.

### Durability & concurrency ✅ *(serial v3.0; parallelism deferred behind the recorded DAG)*
These pin the execution requirements the §9 substrate is derived *from* — so the headline is what v3.0 does **not** require, which keeps the spike unconstrained.

- **Serial execution in v3.0.** One active branch at a time; a check-in **holds the run** rather than letting independent branches keep producing. The dependency DAG is **persisted** — the orchestration plan (§5) already *is* this inter-capability graph — so **parallel-eligible sets (nodes with no unmet dependency) are identifiable at plan time**; v3.0 simply walks a **topological order serially**, leaving exploitation of those sets (non-blocking parallel branch-progress) as a **later optimisation** (⏸), not a re-architecture. **When that optimisation lands, the evidence-parallel / synthesis-centralised split is preserved** — parallelise evidence-gathering branches (search, screening, extraction), but keep synthesis and policy judgement in one centrally-reviewed place; the SOTA lesson (Anthropic, LangChain) is that coherence and cross-cutting consistency degrade when *final writing* is fanned out, so parallelism is for acquisition, never for the conclusion. Chosen for simplicity — parallelism brings concurrent writes, partial failure, and parked-freshness *across* live branches, all for a latency win on runs that are human-gated and minutes-to-hours anyway — and to keep the §9 spike free: requiring durable fan-out/fan-in now would let a feature pre-pick the substrate.
- **"Serial" means orchestration branches, not within-step fan-out.** A distinct concurrency lives *inside* a single component/tool: the **data-parallel fan-out** over a corpus (appraise/extract N documents at once — v2's mature pattern). This is **retained, not deferred** — it's how the evidence engine works. From the durability engine's view it is **atomic**: the fan-out runs (plain async, or LangGraph `Send`), gathers, and the **block/component boundary is the checkpoint**. It needs no durable cross-branch exactly-once, so it doesn't touch the build-light call (§9). The deferral is *only* orchestration-branch parallelism; within-step fan-out sits underneath that line.
- **Durable, resumable runs** via **block-boundary commits + tool-result caching.** Each produced block is a durable checkpoint (the block is already the unit of storage/versioning), and expensive tool results (retrieval, appraisal) are memoised, so a resume after a pause, crash, or deploy doesn't redo completed work.
- **Parked-branch freshness = resume on current structured state, with staleness flagged.** A resumed branch reads the *current* structured state (not a stale snapshot — the §9 operate-on-structured-state principle), and the §3.6 derivation/staleness graph **flags** any input that changed while it waited rather than silently consuming it (flag-don't-drop). Rare under serial v3.0 (nothing else runs during a pause), but the rule holds for resume-after-crash/deploy and survives the later move to parallelism.
- **Live steering via co-pilot = a signal applied at the next block boundary.** A steer issued mid-run (Minimal especially) is delivered to the running capability sub-agent and picked up at the **next block-boundary checkpoint** — non-disruptive, no mid-block interruption — and recorded in the decision log like any other steer. Reuses the durability checkpoints.
- **Agent concurrency is trivial in v3.0.** With serial execution there's a single active agent writer, so block/event writes serialise onto the §9 event-sourced log with no contention; genuine multi-agent write concurrency arrives only with the deferred parallelism. (Human concurrency was already settled in §7.)

> ⏸ **Deferred:** intra-run parallelism — independent branches producing concurrently, with a check-in blocking only the dependent sub-graph rather than the whole run. Added later on the recorded DAG, and cheap if the §9 spike lands on a durable workflow engine.

## 7. Collaboration: scrutiny & consensus

### Purpose & the differentiator ✅
- In-tool collaboration isn't a replacement for Teams/Word — it's the **tight loop before the wide loop**: close-knit, evidence-anchored scrutiny and consensus inside the tool, then circulate more widely for broad discussion.
- The differentiator over Teams/Word: comments are **anchored to specific claims/sections, sit next to their evidence, and the agent can act on them** (re-ground, check an alleged inaccuracy, refine, correct). A comment here is actionable, not inert.
- This resolves the "decide consciously" tension as a deliberate **middle**: scrutiny/consensus are first-class *within the tight loop* (anchored, agent-actionable, closure-tracked), but the tool is not a full org-wide consensus/clearance engine — Teams/Word remain the wide-circulation venue. Neither "just tagging" nor "Policy OS."

### Raising vs closing ✅
- The team's wireframe thinking covers the **raising** actions: comment on a sentence/section, flag a potential inaccuracy, flag something to make more prominent, raise a concern/question — and the agent can respond with suggestions/refinements/corrections.
- But scrutiny and consensus are about **closing**, not just raising: a challenge that gets *answered and resolved*; a group that *reaches agreement*. In v3.0, **closure = lock-on-advance** (below); the richer consensus-closure is deferred.

### The v3.0 model — simplified ✅ *(richer scrutiny/consensus model deferred; door left open)*
Simplify collaboration for v3.0, keep the door open. The v3.0 model reuses machinery we already have and parks the elaborate parts.
- **One driver, defaulting to the owner.** Only one user issues *stateful* commands to the orchestrator (plan / steer / commit) at a time — which **dissolves** the conflicting-commands problem rather than solving it (no arbitration, no command-locking). Everyone else **watches the live event stream (artefacts forming, check-ins, decisions and governance events as they land), comments on artefacts, and runs private Q&A** — but does *not* see the driver's verbatim orchestrator transcript (*Transcript visibility*, below; §9). The driver is a *mutable field* defaulting to the owner — the cheap seam that lets **transferable driving** (hand off the wheel — e.g. the economist takes VfM) switch on later without re-architecting.
- ✅ **Per-user transcript visibility — the orchestrator chat is private to the driver** *(rolls back the earlier all-users-see-the-thread decision)*. Collaborators do **not** see the orchestrator transcript of any other user, the driver included. Everything load-bearing in that conversation is already promoted into the canonical **decision log** at capture time (§9, *promote load-bearing chat to structure*), and collaborators see that, the artefacts, and the live event stream — so they lose only the conversational *nuance* the log compresses out, not any decision. The trade accepted: the driver/owner gets a private working surface, at the cost of that nuance no longer being a shared fallback. **Recorded consequence:** the structured record is now the *sole shared* account of the orchestrator interaction, so promotion-completeness carries more weight than before — a candidate online-eval target (*is everything that mattered actually in the log?*). **Auditability is unchanged:** the transcript stays a non-canonical companion store within FOI scope; *not shown to collaborators* is a display choice, never a disclosure claim (mirroring the §9 governance-event framing).
- ⚠️ **Tenancy modelling flag (carry into §11).** v2 leans on **Clerk org claims** for tenancy; the in-flight **Clerk→Cognito** migration loses first-class orgs (Cognito has no native org concept). Whatever models projects/owners/collaborators here must not assume an IdP-provided org primitive — tenancy needs an explicit decision rather than inheritance from the auth provider. Net-new; flagged, not yet resolved.
- **Concurrency falls out for free.** The orchestrator thread is append-only and serialised (one driver writing); what collaborators watch live is the **§9 event stream** (lifecycle / decision / governance events) and the artefacts it produces, *not* the driver's chat turns — streaming is just delivery over that shared stream. Private Q&A is per-user and read-only, so it runs in parallel without contention; each user's Q&A is **multi-session and persisted** (start a new chat, browse previous ones — §9), private to them and outside the audit spine. The rule, restated: **write-scope governs what may drive shared state; transcript visibility is per-user regardless.** The driver's orchestrator transcript can drive shared state (and promotes its load-bearing turns to the canonical log), whereas private Q&A is read-only — but *neither verbatim transcript is shown to other users*. The only project-scoped shared record is the canonical event stream and the artefacts.
- **The comment/refine window *is* the between-capability check-in.** No separate review-gate dial: in Moderate/Frequent the check-in before advancing is where collaborators comment and the driver refines; in Minimal it advances straight through. Comments are the v3.0 contribution channel (the tight-loop scrutiny), routed by the per-artefact assignment.
- **Lock-on-advance = the v3.0 closure, and it's a *hard* lock on the version.** Advancing to the next capability freezes the prior artefact's version — that *is* "done" for v3.0 (no explicit sign-off step). Locked versions are immutable; changing the artefact means **minting a new version**, by either path: **supersede-by-rerun** (re-running the capability; the locked version stays as history), or — once the deferred editing UX lands — a **human amendment** represented as a new block/unit version carrying amendment state (§3.2). **Both paths emit the same supersession events**, so downstream pinned dependencies flag stale identically whichever way the head moved (§3.6). Never editing-in-place of a locked version.
- **Re-runs cascade through the dependency graph.** A re-run is just the orchestrator re-planning a capability that already ran (plan-then-run + forecast/commit + versioning handle it). The §3.6 dependency graph identifies the downstream consumers (rerun Baseline → Options and VfM depended on it) and the orchestrator **flags and proposes** re-running them — blast radius surfaced, human-gated, never silent.
- **Door open for free.** Soft-locking — in-place edits, with the graph flagging fine-grained stale *claims* to edit rather than whole capabilities to re-run — is a later **behavioural toggle** on the same dependency-graph + versioning machinery, not a re-architecture.

### Review timing ⏸ *(deferred — v3.0 uses the between-capability check-in window, above)*
A **configurable sync/async review gate** (a separate dial composing with the steering mode, per-artefact) is the richer, deferred version; v3.0 collapses this into the mode-gated check-in.
- **Synchronous** — pause after an artefact for the group to reach closure before the next capability runs.
- **Asynchronous** — proceed; the group reviews later (a later consensus change flags dependents stale via the dependency graph).

### Status & closure 🟡 *(two-level lifecycle in v3.0; sign-off + clearance deferred)*
- **v3.0 closure = lock-on-advance** (above), not an explicit sign-off act — advancing past an artefact is what marks it done.
- **Two-level status vocabulary** drives the badges. Labels are policymaker-leaning and UX-finalisable, but the *states* are fixed:
  - **Artefact:** planned → drafting → in review → **done**. *In review* = produced and open to comment/refinement; *done* = locked-on-advance (the version is frozen; changing it means a re-run → new version — a visible re-run affordance carries that, so the label needn't).
  - **Project:** planning → in progress → **in review** → **completed**. *Planning* = the planning conversation, nothing run yet; *in review* = the dedicated post-run phase where users comment, request reruns, and refine (distinct from the mid-run between-capability check-in windows); *completed* = the analysis is agreed done. No separate "closed."
  - "In review" is shared across both levels (same meaning); the terminal words differ on purpose — a frozen artefact (*done*) isn't the whole analysis being finished (*completed*).
- **Principle: the tool only surfaces status it actually drives.** Operational states (which the tool knows) get badges; states that refer to processes happening *outside* the tool (clearance, formal sign-off) do not — a flag with no behaviour behind it implies functionality that isn't there.
- **Version-pinning stays in v3.0 — independent of clearance.** It's inherent in hard-lock versioning (immutable versions; a re-run makes a new version), and its payoff is export/audit: an exported or relied-on version can be flagged *"changed since"* if a later re-run supersedes it (§3.6 staleness graph; §10).
- ⏸ **Deferred to future:** explicit **sign-off as the tight-loop consensus act** and the closure *mechanism* (single sign-off vs challenges-satisfied vs all-assent, plus who-may-do-what); and the **clearance flag** ("request clearance" / "cleared") — since the tool doesn't run clearance, surfacing it in v3.0 would imply a workflow that isn't there. Both return only with a coherent story for how the tool relates to the external process.

### Concurrency ✅
- **The system is the sole manager of canonical versions and provenance state — not necessarily the sole author of every word** ✅ *(principle reworded; panel review)*. In v3.0 the agent remains the only *prose writer* and humans contribute through comments and steers — so there is still no two-people-editing-the-same-text race, no CRDT/OT/locking for prose, and comments stay additive (multiple people on one sentence = no conflict). But the architecture no longer depends on "agent as sole writer" as a hard invariant: future human-authored or human-amended content is representable as a new block/unit version carrying provenance and amendment state (§3.2), without reworking the foundations. Whether and what editing UX users get — direct editing, amend-before-advance, a correction flow, agent-mediated rewriting, export-to-edit, or a combination — is **deferred to user testing**; what is decided now is that the backend can represent human authorship honestly when it arrives.
- **v3.0 keeps it simple:** with a single driver and lock-on-advance, comments arrive in the mode-gated window and the driver triggers a refine before advancing. The richer **per-block lock-and-batch on regeneration** (closing a block to comments mid-regen and batching pending comments so none is orphaned) is ⏸ **deferred** — it earns its keep once live concurrent regeneration / multiple drivers exist, which the v3.0 single-driver flow doesn't have.
- A substantive regeneration's coherence repercussions are still handled by the coherence pass / cascade (§3.6), routed through check-ins with the blast radius surfaced.
- **Two distinct locks — keep them separate.** The user's **persistent pin** (a deliberate "freeze this block/artefact" that **exempts it from the §3.6 cascade**, so a downstream re-run won't silently rewrite pinned content) is a different mechanism from the agent's **transient regeneration lock** (the in-flight close-to-comments above). The pin is a standing user instruction; the regeneration lock is a momentary execution detail.

### Permissions & ownership ✅
- **Single accountable owner** (the project creator / SRO) — accountability doesn't distribute; for government, one name is on what goes out — plus a **collaborator list** (comment, request edits).
- **Per-artefact assignment** — an optional "lead/responsible" field per capability's artefact, capturing the flatter reality (the economist leads VfM, etc.). This is an **assignment, not a permission**: it *routes* (comments, check-ins, coherence flags and catch-me-up go to the responsible person), sets *defaults* (default reviewer; natural candidate to declare that artefact closed), and feeds the *audit trail* — but it doesn't restrict anyone; all collaborators can still comment / request edits in v3.0.
- **Collaborator metadata — affiliation & role.** Each collaborator carries an **org/affiliation and role**, which drives cross-departmental display (who's from where, in a team spanning departments) and can later feed routing. Metadata, not permission — it informs the UI and audit trail without restricting actions.
- Implementation stays light: one owner field + collaborator list + optional per-artefact assignee — no RBAC. **Gate-able later** (e.g. "only the assignee may declare this artefact closed") by hanging a permission on the existing assignment.
- ⏸ **Artefact-level sharing — deferred.** v3.0 leans on **project-level sharing + per-artefact notify**; artefact-scoped visibility is revisited only if a real need appears.
- ⏸ **Cross-project notification / nudge — deferred** (feature layer). Its kernel is the unified event log the §9 substrate already settles, so it's additive when wanted.

### Catch-me-up 🟡 *(v3.0 simple; richer version open)*
- **v3.0: a record of everything that's changed** since the user last looked — a chronological view over the existing **decision log + change log + version history**, scoped by a per-user *last-viewed* timestamp (the only new bit). Almost no new infrastructure.
- ❓ *Open:* the richer version — a *personalised, relevance-filtered digest* (what needs your attention, your assigned artefacts, unresolved threads you raised) rather than a firehose. A filter/synthesis layer over the same log, added later.

## 8. Observability, evaluation & operations

A government-grade evidence tool is judged not only by output quality but by whether its behaviour is *traceable, measurable, costed, and operable*. The cheap-now/expensive-to-retrofit discipline (Principle 9) governs here too: v3.0 lays the instrumentation **seams** that production operations later hang off, without building the heavy operational machinery yet.

### Two observability planes — keep them distinct ✅
Mirroring the §9 two-log separation, observability splits into two planes that must not be conflated:
- **The canonical audit plane** — the decision log (§9): append-only, audit-grade, FOI-facing, long-retained, *ours*. The source of truth for *what the system decided and why*.
- **The operational telemetry plane** — LLM traces (Langfuse) + application/infra metrics (APM): high-volume, *non-canonical*, sensitivity-bound, shorter-retained. For *how the system behaved* — debugging, performance, cost, drift, eval.

Telemetry is never the source of truth (it can be sampled, masked, or expired without touching the audit spine), and the audit log is never a metrics store. **Eval results bridge the two** — computed *from* telemetry, attached *to* canonical addressable units (below).

### LLM observability — Langfuse as the trace backbone ✅ *(carried forward from v2)*
v2 already runs **Langfuse** as its LLM-observability layer — integrated via LangChain callbacks across the synthesis and extraction graphs, the briefing orchestrator, verification, theming and the search wizard, with traces scoped by **session** (`langfuse_session_id`, per project/run), **user** (`policy_user_id`) and **tags**, and **self-hostable** (`LANGFUSE_HOST`). v3 carries it forward as the trace spine rather than reinventing it.
- **The trace hierarchy *is* the decomposition hierarchy.** Langfuse traces→observations map directly onto `tool → component → capability → artefact → project`: a run is a session, each capability/component/tool call a nested span. The same decomposition that makes the system eval-ready makes it observable — one structure, both jobs. (This subsumes the old "structured logging at the tool core, aggregatable across the hierarchy" point and the user-facing **"show working" trace**, which reads off the session's spans.)
- **Per-call capture:** prompt/response I/O, **model + version**, **token usage and cost**, **latency**, errors, and structured-output payloads — the raw material for the cost, performance, drift and quality SLIs below.
- **One tool, several ops jobs.** Langfuse is not just a trace viewer: its **scores** are the online/offline eval channel (realising "eval results attach to units", below); its **datasets** hold offline replay/benchmark sets; its **prompt management** is the versioned prompt registry (below); its **sessions** group a run's full LLM activity. Consolidating onto it rather than bolting on separate tools is the build-light call.
- **Self-hosting is a §11 requirement, not a convenience.** Traces contain verbatim policy content and acquired evidence, so the telemetry plane inherits the deployment sensitivity posture — a self-hosted Langfuse inside own-AWS keeps that content within the trust boundary.

### Application & worker-tier observability ✅ *(cheap seam; new surface in v3)*
Distinct from the LLM plane: standard **APM + error tracking** (Sentry-class) and **OpenTelemetry** spans for the non-LLM app — API latency, DB, request path. The genuinely *new* v3 surface is the §9 **off-request worker tier and `runs` queue**: depth, claim/lease health, stuck or zombie runs (an expired lease mid-run), checkpoint-write success, and resume outcomes — the operational signals a serial-worker substrate lives or dies on, emitted near-free from the dispatch loop §9 already hand-builds.

### Retrieval observability ✅ *(new with the Tier-0 retrieval contract, §4)*
Retrieval quality is evaluated at the tool boundary — without this capture, a weak artefact cannot be localised among query formulation, candidate generation, fusion, reranking, extraction, synthesis and verification. Captured on the **telemetry plane** (the one canonical exception: the gap-coverage profile's attempted-search provenance, §3.3/§4): query inputs and variants; scope filters; **retrieval target** — source passages vs finding dimension values, because **one logging contract spans `retrieve` and `query-findings`** (so the dimension-promotion-gate evidence accumulates without a second logging system); lexical and dense candidate sets; fusion/reranking profile; selected results and near-misses where available; fallback path; latency/cost; and **downstream usage signals** — cited, used in extraction selection, discarded, supported a gap claim, user challenge or correction, grounding failure. The architecture does not define the retrieval eval suite in v3.0; it ensures the necessary I/O is captured so offline retrieval evals and regression tests can be added later without changing the retrieval contract.

### Eval-readiness — an architectural property, mostly a consequence of decisions already made ✅
*(Goal: keep the architecture open to evals, not design the evals — that's a separate exercise. Langfuse **scores** and **datasets** are the concrete tooling for the online/offline channels below.)*
- **The decomposition hierarchy is the eval surface.** Each unit has declared I/O and is observable, so each is an independent eval point. Granular evals (right tool selected? agent doing what it should?) at the bottom; LLM-judge and rubric evals at the block/artefact level; project-level evals at the top. Eval per unit, not one monolith — the hierarchy hands you the units.
- **The trust machinery is already eval instrumentation.** Grounding coverage, verify pass/fail (hallucination rate), trust-tier distribution, coherence-pass results, evidence-gap counts are metrics the system emits as a *byproduct* of the trust architecture. Much of evaluability is read off structure already built.
- **Four properties to guarantee (cheap now, expensive to retrofit):**
  1. **Observable I/O at every boundary** — capture (input, output) per unit, not just events, so any unit can be replayed and judged in isolation.
  2. **Declared I/O contracts** (already have) — the same instrumentation serves **offline** eval (replay a unit against a fixed eval set / benchmark) and **online** eval (monitor live outputs); judge-derived SLIs are treated as debugging/monitoring signals in v3.0, not authoritative quality measures — whether they ever become so is the eval workstream's call. One instrumentation, two modes.
  3. **Eval results attach to addressable units** — scores/judgements hang off blocks/claims/artefacts as annotations (same addressing seam as comments, provenance, and user feedback signals). Version-bound, like every annotation (§3.2): eval results are point-in-time judgements on a specific unit version, so longitudinal quality tracking aggregates at block/capability grain rather than chasing units across regenerations.
  4. **Capture the human signal** — comments, checkpoint overrides, rejected verdicts, edits, and explicit citation validations/challenges (§3.2) are online feedback data; they inform monitoring and **sampling**, but do not substitute for deliberately annotated eval labels.
- **The grounding judge ships persisted and versioned; calibration is the eval workstream's** ✅ *(rolled back this session from the panel-review "ships measurable, uncalibrated until measured" resolution)*. v3.0 commits only the **persistence seam**: claim–source grounding judgements persisted with full judge I/O and judge prompt/model versioning — the same *observable-I/O-at-every-boundary* instrumentation (Principle 11) that lets any unit be replayed and judged later. v3.0 does **not** stamp a calibration status on judgements, does **not** frame judge-derived SLIs as "uncalibrated until measured," and does **not** specify a calibration approach: the **rubric, stratified gold set** (the hard cases — interpretive overclaims, quote-mining, topically-related-but-non-supporting sources, omitted caveats, multi-source synthesis, genuine unsupported claims, Tier-4 laundering), **confusion matrix, thresholds, readiness process, and whether any judge-derived metric is ever treated as a product-quality measure are owned wholesale by the eval workstream** — the architecture's sole responsibility is that the persisted judge I/O makes that work possible. The **judge/generator model-family-diversity routing default is retained, but as a *robustness* choice** (reducing correlated judge/generator blind spots), independent of calibration: where provider availability permits, verify routes to a different family from the generator, and a temporary same-family fallback is recorded as an exception in telemetry.
- Reproducibility framed as **auditability, not bit-for-bit**; run-to-run **divergence as an evidence-robustness signal**; structured outputs keep cross-run eval tractable.

### LLMOps fundamentals — the v3.0 cheap seams ✅
The instrumentation seams to lay now, because retrofitting them means re-touching every call site:
- **Prompt & model versioning travels with the trace and the decision log.** Every LLM call records its **model id + version** and **prompt version** (Langfuse prompt management); any model/prompt that shaped a persisted artefact is referenced from its decision-log entry — so a result is reproducible-as-auditable, and a model upgrade is a *visible, gated* event, never a silent behaviour change. ✅ *(panel-review extension)*: every LLM-derived **persisted result records its execution profile** — model id/version, prompt version, tool implementation version, parser/schema/rubric version where relevant, and trace metadata. **Upgrades set future execution defaults; they do not retroactively invalidate existing project state, extractions or artefact versions** (§4): projects are point-in-time records, old results are never represented as products of a newer regime, and an explicit rerun adopts the new profile. A mid-run emergency provider fallback (routing layer, above) is recorded in the execution profile and surfaced at the next check-in rather than silently absorbed.
- **All inference through the in-house inference routing layer (§11)** ✅ *(replaces LiteLLM, dropped org-wide on security grounds)*. A thin, owned layer — with a declared contract, not a dumb proxy: model routing over **configured inference routes** (§11); provider fallback, retry/backoff, and rate-limit/quota handling; inference-layer caching; **per-call cost/latency/model metadata emission into the trace** (the single emission point); model + prompt version stamping; **judge/generator family-diversity routing** for verify (above); the safety-check insertion point (below); and recording the active route as canonical run/deployment metadata, with material route changes emitted as governance events (§9). It is also where the §9 Responses-API-statefulness caveat is abstracted or enforced.
- **Cost & token accounting rolls up the hierarchy.** Per-call usage/cost (from Langfuse) aggregates capability → run → project → tenant — for budgets, capacity planning, and the cost-attribution a government customer will ask for. A first-class product concern, instrumented from day one.
- **Quality & safety SLIs are read off the trust machinery** ✅. The §3.3/§4 trust outputs — grounding coverage, **Unsupported / mis-cited rate**, trust-tier distribution, coherence-pass results, evidence-gap counts — surface as live SLIs *as a byproduct* of the architecture. In v3.0 the judge-derived ones are **debugging, regression-detection and directional-monitoring signals only**, not authoritative product-quality measures; whether any of them is ever validated and promoted to a quality measure is the eval workstream's decision (above), not pre-committed here. The complementary **safety** seam: input/output checks for PII and prompt-injection/jailbreak from *untrusted acquired and uploaded documents* (a real attack surface for a tool that ingests external content), wired at the inference routing layer and grounding boundaries; ties to the §11 egress story.

### Run summary — a user-facing projection ✅ *(new, this session; assumption-based v3.0, refine with users)*
A consolidated **per-run summary** surfaced to the user (and to a government customer's governance view), built as a **projection** over the run-record (§9) plus the two observability planes — *nothing new persisted* (Principle 10), the same read-model category as catch-me-up (§7). It pulls the user- and governance-relevant subset across both planes into one surface while preserving the plane discipline: governance-bearing lines are **canonical-backed** (durable, FOI-facing), the rest are telemetry-derived (sampled / expirable). The v3.0 cut is **assumption-based and explicitly provisional** — pinned now so the surface exists, to be refined against real user behaviour (the §1 users: No. 10 / HMT / departmental teams as decision-makers; the Policy team as operators):
- **Cost / duration** — wall-clock time and a rough cost band where the estimate model exists (⏸ §5). *(Telemetry — cost/token roll-up, above.)*
- **What it rests on** — counts of sources searched / screened / cited, the source mix by **appraisal tier** and by **origin** (uploaded vs acquired), and the backends touched. *(Corpus/origin facts canonical, §3.3; counts telemetry-derived.)*
- **What left the system** — the external `search` egress, read from the canonical *"what left the system?"* projection (§9/§11) — the governance line a government customer asks for first. *(Canonical-backed.)*
- **How solid it is** — trust-tier distribution, Unsupported / mis-cited and **weakly-grounded** flag counts (§3.3), **below-policy citation count** (the source-policy flag, §5), and evidence-gap count. *(Read off the trust machinery — canonical annotations, §3.3.)*
- **What needs you** — unresolved comments / challenges, pending check-ins, and the Minimal-mode batched deviations (§6), routed to the responsible person where assigned (§7). *(Canonical.)*

**Assumed headline (to test, not a settled claim):** *how solid it is* + *what needs you* lead; cost and egress sit secondary. The ordering and the cut are the first things to validate with users. The summary is a **single-run** view keyed by the run-record; project-level roll-ups aggregate the same fields over a project's runs. It is a **read surface only** — it drives nothing, surfaces no status the tool doesn't already hold (Principle 10), and never becomes a canonical store.

### Operations — v3.0 commits vs deferred ✅/⏸
v3.0 commits the cheap, foundational pieces and *names* the heavier machinery as deferred-with-seam rather than building it:
- ✅ **v3.0:** health checks; basic alerting on the SLIs above; an **eval-capable CI harness** ✅ *(panel-review resolution; replaces "eval-gated CI")* — CI can run offline eval suites against replayable captured I/O, record scores through the Langfuse eval channel, localise regressions to a tool/component/capability/artefact boundary, and enforce configured thresholds **once a suite has been formally adopted by the eval workstream**; a suite becomes a true merge-blocking gate only when that workstream has approved its dataset, scoring method, threshold, ownership and maintenance (provisional suites may run for visibility before they block, and should not be described as the v3.0 eval gate). Separately, **deterministic engineering tests sit in normal CI from day one** — plan→config compile round-trip property tests (protecting §5's "executed == approved"), quote-presence-check determinism (§4), and the §9 event/content transaction invariant — these protect ✅ architectural claims and need no eval workstream. Plus a **retention + PII-masking policy** on the telemetry plane (traces/transcripts expire on a schedule distinct from the audit log); and **access control on the observability plane itself** (it holds sensitive content).
- ⏸ **Deferred (seam left open):** formal SLO/SLA targets and an on-call/incident-response process; **canary / staged rollout** of prompt and model changes (behind the gateway + feature flags); capability-level feature flags; live **drift monitors** beyond the re-gate-on-upgrade rule; and privacy-respecting **product/usage analytics** (adoption, capability mix, steering-mode distribution). Each hangs off instrumentation v3.0 already lays — the gateway, Langfuse scores, the event log — so it's additive when the product matures, not a re-architecture.

### Feedback loops & eval-driven development ✅
- The payoff of per-level eval is **localisation**: a quality drop points at a specific tool/component/capability, so improvement is targeted (refine that capability's spec or prompts; improve that retrieval tool), not "the output's bad, somewhere."
- The loop: define a quality bar per unit → instrument → eval **offline** (gate changes, like regression tests for AI) and **online** (monitor live + human corrections) → localise the failure → refine → re-gate.
- **Merge-blocking evals are adopted suite-by-suite** ✅ *(panel-review clarification)*: a provisional suite can run in CI for visibility before it blocks; it becomes blocking only once the eval workstream has agreed its dataset, rubric, scoring method, threshold and owner.

### Open — the eval design itself ❓
- Designing the actual evals remains a **separate workstream**: selecting the behaviours to test, benchmark/gold-set construction (including any judge-calibration gold set), rubrics, LLM-judge prompts, judge validation, thresholds, QA process, ownership, and which suites become merge-blocking. **The architecture's responsibility is that those evals can be run, scored, traced, attached to addressable units where useful, and made merge-blocking once adopted — not to specify them.** When undertaken, seed the work with a scan of current agent-eval literature and benchmark suites — so it's current rather than from memory.
- **Operational-policy specifics to settle alongside it:** the telemetry-plane **retention windows** (trace/transcript expiry vs FOI/data-minimisation tension), the **PII-masking and safety/guardrail policy** detail, and the SLO targets — operational policy, not architecture, but they ride on the seams above.

## 9. State & persistence substrate

- **Sequence:** derive the substrate from the execution requirements (including the pause/detour behaviour in §6) — don't let the substrate's primitives dictate the domain model. It's the first *build/infra* decision, downstream of requirements, not the first decision full stop.
- **The heavy engine is a parallelism-era decision, not a v3.0 one.** The original leaning toward a durable workflow engine (Temporal/Restate-class) was justified partly by multi-user + agent concurrency and long-parked runs — but §6 has since made v3.0 **serial / single-writer**, so that motivation no longer bites for v3.0. A heavyweight engine's killer feature is durable *concurrency*, which we deferred; adopting one now would let a deferred feature pre-pick the substrate.

### v3.0 substrate decision ✅ *(build-light, grounded in the actual v2 stack)*
Informed by a review of the deployed v2 backend (FastAPI + Postgres-only via Supabase; **LangGraph already in production** for synthesis and extraction graphs, currently compiled with no checkpointer; execution synchronous **in-request** on stateless ECS Fargate behind a shared ALB with a **60 s idle timeout**; data plane self-hosted on own AWS — Aurora PG + pgvector).

- **The real v3.0 gap is off-request execution, and it's engine-independent.** In-request execution on Fargate + the ALB's 60 s idle timeout means the architecture **cannot hold a HITL pause** today — a long *streaming* run survives by emitting, but the escalate/clarify **park** (idle) gets severed. So an **off-request worker tier** is a prerequisite *every* option pays first. The engine question therefore shrinks to "what manages durable state once compute is off the request path" — the lighter question.
- **Decision: build-light.** Attach **LangGraph's `AsyncPostgresSaver`** on the Aurora we already run as the durable-state layer; stand up a **thin off-request worker tier** (a second Fargate service); the "queue" is a **Postgres `runs` table** (status + pending-signal). LangGraph's `interrupt()` / `Command(resume=…)` maps one-to-one onto the escalate/clarify **park-and-resume**. This satisfies the v3.0 requirements — block-boundary checkpoints, park-and-resume on a human signal, tool-result memoisation, many independent serial runs — using a maintained durable-state library **already operated in production**.
- **Why light wins here specifically:** both heavy-engine pillars are absent for v3.0 — durable concurrency is deferred (§6 serial), and bulletproof crash/deploy recovery is **de-prioritised** (low usage; no realistic active runs at antisocial hours; deploys can be timed for quiet windows). LangGraph checkpoints still give *best-effort* resume-from-last-block-boundary for free; we simply don't engineer it to be bulletproof. The "worse half-Temporal" trap doesn't bite because we're not hand-rolling durable execution on raw Postgres — the checkpointer is maintained, and serial-v3.0 never touches the hard primitives (timers, retries, cross-branch exactly-once).

**Known limits + mitigations (recorded):**
- **No durable timers / scheduled wake-ups** — LangGraph waits for an external resume, not "sleep N days." A non-issue for HITL (the human's reply *is* the wake-up); if a later capability needs auto-continue-after-N-hours, add an EventBridge/cron poll over the `runs` table (additive).
- **Exactly-once side effects are the caller's responsibility** — a resume replays from the last checkpoint, so a non-idempotent side effect *before* a checkpoint can re-fire. Mitigated by §6 (checkpoint at block boundaries + memoise expensive tool results); serial-v3.0 keeps this surface small.
- **Dispatch tier — committed pattern.** The one genuinely hand-built piece. The `runs` table is claimed with **atomic lease semantics** (a worker leases a run; a crashed worker's lease expires and the run becomes reclaimable) plus a **polling fallback** alongside `LISTEN/NOTIFY` (which is *not* durable — a notify with no live listener is lost). That is the whole of it: the correctly-scoped residue of the half-Temporal warning — the only place even serial-v3.0 hand-builds orchestration, and a small, well-understood pattern rather than an open risk.
- **No turnkey run-inspection console** (no Temporal-Web equivalent) — but §8 observability + the Project event log hold the data; a thin admin view over `runs`/checkpoints is modest DIY.

**Reversibility seam ✅ — what keeps "buy light later" a clean swap, not a one-way door:**
- **Durable steps + signals behind an interface** (LangGraph nodes + interrupts), mirroring the §11 swappable-inference seam — so swapping the durability backend later (to a Restate-class single binary, when parallelism lands) is a backend swap over the same step functions, not a rewrite.
- **Two logs; the canonical one is ours, not the engine's.** The **Project event log** (§ below — the spine behind the decision log, change log, version history and catch-me-up projections; transcripts as companion stores) lives in **our Postgres tables** as the source of truth, *separate* from LangGraph's execution checkpoint, which is only the resume mechanism. So if LangGraph-native proves insufficient we replace the execution layer **without touching the source of truth** — we're not betting the domain model on the engine. *(v2 already has the precedent: a separate `synthesis_citations` table, the working ancestor of §3.2's `(block, unit, type)` layer; and v2's evidence-coverage computation is a live instance of §3.3's metadata-grounded pattern.)*
- **LangGraph serves both layers, by design.** For v3.0, LangGraph is *both* the reasoning layer **and** (via its checkpointer) the durability layer. Concretely (per §4's capability-realisation): a **fixed harness** interprets the plan-as-data — the graph is never rebuilt per run; deterministic components **fan out within a step** (`Send` / async); agentic components are agent-loop nodes; `interrupt()` sits at the clarify/check-in points. The **two-log separation** is what makes the dual role safe — the engine never owns canonical state — so it's a convenience we can withdraw (swap the execution layer) without touching the source of truth.
- **Nested interrupts — supported pattern, relied on by design.** A sub-agent `interrupt()` inside a subgraph propagates to and resumes through the parent orchestrator graph via the shared checkpointer — a documented LangGraph capability, so we rely on it by design rather than holding it open. The spike **smoke-tests our specific nesting** (orchestrator → capability subgraph → component), not the primitive itself.
- **Inference-seam caveat (carry into §11).** The v2 chatbot leans on **Responses-API statefulness** (`previous_response_id`) and structured outputs. With LiteLLM dropped, the **in-house inference routing layer** (§8, §11) abstracts or enforces this. Bedrock now offers Responses-API-*compatible* inference for supported OpenAI models, but only a subset of capabilities and only in initial (US) regions — so the rule holds regardless of route: our agent-context model prefers structured-state-passing over provider-side conversation state (§9 chat persistence), and **the orchestrator's own loop must not silently depend on `previous_response_id`**.

**A run is one capability execution ✅** *(grain settled this session)*. The unit of off-request work in the `runs` table is a single capability execution; blocks and components are checkpoints *within* a run (the resume points, §6), and the orchestration plan (§5) is the DAG *over* runs. This is the grain the runs table, the run-record and the run summary (§8) all key to — "many independent serial runs" means many capability executions walked in topological order.

**The run-record — the execution correlation spine ✅** *(this session; a consolidation, not a new entity — Principle 10).* A single capability run is already represented across several tables; the run-record names the **correlation**, not a new store. Each `runs` row carries (or links) the foreign keys that tie one capability run together: the **committed per-capability plan version** (§5), the **evidence dataset snapshot(s)** it consumed (§4), the **execution profile** — model/prompt/tool/parser/schema versions (§8) — the **produced artefact/block version(s)** (§3.5), and the **trace/session id** (Langfuse, §8). This makes *"everything about this run"* a single keyed query: it is the spine the §8 cost/quality roll-ups aggregate over, the §3.6 dependency-impact / blast-radius view reads, point-in-time replay rebuilds from, and a future `/runs/{id}` read-API exposes. It **behaves** (replay, audit, roll-up, API), so it clears Principle 10 — but it is a join made addressable, not a new layer. This is the defensible core of the SOTA "work-package" idea minus the deferred-parallelism baggage: **no `branch_run` entity**, because v3.0 is serial (§6); the report's `work_package` / `finding_bundle` / `verification_batch` are otherwise the per-capability plan, the evidence dataset snapshot, and the persisted grounding judgements already modelled.

> ⏸/❓ **Deferred / open:** a dedicated workflow engine, durable timers, and **self-host operability** (per §11, *not* weighted in the v3.0 decision — and the v3.0 build-light choice, "just Postgres + our app," is anyway the *most* self-hostable, so deferring it didn't change the v3.0 answer) — all revisited together when intra-run parallelism lands (§6).

### Canonical log vs transcript stores ✅ *(the "one log" question, resolved)*
The four candidate streams are not peers, so "one log for all four" was the wrong frame:
- **The Project event log is the single canonical, append-only, event-sourced spine** ✅ *(terminology unified; panel review)* — audit-grade, long-retained, *ours*. Lifecycle events (regenerate / lock-on-advance / supersede, §3.5), decision events, and **governance events** (below) are emitted into it; **versioned content is materialised in its own tables** (read version N directly — don't event-source prose) and referenced by those events. Everything user-facing is a **projection** over the log and the materialised content/version tables — there is no second canonical store:
  - **Decision log** — the audit-facing projection of decision-relevant events (the FOI-facing view).
  - **Change log** — the per-artefact projection of edits, refinement requests, locks, reruns and supersessions (§3.5).
  - **Version history** — materialised content versions plus the events that created, locked or superseded them.
  - **Catch-me-up** — a per-user projection of relevant events since a last-viewed watermark (a read model, not a store).
- **Governance events live *in* the Project event log** ✅ *(panel-review resolution — one definition, not three)*: typed events for audit-significant system actions — external egress per `search` call (§4), `agent_judgement_routed` (§6), material inference-route or security-posture changes (§11). They are canonical and long-retained, but **excluded from the decision-log projection by default**: "promotion" into the human-facing project history is a projection rule or event flag (applied when an event is reviewed, challenged, overridden, escalated or otherwise material) — never a data move between stores. The UI can project them into a *"what left the system?"* view on demand. Canonical and long-retained also means **within FOI scope**: "not shown in the project history by default" is a display choice, never a disclosure claim.
- **Transaction invariant** ✅ *(panel-review resolution)*: in v3.0, a write that materialises or updates versioned content and its corresponding Project event log entry commit **in the same Postgres transaction**; if later split across services, a transactional outbox preserves the same invariant. The invariant: **no persisted content change exists without its canonical event, and no content-change event points at a missing materialised version** — the FOI-facing record and the content it references cannot diverge.
- **Transcripts are separate, non-canonical stores — *not* in the event log.** The load-bearing moments are already promoted into the decision log (which back-references the turns); the transcript is *context around* those decisions. Keeping it out of the canonical log keeps the audit spine clean — an FOI extract reads the decision log directly, not a sea of chat.
- **Both transcript scopes are per-user companion stores; they differ only by write-scope, not by visibility** ✅ *(updated — the orchestrator transcript is no longer project-shared; see §7, Per-user transcript visibility)*:
  - **Orchestrator transcript (the driver's)** — keyed to user + project, append-only, **private to its author**. It can *drive shared state*, and its load-bearing turns are promoted to the decision log at capture time — but the verbatim turns are not shown to other collaborators. With transferable driving, each driver sees only their own turns; the *shared* continuity across a handoff is the structured state (plan / decision log / artefacts), which the orchestrator agent operates on regardless (*Chat persistence & agent context*, below), so the handoff is clean for the agent even though no user sees another's chat.
  - **Private per-user Q&A** — per-user, **multiple persisted sessions** (start a new chat; browse previous ones), read-only with respect to shared state. Never part of the audit spine, and **not read by catch-me-up** (which reports shared-state change, not other users' private questions). Pure private continuity.

To be explicit: a transcript is a *separate* companion store, keyed to **user + project** for both scopes, never another stream in the canonical event log; the only shared, project-scoped record is the canonical event log and its projections. The remaining distinction between the two scopes is purely **write-scope** — the orchestrator transcript can promote turns into shared state, Q&A cannot.

### Chat persistence & agent context ✅
The chat is now the orchestrator's surface (§4), so its persistence matters — and it resolves into a **two-layer** model rather than "persist everything" vs "persist nothing":
- **Structured layer = the source of truth.** Decision log + plan + change log + artefacts are canonical, operational, and audit-grade — what the system runs on and what FOI rests on.
- **Transcript = persisted, but a *companion* record.** Persisted for session continuity (a disappearing chat reads as a broken tool) and for the conversational nuance — the "why" behind a decision — that the structured log compresses out. It is **explicitly non-canonical**: it never overrides the structured layer, and the agent operates on the *structured state*, not on stale chat (avoids two-sources-of-truth drift).
- **Linkage carries the "why."** Each decision-log / plan entry back-references the chat turn(s) that produced it (the §5 plan-field ↔ chat-turn provenance), so the deliberation is addressable from the canonical record rather than lost in the transcript. The *load-bearing* why (a verdict's rejected alternatives + reasons) is already structured as artefact content (§4); the transcript adds the softer texture.
- **Agent context ≠ persisted transcript.** Three mechanisms, each to its strength: the **structured state** carries the load-bearing context (a lossless-for-decisions compression — read authoritatively); a **recency window** carries the immediate back-and-forth; **recall-on-demand** pulls older turns from the persisted verbatim when needed (windowing means "not hot," not "gone") — **harness-level context assembly, not a registry tool** (§4): transcripts are not a `lookup` target, and no capability sub-agent reads raw chat through any tool. Prefer window-plus-recall over lossy **summarisation** (a confident-but-wrong summary actively misleads); any rolling summary is a secondary aid with the verbatim retained behind it.
- **The orienting context — harness-assembled, present from turn one** ✅. A surface (orchestrator or Q&A) does **not** start blind and reach for `lookup` to discover the project: the harness composes a compact **orienting context** into every turn, with the read tools (`lookup` / `retrieve` / `query-findings`) reserved for *targeted depth on demand* (the exact supporting quote, a full finding set, a block's provenance) — not for basic orientation. The orienting context is a **read model assembled from current canonical state each turn** (the same category as catch-me-up — a projection, not a stored object; nothing new persisted, Principle 10), comprising:
  - **Project frame** — the identity-and-intent header: project title and top-level policy intent (§1), owner/SRO, project status and collaborators with affiliation/role (§7); plus **framing**, which is the plan's living **Frame** narrative (§5) over the structural intent (current capability set, dependencies, assumptions/boundaries). Per the operate-on-current-state rule, the frame is a **projection over the *current* plan and decision log — never a read of the initial Frame text** (which would be the stale-snapshot trap for incremental users); the accreted intent is already represented structurally because new capabilities/instances enter only via human-gated plan edits (§3.5, §5).
  - **Plan shape** — the orchestration spine and forecast-vs-commit progress (§5).
  - **Artefact inventory** — the plan's instance set and each artefact's status/head version (§3.5).
  - **Recent decisions** — a window over the decision-log projection (below).
  - **Current view** — the artefact/block the user is looking at, supplied by the client (what makes "where does the £0.32 come from?" answerable before any fetch, §4).
  The orchestrator additionally pre-loads the live run/steer state it needs to act; Q&A stays lean for latency (§4).
- **Context-assembly scope follows transcript-visibility scope** ✅ *(closes the §7 per-user-transcript loop)*. A surface's harness may assemble only the **acting user's own transcripts plus shared structured state** — **never another user's transcript**. Otherwise the §7 per-user transcript privacy would be cosmetic (a collaborator could simply ask their Q&A "what did the driver discuss?"). The shared structured state (frame, plan, decision log, artefacts, annotations, corpus, findings) is fully available to every surface; only the verbatim *chat* is per-user. Consequence: the **driver's own** Q&A is the only surface that can still draw on the orchestrator transcript (same author, no leak); a collaborator's Q&A is grounded in shared structured state plus its own session history — and loses no *decision*, only the conversational nuance already compressed out by promotion-to-structure.
- **Promote load-bearing chat to structure at capture time.** Anything that matters — "skip France" → "3 comparators"; stated preferences → the plan's assumptions/framing — is interpreted into structured state when said, so it never depends on recovering it from a window later.
- **Transcripts are separate companion stores** (see *Canonical log vs transcript stores* above), *not* entries in the canonical event log; the context strategy itself is eval-tunable (§8: replay full-vs-windowed context, compare).
- **Rehydration adds no new context loss** ✅. Because the LLM is stateless between calls, an agent's working context is *reconstructed every turn* from the materials above — so resuming a parked/crashed/redeployed run is not special amnesia: it rebuilds context exactly as an ordinary mid-run turn does (structured state + recency window + recall-on-demand). Durable execution persists the *materials*, not a live context blob. The only nuance loss is the accepted windowing trade (soft texture not in the window must be retrieved, and the agent must know to fetch it) — identical whether or not a durable engine sits underneath, and bounded because load-bearing "why" is promoted to structure (and the heaviest "why," a verdict's rejected alternatives, is already structured artefact content, §4).

## 10. Export & cross-boundary traceability ⏸/🟡 *(v3.0 seam — down-weighted; the primary surface is the tool itself)*

- **The primary surface is the tool, not an exported object.** v3.0 does **not** invest in export machinery. Interaction — reading, drilling into provenance, comparing versions — happens in the tool; if a user wants to share an artefact, the eventual path is a **read-only in-tool view** (viewable without logging in), not a separate exported artefact. So export is held as a **seam**, not a v3.0 build.
- **Don't over-engineer the boundary** *(unchanged principle)*. The tool's job ends at a well-cited corpus, not at policing the human-authored white paper (consistent with §1); uplifting content into a white paper is the human's responsibility. If a heavyweight artefact like a PDF is ever produced, it carries the **full text** with citations and a references section — **not** the navigation summaries (§4, *Progressive disclosure*), which are an in-tool device.
- ⏸ **Deferred seam — version-pinned deep-linking & the superseded banner.** Because claim-spans are addressable and **version-pinned** (§3.2), a shared/exported reference *can* resolve back into the artefact at the exact `(block, version, unit)`, and a resolved view of a superseded version *can* carry a visible **"superseded — the current head differs" banner** (§3.6 drift detection) so a stale link never silently lends current authority to old analysis. This was previously scoped as a v3.0 "cheap win"; it is **down-weighted to a seam** — the machinery is cheap to add *when sharing lands* and the rationale is recorded so it survives, but it is not built in v3.0. **Note the banner is *more* load-bearing for a public read-only share view than for a PDF** — a stale shared link reaching a wider audience is the sharper version of the hazard — so version-pinned resolution + the superseded banner become **requirements of the share-view seam**, not optional polish, if and when it is built.
- ❓ *Open — mostly a governance question, not a technical one:* whether a *government* white paper's claims need to stay traceable past the tool (FOI / scrutiny land exactly at that boundary). A policy-process decision about how AI-assisted outputs are treated; ties to §11. *(The unauthenticated reader of a shared view is the same boundary as the minister reading an exported summary — which is why the §4 display invariant and conclusion-fidelity rules matter most on any detached surface.)*

## 11. Governance, security & deployment 🟡 *(kept light by design)*


- **Deployment posture:** hosted on own AWS; database in the same AWS environment. Built **open-source**, so departments needing to handle very sensitive data can eventually run their **own private deployment**. Users decide what's appropriate to upload.
- **Configured inference routes — not a single hard-coded provider** ✅ *(panel-review resolution; replaces "enterprise-tier LLM providers"; LiteLLM dropped org-wide on security grounds, replaced by the in-house routing layer, §8)*. An **inference route** records: route ID; provider platform (OpenAI API, Amazon Bedrock, Azure, …); model family/ID; **data-control posture** (no-training, retention, logging, provider access); **network posture** (public API, private endpoint / PrivateLink-class); **region/jurisdiction**; approved use scope; effective date; and an eval/assurance profile. v3.0 routing policy stays deliberately simple — **one approved route per deployment/environment**, recorded as active inference-posture metadata; **material route changes are governance events** (§9); individual inference calls remain operational telemetry (§8), not governance events. **First-pass route: the OpenAI API** under the organisation's approved business/API data controls. **Target route: Amazon Bedrock** — proprietary OpenAI models entered limited preview on Bedrock in late April 2026 (initially US regions only), so track availability rather than assume it. The **region field is load-bearing**: a model family available on Bedrock only in US regions is not a UK-residency win, while families servable from eu-west-2 (London) are — residency may legitimately drive the §8 generator/judge family split, not just the diversity default.
- **Seam to protect this — the routing layer over configured routes.** The self-host-for-sensitive-data story only holds if a private deployment can replace a configured commercial route with a self-hosted/on-prem route without re-architecting. The in-house routing layer (§8) *is* that abstraction; cheap as a seam, expensive to retrofit.
- **Relation to §9 — deferred for v3.0, returns for the parallelism-era engine.** Self-hosting argues for **self-hostable substrate components** (durable-execution engine, DB, vector store runnable in a department's own environment, not cloud-only managed services). But per the v3.0 steer this is **not weighted in the v3.0 substrate decision** — v3.0 is reasoned AWS-now, with self-host operability recorded as a future selection criterion (§9 deferred/open). The reconciling point: the v3.0 build-light choice ("just Postgres + our app") happens to be the *most* self-hostable option anyway, so deferring the constraint didn't change the v3.0 answer — it removed a consideration that pointed the same way. The constraint returns as a real input when the dedicated workflow engine is chosen (alongside intra-run parallelism, §6).
- **Light notes:** the **decision log** (a projection of the Project event log, §9) already covers the accountability/audit-trail need (FOI-type scrutiny). **Sensitivity is handled at deployment / inference-route / tool-governance tier, not per item, in v3.0 — rationale corrected** *(panel review; §3.3)*: v3.0 **has** live egress (inference calls; search queries), so the deferral of a per-document classification column rests on Principle 10 — no v3.0 behaviour reads the label — not on an absence of egress. Search-query egress is an accepted, documented v3.0 risk made visible through governance events (§9); a per-item sensitivity gate returns when labels drive concrete behaviour (blocking, approval, query generalisation, route selection, private deployment).

**v3.0 egress profiles** ✅ *(panel-review addition)*:
