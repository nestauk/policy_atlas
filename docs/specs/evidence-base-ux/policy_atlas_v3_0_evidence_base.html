04

Build — sections stream, with a check-in

The evidence table lands first, then summary sections stream in block-by-block — content readable as it arrives, and the decision log evolving alongside. Status is carried by the coloured edge (green = done, amber = paused, plain = queued) rather than a tag on every block. When evidence is thin the build pauses: the block flags amber and the question waits in the copilot — answering (or skipping) resumes.

Synthesis

Tax-credit schemes raise business R&D, but additionality varies sharply by firm size. The effect is consistently positive for SMEs across five randomised studies [1,2], and weaker among large incumbents where deadweight loss is higher [4]. The most credible UK estimate puts additionality at roughly £0.32 of additional R&D per £1 of forgone revenue [3].

Evidence on the long-run growth effect is contested: IFS 2024 estimates a +0.3% GDP uplift [3], while OBR 2026 judges the productivity channel uncertain in the medium term [5]. The synthesis presents both as a range.

Key findings

Strong5 RCTs, consistentSME additionality is consistently positive across 5 RCTs [1,2].
Moderatemixed resultsLarge-firm effects are mixed; deadweight is the key risk [4].
Contestedsources disagreeLong-run GDP effect is contested between IFS and OBR [3,5].

Gaps & conflicts

Little UK evidence on effects beyond five years. The IFS / OBR disagreement on the medium-term productivity channel is the main unresolved conflict and is flagged for the analyst to weigh.

05b

Summary — progressive detail

One mechanic, repeated: every section ends with the same bare ▾ Detail row (no prose descriptors, no counts). Collapsed, the page is the claim — that's the director's read. Expanded, it's the workings, never more claims: Synthesis → how it was weighted, what was set aside, why; a finding → its contributing studies, counter-evidence, the grade rationale. Aggregate rating badges are deferred, so expanded detail explains what is stronger, thinner, contested, or set aside without presenting an artefact-level rating. Collapsed = the answer, expanded = the audit trail. Expand all is the whole "analyst mode"; one level down only, deeper hands off to the Evidence table or Library.

Synthesis

Tax-credit schemes raise business R&D, but additionality varies sharply by firm size. The effect is consistently positive for SMEs across five randomised studies [1,2], and weaker among large incumbents where deadweight loss is higher [4]. The most credible UK estimate puts additionality at roughly £0.32 of additional R&D per £1 of forgone revenue [3].

How this synthesis was built
Weighting — randomised studies were weighted above observational ones; the £0.32 figure is IFS's central estimate, chosen over OECD's wider range [4] because it is UK-specific and post-2015.
Where studies disagreed — three papers found null large-firm effects; these are reflected in "weaker", not dropped.
Set aside — 12 pre-2013 studies excluded as pre-dating the current scheme design; one outlier (+£0.90) excluded as an industry-funded estimate.
What is stronger or thinner: stronger on SMEs; thinner on long-run and economy-wide effects.

Key findings

Strong5 RCTs, consistentSME additionality is consistently positive across 5 RCTs [1,2].
How this was weighed
Becker & Hall (2022) — +18% R&D spend among treated SMEs · RCT, n=2,400 [1]
Dechezleprêtre et al. (2023) — elasticity ≈2.6 at the size threshold · RCT [2]
+ 3 further RCTs, consistent direction — see all 5 in the evidence table →
Set against: the BEIS pilot (2025) suggests weaker additionality post-2022 — not yet peer-reviewed, so noted rather than weighed.
Why "Strong": five randomised studies, consistent direction, comparable effect sizes.
Moderatemixed resultsLarge-firm effects are mixed; deadweight is the key risk [4].
Contestedsources disagreeLong-run GDP effect is contested between IFS and OBR [3,5].

Gaps & conflicts

Little UK evidence on effects beyond five years. The IFS / OBR disagreement on the medium-term productivity channel is the main unresolved conflict and is flagged for the analyst to weigh.

09

Re-entering the project — Library opens

The next morning. The user returns to their per-user project copilot thread; their own prior exchange is dimmed under a date divider. The catch-me-up is generated from shared project event history — uploads, comments, version changes and decision-log events — not by exposing collaborators' private transcripts. A suggested next step can start an Options Assessment planning conversation, but it is not a phantom draft. The Library panel opens automatically — sources up top, artefacts below. (On a new project it starts collapsed — frame 01.)
